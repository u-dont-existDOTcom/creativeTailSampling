## Surviving cross-domain connections

### C001 — Active normative edge-case search

**Status:** SURVIVING CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION; not claimed as academically unprecedented.

**Target:** intentional-community values, membership fit, governance calibration, cultural replication.

**Source mechanisms:** active learning / preference elicitation + software-style regression testing.

Do not optimize the values process for maximum agreement on abstract principles. Adaptively select concrete cases where the currently plausible interpretations of members' values are most likely to diverge.

Architecture:

1. collect independent judgments on concrete cases before discussion;
2. infer where members' latent interpretations differ;
3. choose the next case expected to distinguish those interpretations most strongly;
4. separate initial measurement from later deliberation/socialization because elicitation can itself change preferences;
5. resolve only disagreements that materially affect community design;
6. preserve the highest-information resolved cases as a versioned **constitutional regression suite**;
7. rerun those cases when membership, rules, or governance practices change to reveal semantic drift;
8. use minimal discriminating commitments/cases in onboarding rather than inspirational values language alone.

Demote C001 if a target-domain precedent is found that already uses substantially the same adaptive procedure, or if ordinary non-adaptive case discussion performs equally well at revealing consequential hidden disagreements.

### C002 — Flagship founder trap / founder-export externality

**Status:** SURVIVING CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION; provisional, not claimed as academically unprecedented.

**Target:** movements of communities whose success criterion includes founding daughter communities / spreading a way of life.

A highly successful flagship may absorb and retain exactly the experienced, high-agency members the wider movement most needs as founders.

The core mechanism is a multilevel incentive conflict:

1. proven members are disproportionately valuable to the local community;
2. the same members may be disproportionately likely to found viable daughters;
3. exporting them imposes an immediate concentrated cost on the parent;
4. much of the benefit of a successful daughter accrues to the wider movement rather than the parent;
5. therefore locally rational communities can collectively underproduce founders even when all endorse movement growth.

This is stronger than "success makes people comfortable." It predicts a **founder-export externality**.

Distinctive predictions:

- among movements with similar inflow of capable newcomers, unusually high retention of the most experienced/high-agency members by flagship communities may correlate with lower daughter-community formation unless founder export is explicitly organized;
- improving flagship attractiveness could eventually reduce daughter formation by making exit into founding less attractive, producing a non-monotonic relation between flagship quality and movement reproduction;
- federation-level rewards, replacement pipelines, fellowships, or founder-export subsidies should increase daughter formation more than generic recruitment alone if the bottleneck is experienced founders.

Demote C002 if intentional-community or adjacent movement literature already establishes substantially this same local-retention versus founder-export mechanism, or if founder experience/high-agency does not materially predict daughter viability.

## Promising but unpromoted

### P001 — Multi-parent daughter communities as cultural recombination

Seed new daughter communities with experienced members from multiple successful parent communities rather than a single parent clone. Online-community genealogy research supports the broader pattern that diverse prior-community experience predicts early participation in new communities, but the proposed land-based intentional-community transfer may still collapse to ordinary cross-pollination.

Needs a stronger consequence before promotion.

### P002 — Turnkey replication can delete the developmental process

Copying mature community practices may omit the joint problem-solving and adaptation through which the parent acquired governance competence. This remains too close to tacit knowledge/path dependence/local-adaptation theory for promotion.

## Protocol lessons

### M001 — Formalization is not novelty

A familiar root idea does not become a tail discovery because downstream mechanisms are given technical names.

### M002 — User familiarity veto dominates model confidence

If the intended expert user immediately compresses the candidate to common sense or a known theory, record the collision and move outward rather than defending the wording.

### M003 — Nearest-neighbor literature attack before promotion

Candidate survivors require an explicit search for conceptual ancestors before promotion. This prevents obvious retrieval failures but does not prove originality.

### M004 — Cross-domain transfer can count, same-domain rediscovery cannot

A known source-domain mechanism can survive if its transfer to the target problem is nonstandard and produces distinctive predictions or design rules. Renaming an existing target-domain theory does not survive.

### M005 — Empty batches are successful

The protocol should prefer "nothing survived" over promoting a weak result. A high rejection rate is evidence the novelty gate is functioning.
