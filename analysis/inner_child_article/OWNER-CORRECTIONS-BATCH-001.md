# Inner-child article — owner corrections after Tail Batch 001

Date: 2026-08-16

These corrections supersede conflicting interpretations in the preliminary Batch 001 assistant synthesis. They must be preserved in future tail-sampling and any later editing.

## 1. Outcome measurement does not imply premature rejection of reparenting

Prospective outcomes should be measured and the method/protocol can be wrong in some respect. However, lack of results should not be treated as strong evidence that reparenting itself is simply `wrong for the person`.

The more likely differential includes:

- misunderstanding or incorrect use;
- insufficient repetition/dose/duration;
- wrong sequencing or missing prerequisite support;
- an implementation needing more nuance;
- a protocol that should be updated;
- an adjunct or other therapy needed for a current obstacle.

The owner's substantive thesis is that when the inner-child / inner-adult relationship is dysfunctional, some form of reparenting is ultimately necessary. Other therapies can be useful, but they do not eliminate that target.

Future research may test or challenge this thesis explicitly, but must not silently replace it with a generic `switch methods if results are absent` conclusion.

## 2. Depth of experience is not integration

The prior assistant analysis misplaced the categories by redefining therapeutic `depth` as integration/behavioral change.

For this article:

- **depth of experience** concerns the richness / degree of contact with the material and may include access and/or intensity, usually both;
- **integration** is a separate dimension concerning what is incorporated afterward into ordinary functioning, understanding, behavior, or choice.

A deep experience can be poorly integrated. A less intense experience can still integrate well. The article's night/dream passage is making an experiential-depth claim, not claiming that intensity itself proves superior integration or clinical efficacy.

Any future critique must preserve that distinction.

## 3. Internalization remains required within a reparenting sequence

The developmental sequence `receive care → observe care → participate → initiate → internalize` is not rejected.

The prior assistant analysis conflated two different questions:

1. whether the reparenting function becomes internalized; and
2. whether a healthy adult continues to receive external care/support.

For a reparenting article, internalization of the adult caregiving function remains the point of the sequence. Chosen interdependence can coexist with that internalization. Authority dependency is a separate issue.

Any useful addition about interdependence must therefore be framed as an adjacent distinction, not as evidence that the internalization step is unnecessary or that the sequence is wrong.

## 4. Preserve the felt-sense `may` claim

The sentence that the felt sense `may know what the conditioned self was trained to forget` is intentionally modal.

Do not silently weaken it into `felt sense only reports present experience`. The owner holds that felt sense may sometimes access information/preferences/knowing obscured by conditioning.

The existing article already separates emotional truth from historical fact and contains strong source/provenance safeguards around photographs, dreams, hypnosis, and altered-state material. Additional safeguards may be considered if genuinely additive, but they must not erase the modal claim.
