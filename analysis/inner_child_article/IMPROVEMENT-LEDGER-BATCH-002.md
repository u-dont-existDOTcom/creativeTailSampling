## Priority 1 — Parentified pseudo-adult as a function-specific presentation

### Article already owns

- borrowed adulthood;
- Nurturer / Protector / Guide;
- adult apprenticeship and internalization;
- protective parts;
- identity formation;
- the claim that many people remain identified with child-states.

### External evidence / collision

West & Keller (1991), PMID 1951790, explicitly describes Bowlby's compulsive caregiving pattern: adult-looking caregiving can originate in childhood role reversal and coexist with suppression of care-seeking and unmet need. Therefore the broad proposition is established prior art.

Yakın & Arntz (2023), PMID 37941965, analyzes Healthy Adult functioning in schema therapy and identifies distinct processes involving bonding with child modes, balancing adult/child emotion, and opposing critical/demanding voices.

### Additive article residual

Do not assess `adult capacity` as one variable.

A parentified / hyper-responsible person may be highly competent at responsibility, protection, direction, and caring for others while having weak:

- care-receiving;
- self-nurturance;
- play/dependence;
- expression of need;
- capacity to let another person help without taking over;
- permission for the child to be developmentally childlike.

Within the article's model, this can be framed as an **imbalanced adult profile** rather than simple absence of adulthood.

### Why this changes practice

The prescription for a helpless child-identified reader and a parentified pseudo-adult should differ. Telling the latter to become more responsible may reinforce the survival strategy that already dominates.

### Suggested placement architecture

Best location: in `Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult`, immediately before or after `Become the Adult Apprentice`, with a short bridge back to `The Three Adult Functions`.

Do not edit yet.

### Disposition

**HIGH-VALUE PRACTICAL IMPROVEMENT; broad originality rejected.**

## Priority 2 — Repair after a broken inner-adult promise

### Article already owns

- child's negative track record;
- visible action;
- small vows;
- `watch what I do`;
- consistency as trust-building.

### External evidence / collision

Alliance rupture-repair is well established in psychotherapy; see PMID 30335462 and PMID 8698936. Therefore repair itself is not novel.

### Additive residual

The article does not yet tell the reader what to do when the adult inevitably fails after making the vow.

Provisional sequence:

1. name the specific miss without defense;
2. acknowledge what it may mean to the child given the prior track record;
3. make the smallest relevant repair now;
4. diagnose why the promise failed;
5. resize/change the promise or environment rather than making a grander vow;
6. return to care without demanding forgiveness or immediate trust.

### Why this changes practice

Without a repair rule, a missed commitment can reactivate exactly the distrust the vow was supposed to repair.

### Suggested placement architecture

Immediately after `Make a Simple Vow`, before `Give the Vow a Physical Reminder`.

### Disposition

**HIGH-VALUE PRACTICAL IMPROVEMENT; non-novel.**

## Priority 3 — No-arrears rule after missed care

### Plain proposition

A missed reparenting act should trigger diagnosis and return, not accumulated care debt.

### Article already owns

- small repeated promises;
- recovering after missing a practice as one sign adult capacity is becoming yours;
- non-cruelty;
- protector dialogue.

### Additive residual

Make explicit that `recover after missing a practice` does not mean compensatory punishment or a backlog of owed healing sessions.

A miss can be treated as information about:

- capacity;
- timing;
- sequencing;
- environmental design;
- avoidance/protection;
- unrealistic promise size;
- technique fit.

Then resume from the present.

### Supplemental evidence

Nearest roots found in this pass:

- self-compassion after behavioral lapses can support continued intention/self-efficacy and reduce negative reactions (PMID 33368932);
- episodic goal persistence explicitly includes returning after pauses (PMID 35069798);
- CBT homework literature studies barriers and quality rather than reducing every noncompletion to simple refusal.

The exact no-arrears reparenting rule was not located in supplemental native search.

### Testable implication

After a missed low-stakes self-care commitment, `resume-now + diagnose` should produce less shame/avoidance and at least equal or better subsequent adherence than `catch up what you owe`.

### Suggested placement architecture

Could be combined with the broken-promise repair section, but should remain conceptually distinct:

- **repair** concerns relational trust after the breach;
- **no arrears** concerns what obligation the breach creates.

### Disposition

**PROMISING PRACTICAL / POSSIBLE CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION; strict status blocked pending Exa + Parallel Task.**

## Priority 4 — Non-withdrawable Nurturer

### Plain proposition

The Guide and Protector may impose limits or choose difficult action, but the Nurturer does not withdraw care because the child fails, refuses, or disagrees.

### Article already owns

- intrinsic/unconditional worth;
- Nurturer, Protector, Guide;
- child remains in relationship;
- no requirement for gratitude/trust;
- adult leadership without exile.

### External evidence / collision

Parental conditional-regard research strongly supports the source mechanism; see PMID 36345118 and PMID 26190774. The root is not novel.

### Additive residual

Turn unconditional worth into an operational rule between the three functions.

A reader can ask:

> When the Guide says no or asks for something difficult, does the Nurturer disappear until I comply?

If yes, the inherited conditional parent may be masquerading as healthy guidance.

### Testable implication

The internal adult should be easiest to distinguish from the inherited critic precisely after mistakes/disagreement, when care remains present while correction or boundaries continue.

### Suggested placement architecture

Best location: `The Three Adult Functions`, especially the Guide explanation; cross-reference `Make a Simple Vow` and `When Love Is There but Doesn't Feel Safe`.

### Disposition

**HIGH-VALUE PRACTICAL INTEGRATION; known root.**

## Priority 5 — Internal jurisdiction / constitution

### Problem in current architecture

The article simultaneously says:

- listen when a part says no;
- adult leads;
- child should not run the whole life;
- Protector handles safety;
- Guide may require difficult things;
- relationship is communion rather than command.

These principles can conflict in concrete cases.

### Known collision

IFS already says parts provide input while Self retains ultimate decision making. Therefore the broad `internal leader decides` claim is prior art.

### Possible residual

Distinguish different kinds of authority:

- **child-state epistemic standing:** present feeling, pain, fear, desire, and subjective tolerance;
- **Nurturer duty:** care remains available regardless of agreement or performance;
- **Protector jurisdiction:** immediate safety and external boundaries;
- **Guide jurisdiction:** long-range direction, education, standards, chosen growth;
- **present adult responsibility:** integrate inputs and own external actions/consequences.

Possible constraint:

- a Protector override should be safety-linked and narrow rather than becoming permanent authority;
- optional introspection should not be forced merely because refusal can be labelled `protection`;
- child-state testimony about pain deserves hearing without automatically deciding the external action.

### Why this might matter

It could resolve the apparent conflict between permissive avoidance and reenacted coercion without pretending that all internal voices have equal decision power.

### Discriminating tests

Use conflict vignettes and compare the jurisdiction model with generic `adult leads` instructions on:

- consistency;
- coercion;
- avoidant permissiveness;
- later trust/repair;
- preservation of long-range commitments.

### Suggested placement architecture

Not ready for article insertion. First subject the residual to mandatory Exa + independent Parallel Task falsification.

### Disposition

**PROMISING-BUT-BLOCKED STRICT CANDIDATE.**

## Priority 6 — Cross-context internalization test

### Plain proposition

A borrowed adult function should not be considered robustly internalized merely because it can be summoned in therapy or a calm ritual context; test it across ordinary contexts and mild stress.

### Evidence / collision

Multiple-context learning and generalization are established mechanisms in exposure/memory research. This is therefore not a strict discovery.

### Article collision

The article already says to test five percent in daily life, act without the helper present, disagree with the helper, and recover after missing a practice. The remaining value is only to make **context variation** explicit.

### Suggested placement architecture

Potential one-paragraph extension to `Become the Adult Apprentice`, if space justifies it.

### Disposition

**USEFUL BUT LOWER PRIORITY; non-novel.**
