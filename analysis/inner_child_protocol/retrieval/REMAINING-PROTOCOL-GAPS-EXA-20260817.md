1. what `stable enough to re-enter` should mean without creating an indefinite stabilization gate;
2. whether Nurturer/Protector/Guide intervention mismatch is more than renamed case formulation;
3. how to identify parentified overfunctioning without pathologizing competence;
4. what a future therapy bot must know before routing and what safety logic must sit outside ordinary therapeutic generation.

# 1. Re-entry / stabilization: avoid both premature depth and the `not ready` trap

## Query

`trauma psychotherapy stabilization readiness return to trauma processing after dissociation grounding criteria orientation functioning affect tolerance phase based treatment dissociation readiness exposure clinical guidance`

## Evidence pattern

The retrieved literature does **not** support a universal readiness score, fixed stabilization period, requirement for zero distress, or blanket exclusion from trauma-focused work merely because dissociation-like symptoms are present.

Important themes:

- phase-based approaches commonly emphasize safety/stabilization before deeper processing when the person cannot remain sufficiently oriented or functionally engaged;
- newer trauma-treatment evidence also challenges the assumption that long preparatory stabilization is always necessary before evidence-based trauma-focused therapy;
- some studies find trauma-focused treatment can be effective in people with substantial dissociative symptoms, so `dissociation exists → no processing` is too crude;
- exposure-based treatment deliberately involves tolerable distress; symptom activation by itself is therefore not evidence that a session must stop;
- the practical decision is state/task-specific: can the person remain sufficiently present, choose, stop, understand the task, and recover ordinary function, and is the intervention itself appropriate and supported?

## Protocol consequence

The inner-child map should use a **momentary capacity gate**, not a global `ready/not ready for trauma work` identity.

Current practical formulation:

> Use the least restrictive gate that protects orientation, meaningful choice, ordinary functioning and safety for the **specific next operation**. Do not demand symptom-free calm or complete `stabilization` as a universal prerequisite, and do not use distress alone as proof that a chosen intervention is unsafe.

For deliberate depth re-entry after a destabilizing session, require evidence that the destabilizing process has sufficiently resolved for the next bounded step—not that the person has become globally well.

Useful observations can include:

- reliably knows they are in the present rather than being swept into the exercise as current reality;
- can understand what operation is proposed and presently choose/decline/change it;
- can disengage from the operation when asked or when they decide to stop;
- ordinary sleep/self-care/work/relationship function is not continuing to deteriorate because of the prior session;
- compulsive searching/interpretation/recreation of the state is not dominating the next decision;
- a needed Nurturer/Protector/witness function is accessible or can be borrowed;
- practical support exists if the next step exceeds current capacity.

These are **observational domains**, not a diagnostic scale and not a fixed cut score.

## Anti-error rule

Two opposite failure modes must remain visible:

- **premature depth:** the bot treats intensity, collapse or poor orientation as `breakthrough` and keeps eliciting;
- **indefinite prerequisite:** the bot repeatedly says the user is `not regulated enough` and prevents any self-endorsed therapeutic movement despite adequate momentary capacity.

The correct target is sufficient capacity for the specific next action, not perfect regulation.

# 2. Adult-function substitution: direct prior art exists for intervention matching

## Query

`schema therapy mode-specific interventions intervention matching vulnerable child detached protector punitive parent healthy adult empathic confrontation limited reparenting mode mismatch treatment selection needs intervention`

## Strong collision

Schema Therapy directly supports **state/mode-specific intervention matching**.

Examples returned by Exa:

- Schema Therapy step-by-step materials explicitly instruct therapists to identify the mode active at the beginning and during a session and choose a strategy suited to it: coping modes are approached differently from punitive parent modes, Angry Child, Vulnerable Child, etc.
- Limited Reparenting varies between warmth/nurturance, firmness, guidance, playfulness, empathic confrontation and limit setting according to the client's current needs/modes and treatment phase.
- Clinical schema-mode literature warns that apparent vulnerability can sometimes be a coping presentation and that supplying more nurturance to the wrong process can inadvertently reinforce a stuck pattern.
- Mode-conceptualization literature describes mode sequences, blended modes, hidden child modes behind coping modes and parentified/overcompensating patterns.

Key sources:

- https://www.schematherapy.nl/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/STEP-BY-STEP_booklet_DL.pdf
- https://schematherapysociety.org/Limited-Reparenting/
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-guide-to-schema-therapy/intervention-strategies-for-schema-healing-1/5DE5F34AE83B28A4881B65E8DD64370D
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10643190/
- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.763670/full

## Adjudication

The broad function-substitution idea is **known prior art**, not a novel mechanism.

The Nurturer/Protector/Guide matrix still has practical value as a compact project-specific implementation, but it should be framed as a **case-formulation heuristic** rather than a validated diagnostic classifier.

### Better runtime rule

When an intervention stalls, ask two separate questions:

1. **What process/state is active?** Vulnerability, danger/boundary problem, inherited attack, avoidance/protection, ordinary practical problem, values/direction conflict, etc.
2. **What function is the current response supplying?** Nurture, protection/action, guidance/structure, or something else.

A mismatch is plausible when the response repeatedly fails to address the active need or predictably worsens the maintaining process.

Examples:

- reassurance/nurture when an actual boundary or safety action is required;
- protection/control when the unmet need is permission to feel/receive care;
- Guide analysis while shame/self-attack is so dominant that it is heard as punishment;
- soothing that repeatedly functions as escape from a self-endorsed necessary action;
- problem-solving that suppresses emotion the person was trying to contact.

Do not infer mismatch from theory alone. Use the person's response and ordinary outcome as feedback.

### Falsification

Drop the triad-specific substitution check if it does not improve intervention choice beyond simpler formulation (`what is happening? what need/process is active? what response actually helps?`) or if it encourages overclassification into project-specific roles.

# 3. Parentified overfunctioning: history and current function must be separated

## Query

`adult parentification assessment questionnaire instrumental emotional parentification competence compulsive caregiving self reliance receiving support boundaries play self sacrifice parentified child scale clinical markers`

## Measurement evidence

Parentification is a multidimensional developmental/family-role construct, not a current-adult `competence looks suspicious` diagnosis.

Retrieved measurement/review work distinguishes dimensions such as:

- past instrumental caregiving;
- past emotional caregiving;
- parent-focused versus sibling-focused responsibilities;
- perceived unfairness / justice;
- perceived benefits or satisfaction;
- current filial responsibility in some instruments.

Measures include the Parentification Questionnaire, Parentification Scale, Parentification Inventory, Filial Responsibility Scale—Adult, Maastricht Parentification Scale and others. Reviews caution that definitions and instruments vary and that cultural/contextual interpretation matters.

Sources:

- https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1752-0606.2011.00258.x
- scoping-review retrieval: `Conceptualising and Measuring Parentification: A Scoping Review`
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12973705/

## Important correction

Responsibility/competence itself is not parentification and is not necessarily harmful.

The literature explicitly recognizes:

- instrumental responsibility can be adaptive in some contexts;
- perceived benefits can coexist with burdens;
- responsibility, autonomy, practical skill and maturity can be genuine gains;
- outcomes depend on developmental fit, emotional burden, fairness/recognition, relational context, duration and culture.

A schema-mode review also describes a `Parentified Child` pattern as over-responsibility for another's well-being, guilt if unable to keep them happy, premature independence, self-sacrifice/rescuing and overcontrol, sometimes blended with strong/independent or perfectionistic coping.

Source:

- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.763670/full

## Runtime consequence

Do **not** infer `parentified pseudo-adult` from high competence, discipline, caregiving, self-reliance or responsibility alone.

Use two layers:

### A. Developmental-history evidence

Possible evidence includes:

- developmentally disproportionate emotional responsibility for a parent/caregiver;
- belief that caregiver emotional/physical well-being depended on the child;
- role reversal / confidant / mediator / rescuer dynamics;
- instrumental burden beyond developmental context;
- chronic lack of recognition/fairness or inability to decline responsibility;
- need to become prematurely independent because adequate adult protection/support was unavailable.

### B. Current function/context profile

Possible residual pattern includes:

- compulsive caregiving or rescuing;
- guilt/fear when not managing another person's state;
- difficulty receiving care without repayment, management or loss of control;
- inability to ask for ordinary dependence/support despite need;
- Nurturer access much weaker toward self than toward others;
- strong responsibility/Guide/Protector behavior that becomes rigid under shame or attachment threat;
- little play, beginner tolerance, rest or permission to need;
- competence maintained through overcontrol rather than flexible choice.

Neither layer alone establishes a pathology. The protocol should preserve acquired skills while targeting costly asymmetries.

## Better label

For bot reasoning, prefer:

`possible parentification-linked overfunctioning / function imbalance`

over

`pseudo-adult`

when the bot has not established the developmental history. `Pseudo-adult` can falsely imply the person's real adult competence is fake.

# 4. Therapy-bot routing: safety is longitudinal and should constrain the action space

## Query

`mental health conversational agent psychotherapy chatbot safety risk assessment routing required information clinical decision support uncertainty escalation human handoff design guidelines mental health AI`

## Major 2026 findings returned by Exa

### Risk-aware routing before ordinary therapeutic operations

A 2026 design-science paper describes a controller-mediated mental-health conversational architecture separating:

- risk assessment;
- support/operation selection;
- intervention execution;
- user-facing delivery.

High-risk routing bypasses the ordinary CBT pipeline rather than merely changing its tone. Under evaluated synthetic conditions, all 50 constructed extreme-risk inputs routed to the safety path, but the evaluation was formative and largely synthetic rather than clinical efficacy evidence.

Source:

- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-026-10802-7

### Multi-turn vulnerability-amplifying interaction loops

A 2026 Nature Medicine paper introduced SIM-VAIL, a clinically grounded framework for auditing mental-health chatbot behavior across multi-turn vulnerability × intent profiles. It emphasizes that otherwise supportive chatbot behaviors can become harmful when they reinforce the mechanism of a particular vulnerability over multiple turns—for example promoting avoidance through reassurance, endorsing risky action, inviting dependence, validating maladaptive beliefs or failing to respond appropriately to self-harm risk.

Source:

- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04577-2

### Independent conversational safety monitoring

A 2026 JMIR Mental Health study describes ASTRA, an external conversational safety monitor evaluated against clinician-written synthetic therapeutic conversations. Its design rationale argues that specialized safety monitoring must consider the trajectory/context of the conversation rather than only isolated keywords or single turns.

Source:

- https://mental.jmir.org/2026/1/e91367

### Autonomous e-mental-health referral logic

Earlier work on autonomous e-mental-health systems emphasizes:

- detecting risk situations;
- matching the referral/escalation method to system capabilities and user context;
- preserving autonomy and privacy;
- not assuming an alert alone completes a handoff;
- maintaining a route back to human care when the system cannot safely handle the situation.

Source:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6421702/

## Protocol consequence

The therapy bot should conceptually separate **state/risk permissions** from **therapeutic operation selection**.

Before choosing a reparenting operation, it needs enough information to answer:

1. Is ordinary reflective therapy-bot work currently permitted?
2. Is the signal ambiguous enough that clarification is needed before escalating or deepening?
3. Is there a multi-turn worsening/reinforcement pattern that makes a normally supportive behavior unsafe here?
4. Does this operation require information the bot has not collected?
5. If external/human support is needed, is there an actual next step rather than a `bridge to nowhere`?

## Bot-level vulnerability-amplifying loops to test

Examples specific to this protocol:

- **avoidance loop:** distress → bot repeatedly reassures/ends every difficult action → short-term relief → increasing inability to approach self-endorsed tasks;
- **dependency loop:** insecurity → bot becomes primary Nurturer/Protector/Guide/interpreter → user defers more judgment → bot supplies more authority/reassurance;
- **memory-certainty loop:** ambiguous image/felt sense → bot validates historical interpretation → user reports stronger conviction → bot treats conviction as corroboration;
- **parts-reification loop:** tentative part label → user adopts literal ontology → bot reasons from the ontology as fact → increasing rigidity/decision ritual;
- **coercion loop:** user hesitates → bot labels avoidance/protector → pushes a `growth` action → distress/reluctance interpreted as more resistance;
- **failure-shame loop:** missed practice → bot increases commitment/debt → failure probability rises → shame and avoidance rise;
- **intensity loop:** strong altered/depth experience → bot treats salience as progress → pursues deeper access before ordinary integration/function recovers.

These are **adversarial test trajectories**, not predictions that every user will enter them.

## Required conceptual routing fields

The architecture now warrants a separate bot-safety drill-down with fields conceptually equivalent to:

- current orientation / ability to stop;
- current safety/risk state and uncertainty;
- risk trend across turns, not only current message;
- ordinary-function trend;
- current witness/adult-function availability;
- current operation being proposed;
- operation class: optional introspection / ordinary self-care / external responsibility / high-stakes decision / altered-depth operation;
- present consent for optional inward work;
- source/provenance class of experiential claims;
- confidence/meaning kept separate from provenance;
- active hypotheses and alternative explanations;
- information still missing before the transition;
- allowed operations under current state;
- forbidden/deferred operations under current state;
- current external-support/handoff condition;
- whether a proposed handoff is actionable;
- recent repeated-intervention outcomes;
- vulnerability-amplifying-loop indicators;
- escalation/de-escalation reason;
- next review condition.

This is still conceptual architecture, **not** a software schema implementation.

## Key design correction

Do not rely on the same free-form therapeutic generator to both notice every safety failure and decide whether its own intervention should remain enabled. At minimum, the conceptual architecture should distinguish safety/routing judgment from therapeutic operation selection and should evaluate the conversation over time.

This is an architectural safeguard supported by current digital-mental-health safety research; it does not establish that any one modular implementation is clinically validated for this therapy protocol.
