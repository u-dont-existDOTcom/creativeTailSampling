## Required runtime formulation

Real external obligations and dose-sensitive treatment requirements are not erased by the rule.

## Consequences retained for the therapy protocol

1. **Misses become diagnostic observations.** Ask about capacity, clarity, cueing, environment, avoidance, relationship/authority reactions, sequencing, and task design.
2. **Resume from the present.** Do not mechanically add missed self-care sessions to a debt ledger.
3. **Preserve therapeutic function.** Resizing cannot shrink a task until the active mechanism disappears.
4. **Repeated misses escalate formulation.** A pattern is evidence that the current plan is failing in some specific way.
5. **External consequences remain real.** Harm, bills, commitments to other people, safety requirements, and time-sensitive obligations may still require action/repair.
6. **Dose-sensitive treatment is separate from debt.** Adequate exposure/practice may be necessary; this should be clinically planned rather than punitive.

## Discriminating test

A useful direct test would compare commitment-accounting policies after misses:

- punitive accumulation / `make up what you owe`;
- ordinary rescheduling;
- no-arrears + structured diagnostic review + present-tense recommitment.

Measure:

- resumption latency;
- adherence / actual therapeutic dose;
- shame / self-criticism;
- avoidance;
- dropout;
- self-efficacy;
- ordinary functioning;
- target symptoms/outcomes;
- adverse effects.

No-arrears should be narrowed or rejected if it reliably lowers necessary therapeutic dose, increases avoidance, worsens outcomes, or delays appropriate escalation relative to non-punitive alternatives.
