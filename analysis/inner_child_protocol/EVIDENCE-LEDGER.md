# Inner-child therapy protocol — Evidence ledger

Date: 2026-08-17
Scope: evidence relevant to **operational protocol nodes**, not an assertion that the complete composite protocol has been validated as a single treatment.

## Evidence-status vocabulary

- **DIRECT** — evidence is close to the operational claim itself.
- **ADJACENT / TRANSFERRED** — source-domain evidence supports the principle but not the exact inner-child implementation.
- **CONCEPTUAL / OWNER THESIS** — important design claim not established as an empirical treatment mechanism.
- **SAFETY SUPPORT** — evidence supports a conservative safeguard rather than treatment efficacy.
- **MIXED / LIMITED** — evidence exists but leaves important uncertainty.

| Node / claim | Evidence status | Supporting evidence | Limits / counterevidence | Runtime consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Prediction-based trust should use observable behavior | ADJACENT / KNOWN PRIOR ART | Behavioral experiments/expectancy-violation frameworks and exposure learning use predictions/outcomes; article already emphasizes track record | No direct inner-child trust trial | Use concrete domain-specific tests; update trust only as far as evidence warrants |
| No-arrears: missed self-care practice should not become punitive debt | ADJACENT / PRACTICAL SYNTHESIS | CBT homework troubleshooting, relapse-prevention lapse framing, self-compassion after failure, goal re-engagement, habit-return literature support non-catastrophic return and task redesign | ERP/PE and other dose-sensitive treatments show adequate practice/adherence can matter; external obligations remain real; no named validated reparenting no-arrears protocol found | `Abolish punitive accumulation, not accountability or therapeutic dose` |
| Positive contact/play should exist outside crisis | DIRECT developmental evidence; ADJACENT self-reparenting transfer | Parent-child play systematic review and attachment/play studies link positive affect, warmth, responsiveness, synchrony and play with relationship/developmental outcomes | Does not prove scheduling inner-child play improves adult outcomes | Include ordinary positive relationship; do not imply distress is manipulative or withhold care during crisis |

| Altered-state intensity/depth is not integration | CONCEPTUAL distinction + ADJACENT evidence | Psychedelic literature separates acute experience from later integration; systematic review finds integration widely considered important but frameworks under-validated; intensity can correlate with benefit | No single agreed empirical definition/measure of `integration`; mystical intensity can correlate positively with outcomes | Track access/intensity/depth and integration separately; never use intensity alone as healing proxy |
| Cumulative unresolved integration should gate further deliberate depth | MIXED / SAFETY SUPPORT | Integration/adverse-event literature describes lingering distress, disorientation, unresolved material and extended integration; adverse effects can emerge after dosing | No validated universal integration-load score or minimum wait period; repeated sessions can be appropriate in structured protocols | Keep provisional state-based gate on restored ordinary capacity, not calendar time |
| External-helper authority/dependency should be monitored | DIRECT general psychotherapy ethics/dependency; ADJACENT bundle mapping | Boundary/multiple-role ethics, care-dependency, transference and psychedelic-therapy ethics all identify power/dependency/authority/suggestibility risks | A therapist appropriately can provide several functions; multiple roles are contextual rather than automatically harmful | Monitor disagreement/exit/alternatives/domain expansion and autonomous functioning rather than counting roles alone |
| Helper N+P+G+memory-interpreter+belonging bundle is a distinctive nonlinear risk | NOT ESTABLISHED | Components individually have known risks | No evidence located for a special nonlinear bundle-specific mechanism | Reject nonlinear mechanism claim; retain bundle only as a risk checklist |
| Routine outcome/process/adverse-effect monitoring improves fault detection | DIRECT general psychotherapy evidence | Meta-analyses of routine outcome monitoring/feedback show small average benefit and larger benefit in `not-on-track` cases; feedback can reduce deterioration / improve reliable change in some systems; negative-effect measurement literature supports explicit adverse-event tracking | Effects are heterogeneous and not universally positive; measurement itself can have adverse effects in some vulnerable groups if feedback is poorly delivered | Track outcomes + process + adverse signals; use measurement to trigger re-formulation, not to shame |
| `No improvement yet` should route to differential failure diagnosis | ADJACENT / OWNER CORRECTION | Measurement-feedback and case-formulation literature supports identifying not-on-track cases and changing treatment; psychotherapy mechanisms are multi-causal | The exact ordered fault tree is project architecture, not validated hierarchy | Localize misunderstanding, implementation, dose, sequencing, prerequisites, delivery, protocol gaps, adjunct need, narrower mechanism before broad inference |
| Bot safety/risk permissions should precede ordinary therapeutic operation selection | DIRECT digital-mental-health design/safety evidence; not treatment-efficacy evidence | 2026 risk-aware conversational-agent architectures separate risk assessment/routing from operation selection; simulated evaluations show structured escalation behavior can be tested independently | Evaluations are often synthetic/formative; they do not validate this therapy bot or define universal clinical thresholds | Conceptually separate permission/risk routing, operation selection and user-facing delivery; high-risk/out-of-scope states bypass ordinary inner-child operations |
| Mental-health chatbot safety must be evaluated over multi-turn trajectories | DIRECT 2026 safety-audit evidence | SIM-VAIL audits show concerning behavior can accumulate over turns and supportive-seeming behavior can reinforce vulnerability-congruent mechanisms; ASTRA emphasizes conversation-level monitoring | Specific simulated vulnerability profiles/ratings do not prove harm rates in all real-world users | Add longitudinal trajectory review and adversarial tests for avoidance, dependency, memory certainty, parts reification, coercion, failure debt, intensity chasing and model sealing |
| Independent/specialized safety monitoring is preferable to assuming the therapeutic generator will catch all of its own failures | SAFETY SUPPORT / DIGITAL-HEALTH ARCHITECTURE | 2026 conversational-safety systems separate monitoring/risk gating from free-form support and show benefits under controlled evaluations; older autonomous e-mental-health work emphasizes explicit referral/risk-management pathways | Exact modular implementation depends on deployment context; synthetic performance can overstate real-world reliability | Keep safety/routing conceptually inspectable and independently testable; do not let therapeutic plausibility expand its own permissions |
| Broad owner thesis: dysfunctional inner-child/inner-adult relationship ultimately requires some form of reparenting | CONCEPTUAL / OWNER THESIS | Several therapies use corrective attachment/reparenting/Healthy Adult/parts mechanisms, offering convergent plausibility | No evidence located that establishes this as a universal necessity across all people/conditions; multiple effective therapies use other explanatory models | Preserve as explicit owner thesis; do not present as settled empirical fact; allow strong contrary evidence to challenge it after local implementation alternatives are examined |

## Protocol-level evidence warning

The assembled Mermaid protocol is a **research-stage synthesis**. Individual components have varying support, but the full Nurturer/Protector/Guide + borrowed-adult + child/protector routing + identity + altered-state + repair + failure-diagnosis + longitudinal bot-safety architecture has not been validated as one unified intervention. A future therapy bot must not present the topology itself as established clinical efficacy evidence.

Likewise, recent safety papers can support architectural safeguards without proving that any particular deployment is safe. Synthetic/red-team performance, clinician ratings and proof-of-concept routing tests are not substitutes for real-world clinical validation, monitoring and governance.
