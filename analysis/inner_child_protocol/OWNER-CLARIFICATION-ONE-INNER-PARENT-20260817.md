# Owner clarification — one inner parent, three qualities

Date: 2026-08-17
Status: **OWNER-LOCKED CONCEPTUAL CLARIFICATION**
Article editing: not yet authorized

## Canonical claim

There is **one inner parent / integrated adult**, not three separate inner parents.

`Nurturer`, `Protector`, and `Guide` are three distinguishable **qualities/functions of that one inner parent**:

- the inner parent nurtures;
- the inner parent protects;
- the inner parent guides.

The protocol separates these qualities because they may be unevenly developed, context-specific, temporarily inaccessible, confused with maladaptive substitutes, or borrowed/developed one at a time. This analytic separation must not be mistaken for an ontological claim that three independent inner parental entities exist.

## Developmental target

The target is:

`borrow/develop qualities separately when useful → integrate them into one coherent inner parent`

not:

`construct three separate inner parents`.

A mature inner-parent response may express several functions at once. Example:

> `I care about you and I am not abandoning you` **(nurture)** + `I will keep us safe / hold this boundary` **(protection)** + `we still need to do this difficult thing, and here is how` **(guidance)**.

## Borrowed adulthood implication

`Borrow one function at a time` means borrowing **one quality of parenting** when that quality is inaccessible. It does not mean installing a separate internal parent for each function.

External models may also be distributed across people without changing the internal target. One person may model warmth particularly well and another boundaries or judgment, while the eventual internalization is into the user's own **single integrated adult/parental presence**.

## Bot-language rule

Prefer language such as:

- `your inner parent's nurturing side/quality/function`;
- `the protective function of your adult self`;
- `what would your inner parent need to do here—nurture, protect, guide, or some combination?`;
- `one adult response may contain all three`.

Avoid language that casually implies three independent agents, such as:

- `which of your three inner parents is speaking?`;
- `the Nurturer and the Guide disagree about who should win`;
- inviting the user to create three distinct parental characters unless that is explicitly their own useful metaphor and remains nonliteral.

## Article implication

When article editing is authorized, the section currently organized around `The Three Adult Functions` should explicitly explain near its beginning that these are **three parental qualities of one inner parent / integrated adult**.

The article should preserve the usefulness of separating the functions while immediately preventing the likely misreading that reparenting requires building three separate inner parents.

Suggested conceptual sequence (not approved article prose):

1. introduce the **one inner parent / adult self**;
2. explain that healthy parenting has at least three core qualities: nurture, protection, guidance;
3. explain why they are separated analytically—the reader may have one without another;
4. repeatedly return to the integration target: one parent that can flexibly do all three.
