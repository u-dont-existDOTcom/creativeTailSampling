### Primary target: research-induced institutional monoculture / exploration collapse

Core question:
> If a federation research center successfully identifies and spreads a current `best practice`, can it destroy the institutional diversity/natural experiments required to discover better practices later or adapt when conditions change?

Source-domain attack:
- exploration vs exploitation / multi-armed bandits;
- policy experimentation / laboratories of democracy;
- organizational ambidexterity;
- evolutionary diversity / portfolio search;
- technology monoculture/systemic risk;
- adaptive management.

Target communal applications:
- movement-wide governance templates;
- admission systems;
- labor accounting;
- child/family practices;
- land/economic models;
- conflict systems;
- reproduction/fission architecture;
- federation research recommendations themselves.

Candidate operational controls:
- distinguish safety/rights floors from optimization experiments;
- preserve an explicit exploration budget or minority experimental lane;
- prohibit universal rollout when evidence is highly context-dependent;
- maintain counterfactual/control communities where ethical and voluntary;
- treat convergence itself as a research variable;
- require stronger evidence before eliminating the last materially different institutional variant.

Novelty bar:
- generic `diversity is good`, policy experimentation, bandits, and laboratories-of-democracy arguments fail;
- only a distinctive communal research-center architecture or stopping rule can survive.
