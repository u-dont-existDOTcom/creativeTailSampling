## Empirical sequence

### 1984–1987: Short Mountain edge case

A detailed FEC history reports that Short Mountain, a queer community/sanctuary in Tennessee, entered the FEC orbit in 1984 and dropped membership in 1987 because its queer-only sanctuary policy was treated as conflicting with the federation's anti-discrimination principle.

The source is a later historical reconstruction, so do not overstate the exact original assembly reasoning without the minutes.

### Older FEC operational distinction

An older full-member criteria page says blanket restrictions based on sex/gender/age/race/sexual orientation/gender identity and similar categories are not acceptable, explicitly noting prior rejection of one-gender communities and acknowledging the controversy.

The same rule says demographic quotas are acceptable, including gender-balance or age-balance limits.

Historical East Wind policy demonstrates the distinction in practice: during housing scarcity and gender imbalance, available housing went to the minority gender at a three-to-one ratio until the adult population returned to the target balance band.

### September 2025 criteria revision

The current FEC full-member criteria document linked by the official site says it was revised September 12, 2025.

It explicitly warns that the criteria are guides and many cases will require judgment calls about situations that cannot be anticipated.

Current criterion 9 says communities should not have discriminatory restrictions on joining based on protected/oppressed categories, but **may be formed around affinity for a specific oppressed identity if they do not discriminate against other oppressed identities** and should work to reduce structural barriers to access.

Do not claim this 2025 revision was caused by the 1987 Short Mountain rupture. What is established is a historically salient edge case plus later policy evolution.

### G-028 — B — Federation principles need a constitutional casebook, not only abstract clauses

The federation needs a versioned casebook/regression suite containing:

- facts;
- principles in tension;
- independent initial judgments where feasible;
- final rationale;

- affected-party input/consequences;

- later outcome;

This does not automate the substantive answer. It prevents the federation from losing the reasoning and having to reconstruct the same hard constitutional tradeoff under each new crisis.
