# A. C017 target-domain attack

## Candidate under attack

**C017 — Intervention-aware evaluation of rescue-triggering community forecasts**

Core rule:

> Do not use the intervention-produced outcome as an unadjusted label for the forecast that triggered the intervention.

## Target searches

Screened nearby implementations included:

- cooperative and mutualist security/solidarity funds;
- credit-union contingency liquidity and emergency support;
- cooperative-development solidarity funds;
- ecovillage resilience/emergency programmes;
- humanitarian forecast-based/anticipatory action;
- member-organization emergency funding.

### Close but non-colliding target structures located

1. **Cooperative/mutualist solidarity funds** can intervene on request by member institutions and can provide crisis or resilience finance.
2. **Credit-union systems** require liquidity forecasting and contingency funding plans.
3. **Ecovillage networks** organize resilience, emergency recovery, early-warning and mutual-support activities.
4. **Humanitarian anticipatory-action systems** explicitly link forecasts/triggers to pre-disaster finance and then use counterfactual/quasi-experimental evaluation to estimate intervention impact.

The fourth is the strongest adjacent precedent and confirms the causal problem rather than collapsing C017: once action follows a warning, observed outcome is partly an intervention outcome.

## Collision test

No close intentional-community/cooperative mutual-aid implementation was located in which:

1. a member community submits a probabilistic forecast of its own future failure/shortfall;
2. that forecast can trigger federation rescue/support;
3. the later evaluation explicitly treats the forecast as referring to a specified **no-new-intervention** or other counterfactual regime rather than naively scoring it against the post-rescue outcome.

**Disposition: C017 survives provisionally.**

The causal theory is not novel; only the target-domain operational transfer remains claimed.

# B. Orthogonal candidate batch

At least fifteen plain candidates were generated before selection.

## T124-01 — Rights can be live individually but dead under correlated demand

**Plain claim:** A grievance, appeal, emergency-housing, advocacy, mediation, or federation-review route may work for one claimant but fail when several communities invoke it simultaneously after one movement-wide shock.

**Nearest neighbors:** queueing/capacity planning, disaster surge capacity, ombuds/grievance mechanism resource scaling, complaint-system overload.

Target/adjacent guidance already explicitly recognizes scaling grievance resources and excess-demand response.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; STRONGLY PRACTICAL.

Operational rule: stress-test rights/remedies against plausible correlated invocation loads, not only one-case procedural reachability. Size alternate reviewers, emergency beds/funds, advocates, records capacity and response deadlines for surge states or define lawful overflow/escalation routes.

## T124-02 — Strategic quorum withdrawal

**Plain claim:** A minority can sometimes block a decision or review by refusing to participate rather than voting against it.

**Nearest neighbors:** quorum busting, strategic abstention, legislative walkouts, distributed-consensus nonparticipation.

**Verdict:** REJECT.

Practical implication already covered by C015/L057: specify quorum-loss and noncooperation defaults.

## T124-03 — Shared federation services create a hidden blast radius

**Plain claim:** Communities that appear institutionally independent can fail together if they all depend on one federation accountant, identity system, cloud account, insurer, transport hub, landholding vehicle, or records service.

**Nearest neighbors:** common-mode failure, shared-service concentration, supply-chain/systemic risk.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.

## T124-04 — Preserve uncommitted movement capacity

**Plain claim:** Allocating every person, bed, vehicle, dollar or skilled role to current optimization eliminates option value for unforeseen crises/opportunities.

**Nearest neighbors:** reserve margins, slack resources, real options, resilience engineering.

**Verdict:** REJECT.

## T124-05 — Rescue can suppress selection against fragile community designs

**Plain claim:** A federation that repeatedly rescues weak designs can make them appear viable and preserve them in the movement population.

**Nearest neighbors:** soft-budget constraints, bailout moral hazard, ecological rescue effects, treatment/confounding.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL RESEARCH CONTROL.

Record rescue/subsidy exposure separately from unaided robustness.

# Batch 24 disposition

## Retained Creative Tail survivor

- **C017** survives target-domain attack.

## New Creative Tail survivors

- **None.**

## Practical lessons to mirror into `communities`

1. stress-test important rights/remedies under **correlated invocation**, not only one-case liveness;
2. a federation-level bypass is not redundant if every local route depends on the same overloaded/captured appellate body;
3. map shared federation-service blast radius across accounting, records, identity, transport, land, cloud, insurance and specialist personnel;
4. record and learn from near misses, not only realized harms;
5. account for administrative/legibility bias when allocating scarce rescue resources;
6. C009 cohort admission must include applicant waiting/unraveling costs;
7. size mutual-aid reserves for correlated/systemic scenarios rather than independent expected claims;
8. shared roving experts/personnel can transmit bad practices as well as good ones.

## Method lesson

A cross-domain operational idea should not be promoted merely because the target domain uses less formal language. If the target/adjacent practice already recognizes the same structural problem—here, grievance capacity and excess-demand planning—the idea belongs in the practical lessons layer unless the transfer changes the operational design in a genuinely nonstandard way.
