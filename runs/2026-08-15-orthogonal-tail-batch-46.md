# Orthogonal Tail Batch 46 — Confidential Transfer Interest as Behavioral Evidence

Date: 2026-08-15

## Outcome

**No Creative Tail survivor.**

Revealed-preference, exit/voice, residential-mobility, internal-labor-market and search-friction literatures own the root mechanisms.

However, a federation-wide private transfer-interest channel is a useful communal institution and a useful complement to incentive-sensitive surveys.

---

# Core idea

A member who fears saying `I am unhappy here` may still privately explore whether another community would fit better.

A federation can provide a confidential channel where members can:
- express nonbinding interest in other communities;
- browse openings;
- request private introductions/visits;
- declare hard household/location constraints;
- join a transfer waitlist;
- do so without local leadership being notified until the member chooses to proceed.

This is primarily a mobility/right-to-exit institution. Its research value is secondary.

## Literature collision

Residential-mobility experiments show that search assistance/information can substantially change realized movement, indicating that observed staying does not directly reveal strong preference to remain when barriers are high.

Internal talent-market research shows that nominal freedom to move can be constrained by managers and organizational frictions.

Exit/voice research treats available alternatives and mobility as part of response to dissatisfaction.

**Disposition: no novelty claim.**

---

# Practical controls

## B46-L01 — Make exploration private until the member chooses disclosure

Local leaders should not automatically receive:
- who browsed openings;
- who asked for a conversation;
- who joined a nonbinding interest pool.

Exceptions may be necessary for immediate safeguarding/legal reasons, but privacy should be the default.

## B46-L02 — Separate exploration from commitment

Stages can include:
1. private browsing;
2. anonymous/pseudonymous fit inquiry;
3. confidential conversation;
4. voluntary identity disclosure;
5. visit/trial;
6. formal transfer process.

This lowers the cost of learning about alternatives without turning every curiosity into a threatened exit.

## B46-L03 — Never treat transfer interest as a direct dissatisfaction score

Interest can reflect:
- curiosity;
- romance/family;
- work/skill match;
- climate/location;
- preferred values;
- conflict;
- housing;
- desire for novelty;
- severe dissatisfaction.

Use it as one behavioral channel.

## B46-L04 — Compare transfer interest with survey and exit data

Useful patterns:
- high satisfaction + low transfer interest;
- high satisfaction + high private transfer interest — possible survey pressure or strong alternative attraction;
- low satisfaction + low interest — possible weak alternatives/exit barriers;
- low satisfaction + high interest.

Interpret jointly with available alternatives and moving costs.

## B46-L05 — Record search exposure

A person cannot reveal a preference for alternatives they do not know exist.

Track:
- openings shown;
- communities considered;
- search assistance offered;
- mobility/geographic constraints;
- household constraints.

Transfer demand is conditional on the opportunity set.

## B46-L06 — Lower mobility barriers where legitimate

Possible federation supports:
- transport/visit grants;
- temporary housing;
- portable records/benefits;
- neutral exit valuation help;
- childcare/logistics support;
- C013/C045 matching support.

Reducing barriers improves both autonomy and interpretability of staying/leaving behavior.

## B46-L07 — Prevent local retaliation for exploratory transfer behavior

A confidential exploration channel is useless if being discovered causes:
- housing/work retaliation;
- governance exclusion;
- social punishment;
- loss of benefits.

Federation rules may need explicit protection against retaliation for good-faith exploration.

## B46-L08 — Do not let the federation pressure members to transfer

The same infrastructure can become a soft-expulsion mechanism if local communities route inconvenient members into the transfer system.

Separate:
- member-initiated exploration;
- mutually agreed transfer;
- disciplinary/separation processes.

## B46-L09 — Preserve household/couple constraints

Connect to Batch 45: household-linked transfer exploration may require same-community or nearby-community configurations.

## B46-L10 — Use aggregate research outputs only with privacy thresholds

Possible aggregate metrics:
- proportion privately exploring alternatives;
- reason categories;
- time from interest to visit/transfer;
- communities with high inbound/outbound interest;
- outcome after transfer.

Suppress small cells where local identification is easy.

## B46-L11 — Follow non-movers too

Someone can explore alternatives and choose to stay after learning more. That is informative.

Record whether exploration leads to:
- transfer;
- recommitment;
- local change/voice;
- no action.

## B46-L12 — Staying becomes more interpretable when exit is actually usable

A low exit rate in a community where moving is financially/socially impossible says little about preference.

As portability and search improve, staying behavior becomes somewhat more preference-informative—though never a complete welfare measure.

---

## Disposition

No Creative Tail survivor.

Practical rule:

> **Give members a private federation-level way to explore other communities without first confronting local leadership. Treat the resulting behavior as one incentive-reduced evidence channel, while explicitly conditioning it on the alternatives and mobility support actually available.**
