# Batch 13 — Adversarial Screen of C007 and Exit-Liquidity Rejection

Date: 2026-08-15

## Purpose

Attack C007 (`cumulative false-positive vigilance trap`) against operational safeguarding/personnel-vetting systems rather than preserving it merely because intentional-community guidance is less formal.

Also test a new operational candidate: communal exit-equity bank-run dynamics.

---

# 1. C007 adversarial result — DEMOTE from creative-tail survivor

## Original transfer

C007 combined three source-domain facts:

1. genuinely dangerous/capture-seeking members are plausibly a low-base-rate target;
2. positive predictive value can remain low even for apparently respectable sensitivity/specificity;
3. repeatedly exposing harmless members to weak screens creates cumulative opportunities for false flags.

The proposed target-domain rule was an explicit evidence lifecycle rather than an ever-growing suspicion counter.

## Stronger precedent collision

The deeper search found that operational safeguarding and vetting systems already implement much of the supposedly distinctive architecture.

Examples:

- U.S. federal continuous personnel vetting screens continuously but separates information collection from adjudication and evaluates reliable positive and negative information under a whole-person framework.
- Personnel-vetting work explicitly tries to reduce false alarms in automated screening rules.
- UK child-safeguarding procedures classify allegations into outcomes such as substantiated, false, unfounded, unsubstantiated, or malicious; some procedures explicitly state that allegations found false/unfounded/unsubstantiated/malicious should not be carried into references, including repeated cleared concerns.
- U.S. federal child-abuse investigation rules direct investigators to review prior conduct but exclude allegations that ended in an unfounded finding or administrative closure.
- Dynamic-risk research explicitly finds that predictive validity decays with time and supports reassessment rather than indefinite reliance on stale risk scores.

Thus the architecture

> alert -> investigation/adjudication -> outcome classification -> mitigation/clearing -> context/recency -> later reassessment

is already standard enough in adjacent high-stakes domains that it should not remain a Creative Tail Sampling survivor merely because informal communities often lack it.

## What remains useful

The Bayesian base-rate illustration and repeated-exposure calculation remain useful **explanations** for why a community should not accumulate weak suspicion flags indefinitely.

But under the project's anti-elaboration rule, a strong explanatory transfer of an established safety architecture is not enough novelty.

### Status

**DEMOTE C007 to D008 — low-base-rate evidence-lifecycle safeguard.**

Preserve the applied rule:

- risk flags are hypotheses, not convictions;
- record retention and evidentiary weight are different;
- cleared/unfounded signals should not silently continue accumulating weight;
- correlated reports do not become independent simply by repetition;
- irreversible sanctions require conduct-specific evidence and independent review;
- evidence age/context/mitigation matter;
- repeated screening creates its own false-positive exposure budget.

Do not call this a creative-tail discovery.

# 2. Candidate T107 — Exit-equity bank run

## Plain proposition

A communal-property system can be solvent in long-run asset value yet destabilized by expectations that other members will redeem scarce liquid exit equity first.

If departures are paid first-come-first-served from liquid reserves while most wealth is tied up in land/buildings, an anticipated wave of exits can create an incentive to exit early, making the feared liquidity crisis self-fulfilling.

Possible loop:

> fear of future payout shortage -> early exit/redemption -> reserve depletion/forced asset sale -> worsening expected payout -> more early exit.

## Why it initially looked tail-worthy

Existing intentional-community discussion often frames exit equity as fairness, ownership, or usable-exit design. The bank-run transfer reframes it as a **liquidity mechanism with first-mover incentives**.

That would imply design choices such as:

- liquidity reserves;
- scheduled/deferred redemption;
- pro-rata treatment during mass exit;
- no first-come priority during a defined liquidity event;
- transparent redemption capacity;
- avoiding guaranteed appraised-value puts against illiquid communal property.

## Literature collision

This candidate fails the novelty gate.

The neighboring cooperative-capital literature already recognizes almost exactly the mechanism:

- cooperative-bank literature explicitly discusses the theoretical possibility of a **"run on capital"** when members can withdraw and demand reimbursement of shares;
- cooperative law commonly permits delaying/redistricting redemption when payout would threaten the cooperative's financial well-being;
- worker-cooperative literature treats member-exit repurchase obligations as potentially severe liquidity demands and designs delayed/rollover mechanisms around them;
- intentional-community/land-trust literature already warns that a community may be unable to compensate a leaving member without selling land, and some current IC financial-design guidance explicitly conditions exit payments on the community's liquidity and ability to preserve essential property/functions.

Thus the bank-run language is a direct transfer into a target area that already has the liquidity problem substantially recognized.

### Status

**REJECT T107 AS NOVEL.**

Retain only as applied finance vocabulary if useful elsewhere.

---

# 3. Protocol lesson from Batch 13

A target domain need not use the source domain's exact terminology for a candidate to fail novelty.

- "evidence-state machine" failed because safeguarding/vetting already has outcome states, clearing, recency, mitigation, and adjudication.
- "bank run" failed because cooperative/intentional-community finance already recognizes withdrawal/redemption liquidity pressure and designs around it.

The novelty question is structural equivalence, not vocabulary match.
