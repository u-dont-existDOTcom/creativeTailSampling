# Adversarial Tail Batch 07 — Property-Gated Variation, Decomposability, and Reproductive Variance

Date: 2026-08-15

## Goal

Attack Batch 06 survivors C003 and C004 against nearer literatures, while running another orthogonal search for structural facts about communities that **reliably reproduce**, not merely survive or produce many descendants in aggregate.

## Result summary

- **C003 asset-gated cultural mutation: RETAINED provisionally, but narrowed.** Generic property-rights → innovation and material exit-cost mechanisms are known. The still-surviving piece is specifically **collective branch inheritance as a pre-selection filter on which institutional variants become adequately capitalized competitors**.
- **C004 governance commutativity: DEMOTED.** Near-decomposability, modularity, task interdependence, and interface governance already contain too much of the structural claim. Commutativity remains a potentially useful operational test, not a creative-tail finding.
- **C005 reproductive-variance trap: SURVIVED.** Mean daughter production can substantially overstate how reproducible a community design is. The full offspring distribution, especially zero-daughter probability and variance, determines extinction risk.
- **C006 reproductive timing: RETAINED AS DERIVED MEASUREMENT RULE, not promoted as a tail discovery.** Population growth depends on both amount and timing of reproduction; this is classic demography, but the transfer changes what a community-spreading project should measure.

## T55 — Asset-gated cultural mutation, adversarial pass

### Candidate under attack

Property/split rules can control a movement's effective institutional variation rate by determining which branches inherit enough accumulated capital to become viable organizations.

### Nearest-neighbor collisions found

1. **Religious exit-cost literature** explicitly treats loss/renunciation of possessions and property arrangements as material exit costs that can deter individuals from leaving communitarian religious groups.
2. **Church-property law** has extensive doctrine and litigation over whether breakaway congregations retain buildings/assets or whether property remains with the denomination.
3. **Property-rights/innovation research** finds that allocation of ownership rights to strategic assets can affect the quantity and quality of innovation inside firms.

These collisions eliminate any broad claim that 'property affects exit/innovation.'

### Narrow survivor

The still-distinct target claim is about **collective branch inheritance**, not individual exit:

> An incumbent reproduction architecture can alter the set of institutional variants exposed to selection by deciding which organizational branches receive inherited capital before competition among variants begins.

This predicts a specific inferential error: a long-lived canonical community form can appear superior to alternatives even when alternatives were systematically prevented from starting with comparable inherited resources.

The important comparison is therefore not merely 'can dissenters leave?' but:

- can one person exit with enough resources to restart life?
- can a **coherent group** fork with enough capital to instantiate an alternative community design?
- can only parent-approved daughters inherit communal capital?
- are fork resources allocated by ideological approval or by ex ante process/viability criteria?

### Status

**RETAIN C003 PROVISIONALLY.** Demote immediately if scholarship is found that already explicitly models branch asset portability as a mutation/variation-generation filter in organizational evolution.

Relevant literature screened:

- Catherine Magelssen, "Allocation of property rights and technological innovation within firms," *Strategic Management Journal* 41 (2020), DOI 10.1002/smj.3103.
- Jared Bok, "Religious Exit Costs," *Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion* 64 (2025), DOI 10.1111/jssr.12963.
- extensive church-property/schism law literature and litigation, including trust-clause disputes.

## T56 — Governance commutativity, adversarial pass

### Candidate under attack

Federation coordination burden depends on density of non-commuting decisions; redesign shared state so more local decisions become order-independent.

### Literature collision

This collapses too strongly into established **interdependence / decomposability / modularity** traditions:

- Simon-style near-decomposability already concerns subsystems that operate largely independently while retaining weaker cross-subsystem dependencies.
- Organizational-design research explicitly models how task interdependence and decomposability determine feasible divisionalization/hierarchy.
- Polycentric-governance theory treats autonomous but interdependent decision centers and the need for coordination where interdependencies exist.
- Modular interorganizational governance explicitly treats interface governance as the mechanism for integrating imperfectly modular actors/problems.

### Residual useful tool

The distributed-systems question

> Does A then B leave materially the same shared state as B then A?

can still be useful as a **concrete operational diagnostic** for a particular dependency. But it is a technical restatement/test for an established structural design principle, not enough novelty for promotion.

### Status

**DEMOTE C004.** Preserve as an applied operationalization only.

Relevant literature screened:

- Yue Maggie Zhou, "Designing for Complexity: Using Divisions and Hierarchy to Manage Complex Tasks," *Organization Science* 24 (2013), DOI 10.1287/orsc.1120.0744.
- Carlisle & Gruby, "Polycentric Systems of Governance: A Theoretical Model for the Commons," *Policy Studies Journal*, DOI 10.1111/psj.12212.
- near-decomposability/modular interorganizational-governance literature.

## T57 — Reproductive-variance trap

**Status:** SURVIVED CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION.

### Plain proposition

A movement's mean number of viable daughter communities per parent can be high while the underlying community model remains **poorly reproducible**.

The missing variable is the **offspring distribution**: how daughter production is distributed across parent communities.

### Sharp example

Compare two hypothetical designs, both with mean viable daughter count = 2.

**Design A:** every mature community produces exactly 2 viable daughters.

**Design B:**

- 90% produce 0 daughters;
- 10% produce 20 daughters.

The arithmetic mean is 2 in both.

Under a simple Galton-Watson branching model:

- Design A has no extinction path once reproduction begins under these assumptions;
- Design B has eventual extinction probability equal to the smallest solution of `q = 0.9 + 0.1 q^20`, approximately **q = 0.9181**.

Thus a movement can have `R = 2` and still have about a **91.8% chance of eventual extinction from one founding community** if reproduction is sufficiently lottery-like.

This is standard branching-process mathematics; the cross-domain finding is what it does to the intentional-community success criterion.

### Why this matters to the target problem

'Has this movement produced many communities?' and 'Is this community design reproducible?' are different questions.

A movement can become historically large because a few exceptional parent communities produced huge numbers of descendants even if a randomly chosen daughter community is very unlikely to reproduce successfully.

That creates a **superstar reproduction trap**:

> aggregate spread can conceal low typical reproducibility.

### Consequence 1 — scale can be a lucky-survivor statistic

Conditional on a high-variance lineage avoiding early extinction, the surviving realization can become enormous. Looking only at the movement that exists today can therefore make a fragile reproductive process look robust.

### Consequence 2 — the right unit is a reproduction distribution

For each sufficiently mature community, record at minimum:

- `K` = number of viable daughters founded;
- `P(K=0)` = zero-daughter rate;
- median and variance/dispersion of K;
- daughter survival/viability criterion;
- whether daughters themselves become reproductive.

Mean K alone is inadequate.

### Consequence 3 — multi-generation reproduction matters

A parent that founds 20 daughters has not demonstrated a **copyable community model** if those 20 all depend on exceptional parent resources and none can found granddaughters.

The stronger test is lineage depth/distribution across generations, not first-generation daughter count alone.

### Target-domain screen

Searches found:

- intentional-community/Hutterite research measuring fission size and cycle length;
- church-planting work measuring plant survival and whether churches reproduce;
- organizational/religious-schism research;

but no obvious target-domain use of the **full branching offspring distribution / extinction probability** to distinguish aggregate movement spread from typical community reproducibility.

Hutterite demographic research is especially interesting because fission has historically occurred on a fairly regular cycle (about 14–15 years in the 1878–1970 data), suggesting a possible empirical contrast between **regular reproduction** and movements whose growth depends on a small number of extraordinary reproducers. This is only a research hypothesis at present.

### Distinguishing predictions

- Two community movements with equal mean daughter output should have different long-run lineage survival when their zero-daughter probabilities/variances differ.
- Movements dominated by a few hyper-reproductive parent communities should show weaker median daughter-to-granddaughter reproducibility than movements with more evenly distributed reproduction, even if total community counts are similar.
- Historical scale should overstate replicability most strongly when the offspring distribution is highly overdispersed.

### Demotion conditions

Demote if intentional-community/organizational-reproduction literature is found already using the branching offspring distribution (not merely average fission rates) to make substantially this distinction between movement growth and model reproducibility.

Source-domain references:

- branching-process literature on offspring distributions and extinction probability, including *Life* 15 (2025), 1910, DOI 10.3390/life15121910.
- Christophe F. D. Coste, branching-process approach to lifetime reproductive success, *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* 17 (2026), DOI 10.1111/2041-210X.70163.
- Carolyn L. Olsen, Hutterite fission demography, *American Anthropologist* 89 (1987), DOI 10.1525/aa.1987.89.4.02a00040.

## T58 — Reproductive timing / generation interval

### Plain proposition

Equal lifetime daughter output does not imply equal movement expansion rate. **When** daughters become viable/reproductive matters.

This is classic age-structured demography, not a new mathematical insight. Population growth rate depends on both reproductive level and reproductive timing.

### Target consequence

A community-spreading project should measure:

- time from founding to first viable daughter;
- distribution of daughter-founding ages;
- time from daughter founding to granddaughter reproduction;

rather than only lifetime daughter count.

A design that reliably produces fewer daughters but much earlier can spread faster than a design producing more daughters only after long maturation.

### Status

**DERIVED MEASUREMENT RULE, NOT PROMOTED AS A CREATIVE-TAIL FINDING.** The transfer is useful but intuitively close enough to 'shorter generation time spreads faster' that it does not clear the user's novelty bar by itself.

Source-domain reference: generation-time / net-reproductive-rate literature, including *The American Naturalist* 183 (2014), DOI 10.1086/675894.

# Batch 07 selection

## Strongest survivor: C005 — reproductive-variance trap

This is now the strongest connection for the user's stated success criterion because it distinguishes:

- **historical spread** — many communities exist;
- **mean reproduction** — parents average >1 daughter;
- **typical reproducibility** — an ordinary daughter has a high probability of itself becoming reproductive;
- **lineage robustness** — the process has a low extinction probability and does not depend on rare jackpot parents.

A movement can score high on the first two and badly on the latter two.

## C003 survives, narrowed

The broad property/innovation/exit mechanisms are old. Keep only the specific branch-inheritance / pre-selection-filter claim until a closer precedent is found.

## C004 demoted

Commutativity can be retained as an engineering diagnostic, but near-decomposability/interdependence already owns the conceptual territory.

## Exact next test

1. Try to reconstruct offspring distributions for real reproducing community systems (Hutterites, Bruderhof if data permit, church-planting networks, kibbutz/communal lineages where relevant).
2. Ask whether extraordinary spread comes from low `P(K=0)` across many parents or from a few superstar founders.
3. Separate daughter count from viable **granddaughter** production.
4. Tail-sample consequences of high reproductive variance that are not already standard branching-process observations.
5. Continue orthogonal sampling; do not let branching mathematics become another elaboration trap.
