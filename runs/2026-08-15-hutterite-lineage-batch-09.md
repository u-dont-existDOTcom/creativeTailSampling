# Empirical Batch 09 — Reconstructing the Manitoba Hutterite Offspring Distribution

Date: 2026-08-15

## Goal

Move C005 (`reproductive-variance trap`) from a mathematical possibility to an empirical test on a real, long-lived reproducing communal system.

The key question is not merely whether Hutterites produced many colonies. It is:

> Was colony reproduction broadly distributed across mature colonies, or did a small minority of hyper-reproductive colonies account for most daughter colonies?

That distinction is central to the user's success criterion of **copyability/reproducibility**, rather than historical scale alone.

---

## Sources and reconstruction

### Manitoba Historical Society colony genealogy

The Manitoba Historical Society's Hutterite colony table provides, for each listed colony:

- colony name;
- foundation year;
- parent colony;
- `Manitoba Daughter(s)`.

The daughter field is explicitly Manitoba-only. Therefore the reconstruction below is a **Manitoba lineage statistic**, not a complete North-American lifetime offspring count. Some Manitoba colonies founded daughters in the United States or other provinces, so direct-daughter counts here are conservative and apparent zeros can be false zeros for total reproduction.

### Historical completeness consistency check

The reconstructed table contains **62 Manitoba colonies ever founded by the end of 1975**.

A 1977 Manitoba historical monograph reports **60 operating colonies at the end of 1975**. Two colonies in the genealogy are explicitly recorded as having closed before 1975:

- Thorndale (Sharpe): founded 1924, closed 1929;
- Roseisle: founded 1929, closed 1936.

Thus `62 ever founded - 2 closed = 60 operating`, exactly matching the historical total.

This is not proof that every lineage edge is complete, but it is a strong consistency check that the historical colony list itself is not merely a survivor sample through 1975.

### Cross-border undercount warning

The historical appendix `MANITOBA HUTTERITE COLONIES — COLONY DIVISION INTERVALS 1918–1975` includes division destinations marked `(U.S.A.)`, confirming that the MHS `Manitoba Daughter(s)` field omits some true daughters.

Therefore:

- reported `K` values below are **recorded Manitoba daughters**;
- `P(K=0)` is an upper bound on the true total-daughter zero probability if any apparent zeros founded daughters outside Manitoba;
- variance can be distorted by differential cross-border founding.

Do not interpret the reconstruction as a full North-American colony genealogy.

# Main result: older Manitoba cohort is strongly non-jackpot-like

To reduce right-censoring, the most informative first cohort is colonies founded **by 1970**.

For the 49 reconstructed colonies founded 1918–1970:

- `n = 49`
- total recorded Manitoba daughters = **103**
- mean `K = 2.102`
- median `K = 2`
- population variance `Var(K) = 1.724`
- variance / mean = **0.820**
- recorded-Manitoba `P(K=0) = 2/49 = 4.08%`
- top 10% of parent colonies (5 colonies) account for only about **22.3%** of recorded daughters.

The two recorded zeros are:

1. **Roseisle**, which closed in 1936 only seven years after founding;
2. **Pembina** (founded 1961), for which no Manitoba daughter is listed.

Because the source omits non-Manitoba daughters, the true total-daughter zero rate could be lower than 4.08%.

## Interpretation

This does **not** look like a superstar reproduction process.

The distribution is, if anything, mildly **underdispersed** relative to a Poisson distribution with the same mean (`variance/mean ≈ 0.82`). Reproduction is spread across almost the entire mature cohort rather than being generated by a small minority of extraordinary colonies.

This is exactly the pattern C005 predicts for a highly reproducible lineage:

> low zero-offspring probability + reproduction distributed broadly across parent units.

The result does not prove that low reproductive variance caused Hutterite persistence, but it supplies a real empirical example of the difference between **reliable reproduction** and **jackpot reproduction**.

# Non-stationarity and censoring

The 1971–1985 cohort differs sharply from the 1918–1970 cohort in the Manitoba-only reconstruction:

- 1918–1970: 2 zero-Manitoba-daughter colonies out of 49;
- 1971–1985: 14 zeros out of 23.

A simple 2×2 comparison is extremely different statistically, but causal interpretation would be premature for at least four reasons.

## 1. Right-censoring

Later-founded colonies have had less time to reach fission maturity.

## 2. Geographic censoring

Only Manitoba daughters are counted. Later colonies may disproportionately establish daughters elsewhere.

## 3. Branching regime changed

Recent Hutterite research reports that colonies increasingly introduced industrial operations specifically to create jobs and **delay the need to branch**. Thus the historical fission hazard is not stationary.

## 4. Exceptional historical shocks

Research on Manitoba branching reports that the 1990s Schmiedeleut schism accelerated branching and created some prematurely founded/undercapitalized colonies. Land availability and land prices also changed the geography and timing of branching.

Therefore the older cohort is currently the cleaner descriptive test of whether long-established Hutterite reproduction was jackpot-like.

# C005 verdict after Batch 09

## Empirically strengthened

C005 survives and is now more than a toy branching-process example.

The older Manitoba Hutterite lineage shows:

- almost universal recorded reproduction among mature colonies;
- median reproduction close to the mean;
- no dominance by a few hyper-reproductive parents;
- variance not larger than the mean;
- repeated multi-generational organizational fission over a century-scale history.

This is consistent with the hypothesis that one ingredient of robustly spreading community systems is **low variance in parent-level reproduction**, not merely high average reproduction.

## Still not demonstrated comparatively

We still need at least one movement with a genuinely parent-resolved, strongly overdispersed reproduction distribution to show that the distinction explains meaningful differences in movement robustness.

The U.S. church-planting literature is suggestive because many congregations never plant another congregation, but available aggregate reports do not yet provide a directly comparable full parent-offspring distribution with adequate follow-up.

# Important demotion: large-propagule / seedability branch

A tempting consequence was:

> Hutterite fission may be reliable partly because each daughter inherits a large fraction of an already functioning society, so reliable fission does not demonstrate that the system can be recreated by a small founder team.

This distinction is useful, but it does **not** clear the novelty gate.

Cultural-evolution and evolutionary-life-cycle literatures already analyze how larger/symmetric propagules preserve complex institutions/traits more faithfully and how propagule size trades off fidelity against reproduction rate.

Therefore preserve only a measurement distinction:

### D006 — Propagule burden / independent seedability

When evaluating a 'copyable' community, report how much mature social/material structure must be transferred into each daughter:

- number/fraction of experienced people;
- capital/land/tools;
- continuing parent/federation support;
- whether the daughter can later reproduce without the same extraordinary subsidy.

A model can be highly reliable at **fission reproduction** while being difficult to instantiate de novo from a small founder group.

Useful, but not a Creative Tail Sampling discovery.

---

# Next empirical test

The strongest next comparison is a community movement with:

1. identifiable parent→daughter relationships;
2. enough mature parents to estimate an offspring distribution;
3. many zero-daughter parents and some prolific parents;
4. sufficient follow-up for daughter→granddaughter reproduction.

Potential targets:

- church-planting networks with parent-child identifiers;
- denominational congregation genealogies;
- other intentional-community federations with explicit daughter lineages;
- communal religious groups with documented fission trees.

The comparative question is now precise:

> Holding mean reproduction roughly comparable, does lower parent-level reproductive variance predict deeper, more robust organizational lineages?
