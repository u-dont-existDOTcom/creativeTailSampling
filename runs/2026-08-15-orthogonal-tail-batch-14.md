## T108 — Arithmetic-growth mirage under shared environments

**Status:** SURVIVED CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION; provisional.

### Plain proposition

For a reproducing community movement exposed to time-varying common environments, a pooled arithmetic mean of daughter production can say “above replacement” even while the typical lineage shrinks toward extinction.

The relevant long-run object is multiplicative growth across environments.

### Source-domain result

Classic population and branching-process work in random environments distinguishes arithmetic expected population growth from typical long-run growth. Under standard branching-process-in-random-environment assumptions, criticality is determined by the expected log of the environment-specific mean offspring number (`E[log m]`), subject to regularity conditions—not simply by `E[m]`.

Lewontin and Cohen’s 1969 result makes the paradox explicit: expected population size can grow without bound while extinction probability approaches one under environmental variation.

### Sharp illustration

Suppose successive movement-wide environments are equally likely to be:

- favorable: expected viable daughters per parent `m = 4`;
- hostile: expected viable daughters per parent `m = 0.1`.

Arithmetic mean:

`E[m] = (4 + 0.1)/2 = 2.05 > 1`.

A naive “average reproduction number” says strong growth.

But the geometric mean is:

`sqrt(4 × 0.1) ≈ 0.632 < 1`,

and

`E[log m] ≈ -0.458 < 0`.

Under a standard iid random-environment branching model satisfying the usual conditions, this is subcritical despite the arithmetic mean above 2.

The numerical values are illustrative, not estimates of any real community movement.

### Why C005 is not enough

C005 concerns **within-environment parent-level reproductive variance**: are daughters produced broadly or by jackpot parents?

C008 concerns **environment-level multiplicative variation shared across many parents**: does the same historical/legal/economic period raise or suppress reproduction across the movement simultaneously?

A movement can therefore have:

- low parent-level reproductive variance within each period;
- high arithmetic mean daughter output overall;
- yet poor long-run lineage persistence because bad common environments dominate multiplicatively.

### Three-step consequence

1. Researchers pool daughter counts across decades/jurisdictions and calculate mean reproduction > 1.
2. A few very favorable periods contribute extremely high daughter counts and lift the arithmetic mean.
3. Reproduction is nevertheless multiplicative across generations: a severe hostile period shrinks the lineage base from which later growth occurs.
4. If typical log growth is negative, favorable periods do not compensate in the way the arithmetic average suggests.
5. A historically large surviving movement can therefore look far more robust under pooled mean reproduction than its environment-conditioned lineage process actually is.

### Measurement rule

For movements exposed to meaningful common environmental variation, record reproduction conditional on macroenvironment rather than reporting one pooled `R`.

Candidate fields:

- legal/jurisdiction regime;
- land-price/access regime;
- recession/boom period;
- persecution/legal-hostility period;
- war/migration shock;
- policy regime affecting communal ownership/schooling/taxation;
- environment-specific offspring distribution;
- serial correlation/duration of regimes.

Estimate the long-run stochastic/log growth process only where data/model assumptions make that defensible.

### Operational implication

Many daughter communities in the **same failure domain** are not many independent environmental bets.

Movement-level robustness can be increased by reducing common environmental dependence—for example geographic/jurisdictional/economic diversification—but that generic diversification point is not itself novel. The tail contribution is the criterion showing why arithmetic average reproduction can give the wrong sign.

### Target-domain screen

Intentional-community literature explicitly recognizes that legal status, land, markets, public administration, and socio-economic context affect viability. The targeted search did not locate community-reproduction work applying branching-process-in-random-environment or `E[log m]` logic to distinguish pooled average daughter output from long-run lineage persistence.

### Demotion conditions

Demote C008 if target-domain community/organizational-reproduction scholarship is found already applying substantially this stochastic-log-growth criterion.

Also demote if, for the movements of interest, macroenvironmental variation is mostly idiosyncratic across communities rather than shared enough for the random-environment distinction to add information beyond ordinary C005 offspring variance.

## T110 — Federation-wide deferred-acceptance matching for prospective members

### Candidate

If many communities have limited openings and many seekers apply simultaneously, independent bilateral recruiting can generate timing pressure and unstable allocations. A federation could collect ranked preferences and use deferred acceptance/stable matching rather than first-come/early-offer recruitment.

### Target screen

The intentional-community ecosystem already contains:

- large searchable directories;
- ICmatch compatibility filtering/matching between seekers/founders;
- community waitlists and ready-to-join pools;
- matchmaking events.

The targeted search did not locate a federation operating a Gale-Shapley-style allocation of scarce residential slots.

### Why not promote

The transfer is technically nonstandard but currently too contingent:

- many communities are not simultaneously capacity-constrained;
- community preferences over sets of members can involve complementarities/diversity constraints rather than simple rankings;
- households/couples complicate stable matching;
- preferences change substantially after visits and trial membership;
- no evidence yet that timing/unraveling is an important movement bottleneck.

**Verdict:** PROMISING TECHNICAL POSSIBILITY, NOT A FINDING.

Promote only if empirical evidence shows substantial multi-community matching failures that the mechanism addresses.

## T112 — Recruitment bullwhip / delayed-membership overshoot

**Candidate:** Long recruitment/onboarding delays plus recruitment effort based on current vacancies can generate waves: undercapacity -> aggressive recruiting -> many entrants arrive together -> integration overload/overcapacity -> recruiting stops -> later attrition produces another deficit.

**Target collision:** community/coliving guidance already recognizes that absorbing many newcomers at once can destabilize culture/onboarding, and historical community guidance documents desperate admission waves under financial stress.

The delayed-feedback control framing sharpens the mechanism but does not clear the user’s novelty bar.

**Verdict:** REJECTED AS NOVEL.

# Batch 14 selection

## Promote provisionally: C008

The useful surprise is not “bad years matter” or “diversify jurisdictions.” It is the sign reversal:

> `arithmetic mean reproduction > 1` does **not** guarantee positive long-run lineage growth when reproduction varies multiplicatively across shared environments.

This extends C005 from parent-level stochasticity to environment-level stochasticity without collapsing into the same statistic.

## No operational flagship emerged

The search did not produce a new grand operational architecture. Deferred-acceptance matching remains technically interesting but lacks evidence that the target bottleneck is important enough to merit promotion.
