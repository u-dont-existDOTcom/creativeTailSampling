## F001 — Modernity may have specialized relationships, not merely labor

A central structural change in modern society may be the **unbundling of multiplex relationships**.

A multiplex relationship connects the same two people across several functions at once. A person can simultaneously be kin, neighbor, coworker, childcare backup, trading partner, confidant, ritual companion, emergency help, source of information, and political ally.

Modern institutional life tends to split those functions across separate people and institutions:

> coworker ≠ neighbor ≠ childcare provider ≠ therapist ≠ landlord ≠ friend ≠ teacher ≠ elder ≠ emergency help ≠ political collaborator ≠ religious community ≠ romantic partner

The important distinction is between **occupational specialization** and **relational specialization**. A sophisticated society may be able to preserve the first without requiring the second.

### Recovered consequences

- repeated-game incentives operate across domains;
- shared context accumulates across domains;
- reciprocity can cross domains and remain loosely accounted;
- relationships can persist when one shared function disappears;
- reputation is harder to compartmentalize;
- existing relationships can absorb new needs without creating a separate institutional interface for each one;
- romantic partners can become pathological residual multiplexers after surrounding relationships unbundle;
- several apparently separate modern problems may share relational unbundling as a contributing cause.

## F002 — Epistemic independence may require deliberate communication boundaries

Collective intelligence can fall when communication synchronizes people before they form independent estimates. Temporary epistemic firebreaks — independent observation/deliberation first, aggregation afterward — may sometimes improve collective judgment.

This remained provisional because it sits close to known correlated-error / wisdom-of-crowds ideas.

## F003 — Final package ratification can matter after component-wise choice

Clause-by-clause majority decisions can assemble a total package that a majority would reject when evaluated as a whole. Final whole-package ratification can therefore be non-redundant.

Any model must retain realistic options such as reject-both and status quo; paradoxes produced by deleting those options were rejected.

## Novelty rule learned from these rejections

Do not count a proposition merely because it is true. It must remain non-obvious after plain restatement, survive compression attempts into familiar named concepts, produce consequential downstream implications, and remain coherent under realistic choice sets.
