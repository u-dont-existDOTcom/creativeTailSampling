# Orthogonal Tail Batch 32 — Frequency Dependence, Scale Closure, and Best-Practice Replication

Date: 2026-08-15

## Outcome

The batch produced strong research-design lessons, but the underlying mechanisms are already established in policy scaling/general-equilibrium research, frequency-dependent selection/congestion models, and ecovillage scholarship on external embeddedness and context-sensitive replication.

# A. Strongest practical result — every "best practice" needs a scaling regime

A community research center should not record merely:

> practice X worked well in community Y.

It should also classify **why the payoff might change when adoption rises**.

Useful categories:

### 1. Approximately scale-invariant

The benefit is expected to remain similar as more communities adopt, subject mainly to local implementation quality.

### 2. Frequency-dependent

The payoff changes with prevalence/adoption.

Examples:
- many communities adopt the same niche business and saturate the market;
- many communities recruit the same scarce member profile;
- many depend on the same donor/grant niche;
- one strategy works partly because surrounding institutions use a different strategy.

### 3. Shared-capacity limited

The practice works at low adoption because a finite external resource is available:
- specialist trainers;
- mediators;
- clinicians/professionals;
- land/site opportunities;
- finance;
- federation staff;
- suppliers/markets.

### 4. Wider-system dependent / not scale-closed

The community imports critical inputs from the surrounding society that it imagines partially replacing:
- trained adult recruits;
- professional education;
- technology;
- capital;
- public infrastructure;
- external markets;
- specialized services.

A design can be highly successful as a niche while failing as a universal model.

### 5. Positive network-effect / increasing-return

Some practices may improve as adoption grows because a larger movement creates:
- intercommunity mobility;
- shared training;
- larger markets;
- federated insurance;
- specialist pools;
- political leverage.

Do not assume scale effects are always negative.

# B. Source-domain collision

## Policy scaling and general equilibrium

Economics/policy-evaluation literature directly studies why small-scale treatment effects can change when interventions scale, including general-equilibrium spillovers, saturation, implementation, and market responses.

Therefore `a successful local intervention may not scale` is not novel.

## Frequency dependence and congestion

Evolutionary-game/congestion/minority-game traditions already model payoffs that depend on how common a strategy becomes.

Therefore `a strategy can stop being best when everyone adopts it` is not novel.

## Ecovillage target-domain evidence

Ecovillage/intentional-community research already documents that:

- communities remain embedded in and dependent on wider socioeconomic systems;
- wider-system dependence can inhibit or enable transition/decarbonization;
- ecovillage enterprise interacts with mainstream market logics;
- scaling/replication should adapt to context rather than clone one standardized model;
- expansion can alter practices, membership composition and competence.

This is too close for a Creative Tail promotion.

# C. Candidate audit

At least fifteen candidates were screened.

## T132-01 — Best practice becomes worse as adoption rises
**Verdict:** REJECT. Direct frequency-dependent/general-equilibrium principle.

## T132-02 — Same niche business saturates across a movement
**Verdict:** REJECT AS OBVIOUS/KNOWN.

## T132-03 — Recruitment strategy competes for a fixed applicant pool
**Verdict:** REJECT. Matching/congestion/market-thickness logic.

## T132-04 — Shared mediator/professional becomes bottleneck at scale
**Verdict:** REJECT. Capacity/congestion already in practical frontier.

## T132-05 — Scale-closure test

Ask whether a community design can still reproduce its critical inputs if the surrounding mainstream source shrinks as the model spreads.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; STRONGLY PRACTICAL. External embeddedness and scalability literature own the root mechanism.

## T132-06 — Skilled-recruit import dependence

A movement can look self-reproducing while importing adults whose education/skills were produced outside it.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; practical measurement target.

## T132-07 — Capital-import dependence

Expansion depends on wealth accumulated in the system the movement aims to replace.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL. External dependence/capital formation.

## T132-08 — Policy ranking reversal at high saturation

A > B at low adoption but B > A at high adoption.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL. General-equilibrium/frequency-dependent ranking.

## T132-09 — Optimal movement is a portfolio, not one best model

When payoffs are frequency-dependent, movement-level optimum can require a mixture of practices/community types.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL. Mixed equilibria/portfolio/congestion logic; practically useful warning.

## T132-10 — Positive scale effects

Some practices become better as network size grows through shared specialists, mobility, insurance, markets or bargaining.

**Verdict:** REJECT. Network effects/economies of scale.

## T132-11 — Adoption-response curve

Estimate practice outcome against movement adoption/saturation rather than one average effect.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL RESEARCH DESIGN. Dose/saturation/general-equilibrium analysis.

## T132-12 — External-input closure matrix

For each critical input, identify source today and plausible source at target movement scale.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; strong operationalization.

## T132-13 — Scale simulation before evangelism

Before promoting a practice movement-wide, model what changes when 10%, 50%, 90% adopt.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; practical.

## T132-14 — Keep innovations diverse until scale effects known

Avoid premature monoculture of one observed winner.

**Verdict:** REJECT. Portfolio/exploration/diversification.

## T132-15 — Separate demonstration value from universalizability

A niche community can be valuable as a demonstration even if its complete design cannot be universalized.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; important conceptual distinction.

# D. Practical lessons to mirror into communities

1. every claimed best practice should state its expected scaling regime;
2. distinguish local efficacy from movement-wide equilibrium effect;
3. record whether payoff depends on practice rarity/prevalence;
4. identify finite shared-resource bottlenecks before replication;
5. run a **scale-closure matrix** for critical inputs: where do skills, capital, markets, technology, services and infrastructure come from now, and where would they come from if adoption became large?;
6. measure how much a community's success depends on importing adults/skills trained elsewhere;
7. distinguish a valuable demonstration/niche from a universally reproducible design;
8. estimate or at least hypothesize adoption-response curves rather than assuming constant effect;
9. check for positive as well as negative network effects;
10. do not standardize the entire movement around one observed winner before its scale effects are understood;
11. when frequency dependence is plausible, compare portfolios/mixes of community practices rather than only ranking them individually;
12. record wider-system dependence explicitly in public research reports so apparent autonomy is not mistaken for scale closure.

## Method lesson

The target-domain version of a powerful general-equilibrium insight can be very useful without qualifying as a Creative Tail discovery. Here the correct artifact is a **research-center scalability control**, not a new theory.
