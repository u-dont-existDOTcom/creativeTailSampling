## Result summary

One new operational connection survives the current target-domain screen:

### C007 — Cumulative false-positive vigilance trap

For a rare target such as a genuinely dangerous/capture-seeking member, even a seemingly accurate risk screen can have a low positive predictive value. If weak screens are repeated over years and their flags accumulate without clearing, harmless long-tenure members can become increasingly likely to acquire a suspicious record purely from repeated exposure.

This creates a specific design rule:

> **Risk-screen outputs must be treated as temporary hypotheses, not additive convictions.**

Irreversible membership, discipline, or expulsion decisions require conduct-specific evidence and explicit handling of base rates, dependence among signals, adjudicated false positives, and evidence age.

Target-domain community guidance located in this pass discusses trial membership, disclosures, references, criminal histories, good-faith declarations, and ongoing membership decisions, but the targeted search did not identify an intentional-community framework explicitly incorporating low-base-rate predictive value plus cumulative false-positive exposure.

## T92 — Low-base-rate danger screening has low positive predictive value

**Status:** SURVIVES AS ROOT OF C007.

### Plain proposition

If truly dangerous/capture-seeking members are rare, a screening signal can be reasonably sensitive and specific while **most positive flags are still false positives**.

This is Bayes, not new mathematics. The target transfer matters because community admission/discipline is often discussed as though a strong-sounding screening practice could label a person as dangerous rather than merely update uncertainty.

### Illustrative calculation

Assume, purely for illustration:

- prior prevalence of target dangerousness = 1%;
- sensitivity = 80%;
- specificity = 95%.

Among 10,000 people:

- true dangerous = 100;
- correctly flagged = 80;
- harmless = 9,900;
- falsely flagged at 5% = 495.

Thus only:

`80 / (80 + 495) = 13.9%`

of positive flags correspond to the target condition.

Even at 99% specificity with the same sensitivity/prior, PPV is only about 44.7%.

To reach roughly 62% PPV in this illustration requires ~99.5% specificity.

The numbers are examples, not estimates of any actual community screening tool.

### Source-domain precedent

Personnel-reliability/security literature explicitly warns that very low base rates make dangerous-person screening extremely difficult because diagnostics would require extraordinarily high accuracy. This is established source-domain knowledge.

### Target-domain screen

Intentional-community sources found in the targeted search discuss:

- structured trial/provisional membership;
- required disclosures;
- felony/sex-offender/addiction history;
- references;
- good-faith declarations;
- suspension/reconsideration when new information appears.

No located target source framed these procedures through low-base-rate PPV or warned that apparently strong screening signals can remain dominated by false positives.

### Verdict

**SURVIVES AS CROSS-DOMAIN TRANSFER**, but the stronger result is T93 below.

## T93 — Repeated vigilance makes false flags accumulate over tenure

**Status:** SURVIVED; strongest component of C007.

### Plain proposition

Even when each individual safety screen has a small false-positive rate, repeatedly applying weak screens to harmless people causes the probability of at least one false flag to rise with time/exposure.

If the institution retains every weak flag as cumulative evidence, **longer tenure itself creates more opportunities to acquire suspicion**.

### Simple illustration

If independent screens each have a 5% false-positive probability for a harmless person:

- after 1 screen: 5% chance of at least one false flag;
- after 10: `1 - 0.95^10 ≈ 40.1%`;
- after 20: `≈ 64.2%`.

At a 1% false-positive rate:

- after 50 independent screens: `1 - 0.99^50 ≈ 39.5%`.

Real community signals are not independent, so these numbers are illustrative rather than a model of actual complaints. Correlation can increase or decrease particular accumulation patterns; the key point is that repeated exposure makes naive additive flag-counting invalid.

### Cross-domain connection

Repeated medical screening has the same structural problem: imperfect tests can generate substantial cumulative lifetime false-positive risk even when each screening event appears reasonable.

The community analogue is stronger because a weak flag can affect reputation, trust, eligibility, membership, family relations, or later interpretation of ambiguous conduct.

### Three-step consequence

1. A community uses repeated low-stakes signals to detect rare dangerous behavior early.
2. Harmless members occasionally receive false flags.
3. If flags are retained indefinitely and later counted as corroboration, long-tenure members accumulate more opportunities for apparent evidence.
4. Investigators may then treat 'multiple concerns over time' as stronger independent evidence even when some were false, correlated, or generated by the same authority/narrative chain.
5. A vigilance system designed to catch rare capture can therefore gradually manufacture a population of apparently suspicious insiders.

### Non-obvious operational implication

Do **not** model a member's safety record as an ever-increasing count of concerns.

Every signal needs a status, such as:

- unverified lead;
- corroborated conduct evidence;
- contradicted/cleared;
- duplicate/correlated with another signal;
- resolved with remediation;
- stale/no longer predictively relevant;
- legally/reporting-mandated record retained but not treated as guilt.

The system should distinguish **record retention** from **evidentiary weight**.

### Design architecture

For low-base-rate dangerous-person screening:

1. **Screening controls exposure; it does not adjudicate character.** A weak concern can justify reversible precautions or more evidence collection, not a durable dangerous-person label.
2. **Escalation is conduct-specific.** Irreversible sanctions require documented behavior and an independent review path.
3. **Correlated signals do not multiply evidence.** Complaints copied from one narrative, authority chain, rumor, or prior flag are one information cluster unless independently sourced.
4. **Cleared flags stop counting as risk evidence.** They may remain in a protected audit log, but cannot silently remain in a cumulative suspicion score.
5. **Evidence has age and context.** Old weak signals require an explicit rule for relevance rather than permanent additive weight.
6. **Repeated testing is itself part of the error budget.** The community audits false-positive/appeal outcomes as well as false negatives.
7. **Actions scale with posterior uncertainty and cost asymmetry.** Reversible protective actions can use lower evidentiary thresholds than expulsion, public accusation, family separation, property loss, or law-enforcement claims.

### Target-domain collision test

The existing community-research gap bank already contains adjacent safeguards:

- labels are hypotheses, not conduct records;
- multiple reports are not independent when produced within one belief/authority chain;
- conduct-specific evidence and independent review are required;
- long probation/inner work has not been validated as a dangerous-person filter.

C007 does **not** replace those findings. It supplies the quantitative/statistical reason for a further rule that was not explicit there:

> **weak flags must not accumulate indefinitely merely because the same person has been exposed to many screening opportunities.**

The targeted search did not locate an intentional-community membership/safety procedure explicitly handling cumulative false-positive exposure in this way.

### Demotion conditions

Demote C007 if target-domain work is found already applying low-base-rate predictive-value and repeated-testing/multiple-screen logic substantially this way to member-danger assessment.

Also demote if the proposed operational consequence adds nothing beyond existing conduct-specific due process in practice.

# Batch 12 selection

## Promote provisionally: C007 — cumulative false-positive vigilance trap

The source mathematics is standard. The cross-domain result is the operational combination:

- the dangerous target is rare;
- positive predictive value can therefore remain low despite respectable sensitivity/specificity;
- repeated screening creates cumulative false-positive exposure;
- naive retention/aggregation of weak flags can make long tenure itself produce suspicious records;
- a safe community therefore needs an **evidence-state machine**, not a suspicion counter.

This complements the existing research finding that dangerous-person labels must be conduct-specific and independently reviewable.

## No other Batch-12 candidate survived

The rest collapsed into established target or neighboring literatures.

## Exact next attack

Before stronger promotion of C007:

1. search threat-assessment/safeguarding systems for existing operational rules on evidence decay, cleared flags, correlated complaints, and repeated-screen false-positive budgets;
2. determine whether 'evidence-state machine' is genuinely useful beyond normal due process;
3. if it survives, turn it into a concrete community safety data schema and adversarial test cases;
4. continue orthogonal sampling so the project does not become anchored on safety screening.
