## Supplemental collision/evidence checks

### Parentified pseudo-adult

Strong target-domain collision:

- West & Keller (1991), *Parentification of the child: a case study of Bowlby's compulsive care-giving attachment pattern*, PMID 1951790.
- The paper explicitly distinguishes reciprocal adult caregiving from child-origin caregiving organized through the attachment system; the latter suppresses care-seeking while preserving unmet need.

Disposition:

- broad claim `adult-looking caregiving can be child survival` = **known**;
- narrower article-specific mapping = potentially useful: a person may show strong Protector/Guide-like competence while Nurturer access, care-receiving, play/dependence, or bidirectional child-adult relationship remain weak.

Supporting adjacent literature:

- Yakın & Arntz (2023), *Understanding the reparative effects of schema modes: an in-depth analysis of the healthy adult mode*, PMID 37941965. Healthy Adult / child interaction was described as bidirectional, with bonding, balancing, and opposition to critical/demanding voices.
- Schema-therapy literature also treats Healthy Adult functioning as dimensional rather than reducible to one visible competence.

### Cross-context internalization

Direct source-domain prior art:

- Craske et al. / inhibitory-learning exposure literature emphasizes retrieval cues, stimulus variability, and multiple contexts to promote generalization.
- A 2025 systematic review of exposure-therapy generalization (PMID 40273831) identifies limited transfer to treatment-unrelated contexts as a relapse problem and reports multiple-context exposure as a promising strategy.

Disposition: useful transfer criterion, not a strict originality candidate.

### Broken-promise repair

Direct psychotherapy prior art:

- Eubanks, Muran & Safran meta-analysis of alliance rupture repair, PMID 30335462.
- Safran & Muran (1996), PMID 8698936.

Disposition: **known but missing practical improvement**. The article has trust, track record, vows, and repetition but lacks a compact repair protocol for the inevitable missed promise.

### Non-withdrawable Nurturer

### Internal jurisdiction / constitution

Direct root collision:

- IFS Institute's model outline states that when Self leads, parts provide input while Self retains ultimate decision making.

Therefore `adult/Self leads, parts advise` is not new.

Potential residual not resolved by supplemental search:

- Protector safety override is narrow rather than standing;

- optional introspective work should not become coercive merely because reluctance is reclassified as pathology.

Adjacent source domains include pediatric shared decision-making, assent/autonomy, trauma-informed relational consent, and limits on coercion. These make the transfer plausible but do not establish novelty.

## Candidate frontier after Batch 002

### Highest-value practical improvements now

#### P1 — Parentified pseudo-adult as function imbalance

Article already owns: borrowed adulthood, three adult functions, protector work, adult/child relationship.

Additive residual:

- visible competence does not prove integrated adulthood;
- assess each adult function separately;
- assess giving care **and receiving care**;
- some highly competent people may need less `become responsible` and more Nurturer access, dependence, play, receiving, and permission not to manage everyone.

Three consequences:

1. Generic `strengthen the adult` instructions could accidentally reinforce compulsive caregiving in parentified readers.
2. High competence at work/caretaking should not be used as a proxy for self-reparenting capacity.
3. A function-specific assessment predicts asymmetric profiles: strong responsibility/protection with weak self-soothing, help-seeking, play, or care-reception.

Discriminating test:

- compare ability to give competent care under stress with ability to receive care, disclose need, relinquish control, and remain non-defensive while another person helps. A large stable asymmetry supports the pseudo-adult formulation more than a unitary `strong adult` formulation.

Strict status: broad root rejected; local operationalization practical only unless mandatory retrieval later leaves a narrower residual.

#### P2 — Non-withdrawable Nurturer

Article already owns: unconditional worth, Nurturer/Protector/Guide, adult leadership.

Additive residual:

- the adult may say `no`, set limits, or choose difficult growth **without turning warmth into a reward for obedience**.

Three consequences:

1. Failure/disagreement becomes a load test of the Nurturer rather than evidence the child has forfeited care.
2. Guide direction becomes easier to distinguish from the inherited demanding/critical parent.
3. A reader can evaluate internal dialogue by asking whether affection disappears exactly when the child refuses or fails.

Discriminating test:

- evoke a minor mistake or disagreement and observe whether warmth/acceptance remains while boundaries or correction are maintained. Conditional withdrawal predicts a different pattern from the proposed Nurturer rule.

Strict status: known root, practical integration only.

#### P3 — No-arrears rule

Plain proposition:

> A missed reparenting act should trigger diagnosis and return, not accumulated care debt.

Three consequences:

1. If three planned practices are missed, the person does not `owe` three replacement sessions; backlog itself can turn care into punishment.
2. The adult should investigate why the commitment failed—capacity, timing, sequencing, avoidance, unrealistic promise—before making a larger vow.
3. Trust repair is indexed by **return after the miss**, not by compensatory overperformance.

Discriminating test:

- after a deliberately or naturally missed low-stakes practice, compare `catch-up debt` instructions with `acknowledge + diagnose + resume now` instructions on shame, avoidance, next-practice completion, and felt trust in the internal adult.

Strict status: PROMISING-BUT-BLOCKED.

#### P4 — Internal jurisdiction

Plain residual:

> Internal leadership may work better if roles have different kinds of authority rather than one undifferentiated hierarchy.

Provisional jurisdiction:

- **child-state:** privileged witness to current felt experience, pain, fear, desire, and whether optional introspection feels tolerable;
- **Nurturer:** care remains available regardless of agreement or performance;
- **Protector:** immediate safety and boundaries; overrides should be narrow and safety-linked;
- **Guide:** long-range direction, standards, learning, and difficult growth;
- **present adult:** integrates the inputs and carries responsibility for external actions and consequences.

Three consequences:

1. `Listen to the child` no longer means `let the child decide every action`.
2. `The adult leads` no longer licenses reinterpretation of every refusal as pathology or disobedience.
3. Protector emergency authority can be distinguished from chronic internal authoritarianism.

Discriminating test:

- construct repeated conflict vignettes (avoidance of optional imagery, desire to contact an unsafe person, exhaustion versus duty, urge for substance use, disagreement with therapist/Guide) and compare decisions generated by the jurisdiction model versus an unstructured `adult leads/parts speak` model. Measure coercion, avoidant permissiveness, consistency, and later repair/trust.

Strict status: PROMISING-BUT-BLOCKED.
