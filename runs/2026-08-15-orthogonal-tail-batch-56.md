# Orthogonal Tail Batch 56 — Community Resolution Plans: Preserve Critical Functions, Not Necessarily the Institution

Date: 2026-08-15

## Outcome

One provisional cross-domain survivor:

- **C025 — Community resolution / living-will architecture**

The source model is established in financial-institution resolution: regulators distinguish preserving **critical functions** from preserving the failing institution. Essential functions can be transferred to another or bridge entity while the nonviable residual firm is wound down.

The surviving target transfer is to intentional communities:

> **Before crisis, define which member-critical functions must remain continuous if the community becomes nonviable, and preplan how those functions can be transferred, bridged, or made portable without making institutional survival the default objective.**

---

# Source-domain collision boundary

Bank-resolution regimes explicitly aim to preserve critical functions while allowing a failing institution to be restructured, transferred or wound down. Resolution planning / `living wills` exists so failure need not become an improvised bailout.

The Financial Stability Board's framework allows critical functions to continue in a recapitalized entity or a bridge institution while the residual nonviable firm is liquidated.

No source mechanism here is novel.

---

# Target-domain screen

Intentional-community/cooperative documents located in the target domain commonly address:
- dissolution votes;
- liquidation trustees;
- debts/liabilities;
- asset sale/distribution/asset locks;
- legal winding-up procedures.

Examples include FEC/communal operating agreements and community-benefit/cooperative dissolution rules.

A real EcoReality wind-down describes property liquidation and distribution to shareholders after the founders could no longer continue operations.

The screened material did **not** reveal a close target architecture that separately maps:
- member-critical functions;
- continuity requirements;

- portable rights/benefits/records;
- institutional recovery versus orderly member-centered resolution.

This bounded search is not proof no community has ever done it.

# C025 — Community resolution / living-will architecture

## Plain proposition

When a commune enters severe distress, `save the community` and `protect the people/functions the community provides` are different objectives.

The institution may provide:
- housing;
- food;
- income/work;
- medication/care;
- childcare;
- schooling;
- transport;
- records/documents;
- benefits/savings;
- family/social contact;
- land access;
- identity/legal status;
- dispute/appeal systems.

If the legal/social organization is no longer viable or safe, federation rescue can preserve those functions **without necessarily preserving the same leadership, property entity, governance system, or community itself**.

---

# Operational architecture

## C025-01 — Maintain two plans: recovery and resolution

### Recovery plan

How can the existing community return to viability?

Examples:
- temporary liquidity;
- staffing help;
- mediation;
- management support;
- debt restructuring;
- recruitment;
- temporary federation services.

### Resolution plan

If recovery fails or preserving the institution is not in members' interests, how do critical member functions continue during orderly wind-down/transfer?

Do not let `we have no resolution plan` become a reason to keep rescuing a nonviable institution.

## C025-02 — Define member-critical functions in advance

For each community identify which functions cannot simply stop overnight.

Possible classes:
- shelter/housing;
- food/water;
- health/medication/care;
- child education/care/safeguarding;
- cash/benefits/payroll/savings;
- transport;
- identity/legal/medical/education records;
- communication/family contact;
- safe exit/relocation;
- stewardship of animals/land/critical infrastructure.

The list is community-specific.

## C025-03 — Define continuity horizon for each function

Examples:
- medication: no interruption;
- food/water: immediate continuity;
- emergency housing: days/weeks;
- schooling: rapid bridge with longer transition;
- asset payout: months may be tolerable if necessities are protected.

This prevents all functions being treated as equally urgent.

## C025-04 — Map function → provider → fallback provider

For each critical function record:
- current provider/owner;
- dependency/key person/system;
- fallback inside community;
- federation bridge option;
- outside provider where appropriate;
- activation authority.

## C025-05 — Make member benefits/records portable where feasible

Examples:
- personal records;
- vested savings/benefits;
- health/education records;
- qualifications/work history;
- research contact;
- federation membership rights where appropriate.

Institutional collapse should not erase the person's history or entitlements.

## C025-06 — Create bridge housing / bridge community capacity

A federation can maintain limited transition capacity for members leaving a failing unit:
- temporary housing;
- food;
- transport;
- childcare;
- document recovery;
- job/work placement;
- C013/C045/C046 transfer support.

The bridge does not need to replicate the failed community permanently.

## C025-07 — Separate member rescue money from institutional bailout money

A federation may choose to fund:
- household transition;
- emergency necessities;
- portable benefits;
- asset/legal resolution

without giving unrestricted funds to incumbent community leadership.

This is especially relevant where governance capture or mismanagement is part of the failure.

## C025-08 — Predefine recovery-to-resolution trigger

Possible trigger dimensions:
- insolvency/runway;
- sustained inability to provide necessities;
- governance incapacity;
- loss of legally required competence;
- severe population loss;
- member vote;
- failed recovery milestones;
- safeguarding/rights condition where preserving the institution would worsen harm.

No universal trigger is proposed.

## C025-09 — Independent trigger/review route where local leadership has a preservation conflict

Leaders/founders have natural reasons to preserve the organization they built.

When the question is whether that organization should cease/control should transfer, do not make incumbent leadership the sole decision-maker.

Preserve member rights, legal ownership and due process.

## C025-10 — Map the `critical function` independently of the legal entity

Example:

`childcare` is a function.

It might be continued temporarily by:
- another sister community;
- selected families;
- a federation service;
- lawful outside providers.

The fact that the failed community operated childcare does not imply that its childcare department or property entity must survive.

## C025-11 — Protect family/relationship continuity during resolution

Resolution should not scatter households or children mechanically according to asset convenience.

Map:
- households;
- coparents;
- dependents/caregivers;
- child contact;
- essential support ties.

## C025-12 — Protect evidence/records before leadership changes or liquidation

When failure includes conflict/misconduct, records can disappear precisely during wind-down.

Use:
- preservation triggers;
- independent copies/custody;
- access/correction rights;
- privacy controls.

## C025-13 — Plan animal/land/ecological obligations

A failing agricultural/ecological community can leave:
- animals needing care;
- crops;
- water systems;
- hazardous materials;
- land stewardship obligations.

These are resolution functions too.

## C025-14 — Resolution can include transferring viable functions into a successor/daughter entity

Not every failure requires total liquidation.

Possible structures:
- new community with different governance;
- merger into sister community;
- bridge entity;
- subdivision into viable households/projects;
- federation takeover of one function only.

## C025-15 — Asset rules must not crowd out member-function continuity

Legal liquidation rules often focus on creditors/assets. The internal resolution plan should separately show how immediate necessities continue while those processes unfold, within law and creditor/member rights.

## C025-16 — Test the plan before crisis

Use tabletop resolution exercises:

`If this community becomes unable to operate next Monday, where do medication, food, children, records, cash access and housing go?`

A plan that requires the failing leadership/system to function perfectly during failure is not a resolution plan.

# Research predictions

If C025 is useful, communities/federations with explicit resolution architecture should show:
- less pressure for repeated unconditional institutional bailouts;
- faster continuity of necessities during failure;
- less record/benefit loss;
- less forced staying because there is nowhere to go;
- cleaner distinction between supported recovery and orderly closure;
- lower harm when key founders/operators suddenly disappear.

These predictions need real target evidence.

---

# Critical limits

- a federation cannot override property/legal rights merely by calling something `resolution`;
- bridge capacity costs resources and can create moral hazard;
- premature resolution can destroy a recoverable community;
- institutional continuity can itself be valuable;
- resolution authority can become abusive if poorly constrained;
- some functions are deeply relational and cannot be transferred like bank accounts;
- child/family decisions require appropriate rights/legal standards;
- community members must retain meaningful voice and exit rights.

---

## Disposition

**SURVIVES provisionally as C025 — Community resolution / living-will architecture.**

Claim only the intentional-community federation transfer. Bank resolution, living wills, bridge institutions and continuity of critical functions are established source-domain concepts.

Core rule:

> **Design failure so the people and critical functions do not have to fail with the institution.**
