## Empirical collision results

### 1. Cross-community mobility has a distinct interoperability failure mode

Historical FEC/Twin Oaks dual-member policy is the strongest new evidence in this run.

A dual member needed a primary community for tax/property/legal/membership purposes. Switching official residence required coordination across labor, taxes, accounting, health, and PEACH. PEACH costs were reconciled between communities. Crucially, the policy explicitly allowed communities to limit switching frequency if money/labor accounting became too burdensome.

Historical labor-exchange rules also tracked reciprocal labor debts and could restrict outgoing LEX when the destination community already owed a large balance.

East Wind dual-membership/economic-agreement records show analogous home/secondary-community, health/emergency-cost, work, and residence allocations.

Internal corpus collision:

- G-012 already owns portable exit benefits;
- G-016 already names intercommunity migration as continuity infrastructure;
- neither operationalizes compatibility between autonomous community accounting/benefit/status systems.

Residual: **formal permission to move is not the same as administratively usable mobility.**

Disposition: article-gap survivor G-026 (class B), **not** strict Creative-Tail promotion.

### 2. Current FEC rebuild: narrower visible operating surface

The 2026 official FEC site publicly lists:

- monthly calls;
- annual assembly;
- PEACH;
- exchange trips;
- mini-grants;
- reduced outreach during rebuilding.

The old archive also described Community Loan Fund and Mutual Aid Scholarship Fund activity. Targeted searches of the rebuilt official domain did not locate current versions of those two programs. This is recorded only as **unresolved current status**, not evidence of formal termination.

The May 2025 delegate update identified corporate/tax registration, policy review, and website-control recovery as central rebuild priorities.

### 3. Membership topology evolved toward stable non-convergent affiliation

Historical evidence:

- Short Mountain left after a conflict between its queer-sanctuary admission policy and FEC anti-discrimination criteria;
- Allied status later allowed communities such as Ganas and Living Energy Farm to maintain relationships without a full-membership trajectory;
- Re-forming status handled major post-accreditation institutional change.

Current 2026 official site displays:

- Full Member Communities;
- Communities in Dialogue, explicitly transitional toward full membership;
- Friends of the FEC, stable like-minded non-members that either do not meet criteria or choose not to pursue membership.

Alpha Farm moved from Community in Dialogue in the May 2025 rebuild update to Full Member on the 2026 official site.

Disposition: strengthens G-025; do **not** mint a generic `preserve diversity` gap.

### 4. Federation dispute expectations outgrew a pure logistics description

2019 Commune Life/FEC participant material says the FEC was not designed as a governing body, yet people brought member-community conflicts/harms to it. Mediation/conflict and harm/abuse teams were discussed but were difficult to make durable because commune work consumed capacity.

The exact East Wind participant interview about the 2018 assembly was located through AskRigor/YouTube. The uploader chapters the 2018 FEC segment as covering conflict within/across communities, justice, bylaws, expulsions, burnout, racism, and mediation. The transcript itself was not available, so no disputed event reconstruction was inferred.

The 2024 FEC near-collapse account says unresolved Acorn/Twin Oaks grievances and lingering East Wind resentment about the 2018 assembly coexisted with delegate/compliance/capacity failure.

Disposition: strengthens G-023/G-024 and existing jurisdiction/review work; too collisional/obvious to justify a separate gap.

### 5. Formal federation failure did not equal local-network failure

During the March 2024 Twin Oaks wildfire, nearby Acorn sheltered Twin Oaks evacuees and animals. Earlier records show Twin Oaks helping Acorn after a fire, labor exchanges across communities, and persistent Twin Oaks-Acorn business ties.

Current Twin Oaks material still describes its Southern Exposure Seed Exchange relationship after Acorn had left the FEC.

Residual: the practical ecology has multiple overlapping layers:

1. bilateral/local-cluster relationships;
2. function-specific institutions such as PEACH;
3. general federation/corporate coordination.

Disposition: sharpens G-024 (`federation entity != entire network`); not a new gap.

### 6. Acorn departure remains causally underdetermined

The current official FEC site does not list Acorn. Commune Life says Acorn left but the author does not understand why. A later Allen Butcher paper attributes the break to an Acorn-originating conflict that crossed into Twin Oaks when several Acorn members moved there.

The public evidence located in this run is insufficient for blame attribution or a reliable reconstruction. The case is retained only as a stress test coupling mobility, sister-community relations, dispute jurisdiction, and federation affiliation.
