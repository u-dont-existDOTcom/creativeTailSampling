# Orthogonal Tail Batch 41 — Resident-Only Outcome Selection / Leaver Censoring

Date: 2026-08-15

## Outcome

**No Creative Tail survivor.**

The root mechanism is established selection bias / conditioning on a post-treatment variable, with mature causal-inference frameworks such as principal stratification and survivor-average causal effects. Intentional-community quality-of-life research itself already acknowledges selection bias.

However, the communal research-center consequence is foundational.

---

# Core problem

Suppose Community A has a demanding social architecture that causes dissatisfied residents to leave quickly, while Community B retains a broader range of satisfied and dissatisfied residents.

A cross-sectional survey of **current residents only** can make A look happier because the practice changed who remains in the sample.

Likewise:
- a family system can look stable after high-conflict households leave;
- a strict labor system can report high worker satisfaction after low-fit members exit;
- an intense spiritual system can report high commitment because doubters depart;
- a childcare regime can look accepted if dissatisfied parents leave the community;
- an expulsion-heavy community can reduce internal incident rates by removing the people involved.

The outcome is conditioned on a post-exposure state: still being present.

## Literature collision

Causal inference treats closely related problems through selection-bias, principal-stratification and truncation-by-death frameworks: when treatment affects whether an outcome is observed, comparing observed survivors can be causally misleading.

Intentional-community quality-of-life research has explicitly acknowledged selection bias as an alternative explanation for high reported life satisfaction.

Residential/retention research more broadly directly compares stayers and leavers/former candidates.

**Disposition: reject novelty claim.**

---

# Practical research-center architecture

## B41-L01 — Current-resident outcomes are descriptive, not automatically causal

Label them explicitly:

> `Outcome among people still resident at measurement time.`

Do not rewrite that as:

> `Effect of this community design on people.`

## B41-L02 — Create fixed-entry cohorts

For serious comparative research, define cohorts at a clear entry point:
- applicant accepted;
- provisional residence starts;
- full membership begins;
- household/child enters;
- specific governance/labor/family intervention begins.

Follow the cohort for a fixed period **whether or not people remain**.

## B41-L03 — Treat leaving as both an outcome and an observation transition

Record:
- timing of exit;
- voluntary / pressured / expelled / practical necessity;
- stated reasons;
- housing/material consequences;
- family/relationship continuity;
- later wellbeing;
- later view of the community;
- whether exit itself made later follow-up difficult.

Exit is not merely missing data.

## B41-L04 — Report at least three different quantities

1. **Current-resident state** — what current members report now.
2. **Fixed-entry cohort outcome** — what happened to everyone who entered over a defined horizon.
3. **Leaver trajectory** — outcomes after departure and reasons/conditions of departure.

Do not collapse them.

## B41-L05 — Retention and satisfaction must be analyzed jointly

A system can achieve:
- high satisfaction + high retention;
- high satisfaction + low retention (possible selection);
- low satisfaction + high retention (possible exit barriers);
- low satisfaction + low retention.

One dimension cannot stand in for the other.

## B41-L06 — Report why the denominator changed

For every repeated outcome survey, reconcile:
- new entrants;
- departures;
- deaths;
- refusals/nonresponse;
- expulsions;
- children aging into/out of the cohort;
- households splitting/merging.

A trend line with a changing population is not necessarily a within-person improvement.

## B41-L07 — Do not solve the problem by simply penalizing turnover

Leaving can be:
- evidence of mismatch/harm;
- healthy self-selection;
- successful temporary participation;
- planned graduation;
- household opportunity;
- adaptive fission/migration.

Interpret exit type and later outcome rather than assuming all retention is good.

## B41-L08 — Preserve former-member contact infrastructure for research

If ethically and legally appropriate:
- maintain opt-in independent contact channels;
- separate research contact from community leadership;
- offer confidential follow-up;
- preserve ability to participate after departure;
- avoid making benefits/claims dependent on favorable research participation.

## B41-L09 — Use intent-to-treat-like movement estimates where meaningful

For an intervention introduced to a defined membership cohort, an outcome among everyone exposed/eligible can answer a different question from the outcome among those who stayed compliant/resident.

The exact causal method depends on design and assumptions; do not mechanically import clinical-trial formulas.

## B41-L10 — Children/households require trajectory follow-up, not resident snapshots

Especially for children, a community cannot claim success because the children remaining inside appear well if dissatisfied families disproportionately leave.

Follow education, health, family contact, and wellbeing through transitions where ethically feasible.

## B41-L11 — Compare admission/exit selection functions between communities

Some outcome difference can be caused by:
- who is admitted;
- who stays;
- who is asked to leave;
- who can afford to leave;
- who responds to research.

These selection mechanisms belong in the explanatory model.

## B41-L12 — Satisfaction among stayers is still useful

Do not discard it. It answers a real descriptive question:

> `How do people currently choosing/able to remain experience the community?`

Just do not confuse it with movement-wide, entrant-cohort, or causal success.

# Combined research-center warning

Batch 38–41 now define four separate comparison hazards:

1. **C022 measurement comparability:** same construct may be coded differently;
2. **Batch 39 value comparability:** comparable outcomes may be valued differently;
3. **Batch 40 diffusion endogeneity:** recommendations can change the institutional variation being studied;
4. **Batch 41 selection:** practices can change who remains observable.

A communal research center must state which of these gates each claim has passed.

---

## Disposition

No Creative Tail survivor.

Strong practical rule:

> **Never use current-member wellbeing as a standalone measure of community design success. Follow entry cohorts and leavers so a design cannot improve its apparent outcomes merely by changing who remains in the denominator.**
