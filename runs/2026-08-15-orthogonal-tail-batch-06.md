## T46 — Asset-gated cultural mutation

**Status:** SURVIVED CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION.

### Plain proposition

The legal/property rules governing organizational splits can determine the **effective cultural mutation rate** of a reproducing community movement.

The key distinction is between:

- **sanctioned reproduction**: a daughter community recognized by the parent/movement and allowed to inherit capital/resources;
- **unsanctioned cultural fork**: a dissenting faction whose alternative norms or governance would constitute a real variant.

If sanctioned daughters inherit substantial accumulated capital while dissenting forks must leave without it, the movement can reproduce rapidly while exploring very little cultural design space.

### Empirical anchor

Hutterite colonies make the contrast unusually visible. Their own description says that when a colony establishes a daughter, the assets are valued and divided between the home and daughter colonies. Supreme Court of Canada cases describe colony rules under which members who leave or are expelled are not entitled to colony property; one case explicitly records dissidents leaving the colony without a share of accumulated property.

The point is not that Hutterites should structure things differently. They are a natural example showing that **demographic branching and ideological branching can face radically different capital inheritance rules inside the same reproductive system**.

### Three-step consequence

1. New organizational variants require not just ideas and people but capital, land, tools, reputation, networks, and accumulated organizational resources.
2. Property rules determine which kinds of branch receive those inherited resources.
3. Parent-approved branches can therefore have high viability while parent-disapproved variants face a severe founding handicap unrelated to whether their alternative design would work.
4. Observed movement evolution is then not simply 'selection among community designs.' It is **selection among designs that the incumbent reproduction architecture allowed to become adequately capitalized competitors**.
5. A movement may therefore look empirically stable and well-adapted while its institutional search process is highly censored.

### Non-obvious implication: selection blindness

A movement can mistakenly infer that its canonical model outcompetes alternatives when alternatives were rarely given comparable starting capital.

This is analogous to changing a mutation operator before natural selection operates: the mutation-generation process constrains what selection can discover.

### Design consequence

If a movement explicitly wants both fidelity and experimentation, it needs to decide its **fork-capital policy** deliberately rather than treating property distribution as an unrelated legal detail.

One possible architecture is a precommitted **fork escrow / daughter seed right** triggered by process conditions rather than doctrinal approval—for example minimum cohort size, cooling-off period, transparent accounting, nonviolence, and independent viability review. The purpose would be to allow bounded institutional variation without making opportunistic asset stripping easy.

This is a design hypothesis, not a recommendation yet.

### Distinguishing predictions

- Movements where dissenting branches can retain/receive meaningful seed capital should generate more viable institutional/doctrinal variation than otherwise similar movements where exit forfeits nearly all accumulated communal capital.
- High rates of ordinary daughter formation do **not** imply high rates of institutional experimentation if asset inheritance is conditional on parent approval.
- Property/legal architecture should predict the distribution of daughter-community variants even after controlling for intensity of ideological disagreement.

### Demotion conditions

Demote if existing organizational/religious-property scholarship already explicitly establishes property portability as a control on cultural variation/mutation rather than merely documenting property disputes after schism.

## T47 — Conditional fork escrow

**Candidate:** Precommit seed resources to qualified minority forks independent of majority doctrinal approval.

**Status:** DESIGN CONSEQUENCE OF T46, not separate discovery.

The difficult mechanism-design problem is avoiding both extremes:

- zero portability, which may suppress genuine alternatives;
- automatic proportional asset portability, which can create incentives for strategic fission/asset stripping.

A useful fork protocol would therefore condition access on process/viability criteria rather than ideological conformity.

## T49 — Governance commutativity as a scalability criterion

**Status:** SURVIVED CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION.

### Source mechanism

Distributed-systems/computer-systems research uses operation commutativity as a structural criterion for concurrency/scalability: if two operations can occur in either order with equivalent externally visible results, they need not be serialized through the same coordination bottleneck.

### Target transfer

A federation of daughter communities can apply the same test to governance domains.

For two proposed local actions A and B, ask:

> If community X does A while community Y does B, would A-then-B and B-then-A leave the relevant shared movement state materially equivalent?

If yes, those decisions are candidates for independent/local action.

If no, they require some form of coordination, ordering, shared constraint, or conflict-resolution protocol.

### Why this is not merely subsidiarity

Generic subsidiarity says decisions should be made locally when possible. The commutativity transfer gives a sharper **diagnostic and design objective**:

> Don't only ask which existing decisions can be decentralized. Redesign shared state so that **more decisions become commutative**.

Examples of architectural moves that can increase commutativity:

- partition a single shared discretionary budget into preallocated local budgets;
- give daughter communities separate land/resource namespaces where actions cannot create competing claims;
- distinguish local practices from a minimal set of movement-wide promises;
- make shared registries append-only where possible instead of requiring one group to overwrite another's state;
- predefine resource ceilings or interfaces so independent choices remain compatible.

This converts some coordination problems into interface-design problems.

### Three-step consequence

1. Federation growth increases the number of potentially simultaneous local decisions.
2. Coordination cost rises mainly where those decisions can change the same shared state incompatibly.
3. Centralizing all decisions prevents inconsistency but destroys autonomy/scalability; decentralizing all decisions creates conflict.
4. By restructuring assets, scopes, namespaces, and interfaces, the federation can make a larger share of decisions order-independent.
5. Therefore governance scalability may depend less on raw population/community count than on the **density of non-commuting decision pairs**.

### Distinguishing predictions

- Coordination burden should rise more sharply with the number/density of cross-community state conflicts than with raw movement size after controlling for ordinary meeting frequency.
- Federations that partition resources and decision domains so local actions rarely conflict should scale to more daughter communities with less growth in central governance overhead.
- Governance reforms that merely delegate authority without reducing shared-state conflicts should produce more disputes than reforms that redesign domains to reduce non-commutativity.

### Literature screen

Polycentric-governance research clearly studies multiple autonomous decision centers, overlap, subsidiarity, coordination, and the distinction between functional polycentric systems and mere fragmentation. A 2026 community-governance paper emphasizes modularity, forkability, and polycentricity. The targeted search did **not** locate an explicit target-domain use of operation commutativity/non-commutativity as the criterion for what can safely decentralize or as a design objective for increasing decentralizable operations.

This is therefore kept as a cross-domain connection, not a claim of a new theorem about governance.

### Demotion conditions

Demote if governance/federalism literature is found that already uses substantially the same order-independence/commutativity criterion, or if non-commuting decision density adds no explanatory value beyond existing measures of resource/interdependence overlap.

## T53 — Active normative edge-case search revisit

**Status:** C001 RETAINED.

Target-domain searches found intentional-community values statements, case studies, governance agreements, consensus case studies, and deliberative-democracy 'value mapping,' but no obvious precedent for adaptively selecting the next concrete case based on expected information gain and then preserving resolved high-information cases as a regression suite.

The active moral-preference-elicitation literature does, however, warn that active questioning can fail when moral preferences are unstable, sequence-sensitive, noisy, or poorly represented by the assumed model.

**Protocol refinement for C001:**

- collect independent baseline responses before discussion;
- randomize/replicate some cases to detect sequence effects;
- treat preference change after deliberation as a separate phenomenon from initial preference measurement;
- do not pretend the group has one fixed latent utility function if responses are path-dependent.

## Keep

Still survives as a useful cross-domain governance method, with stronger caveats around preference instability.

### C003 — Asset-gated cultural mutation

Current strongest reproduction-specific connection. It separates **how often a movement branches** from **how much institutional variation its branching process can actually generate**.

Most important downstream insight:

> A movement can reproduce extremely well while being evolutionarily/search-wise almost sterile if only parent-approved variants inherit the capital required to survive.

### C004 — Governance commutativity

Current strongest distributed-systems transfer. It proposes a concrete scalability criterion and, more importantly, a design objective: restructure shared state so a larger fraction of daughter-community decisions become independent/order-insensitive.

## Demote

### C002 — Flagship founder trap

Move from 'surviving connection' to known/derivative applied hypothesis because spinout and movement-organization literature already occupies too much of the mechanism's conceptual neighborhood.

## Next frontier

Attack C003 and C004 rather than elaborate them rhetorically.

For C003:

- search property-rights/religious-economics literature specifically for evidence that asset portability changes schism/innovation rates;
- distinguish ordinary exit rights from **branch inheritance**;
- model the tradeoff between institutional search and strategic asset stripping;
- ask whether a minority-fork escrow can be incentive-compatible.

For C004:

- translate real community decisions into a state-transition model;
- identify which common decision classes commute and which do not;
- check whether Ostrom/polycentric/interdependence theory already contains an equivalent criterion under different vocabulary;
- derive a measurable 'non-commutativity density' rather than leaving the idea metaphorical.

If either collapses under that attack, demote it immediately.
