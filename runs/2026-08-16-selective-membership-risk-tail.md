# Selective-membership risk tail — provisional checkpoint

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: retrieval-free candidate generation; **not novelty-promoted**

## User correction that kills the previous branch

The prior minority-relationship line drifted toward designing an all-inclusive social system. An intentional community has a different objective: preserve a high-safety, high-trust residential environment through selective membership. It does not have an obligation to retain a person whose generalized risk exceeds the community's acceptable threshold.

Restorative justice is useful when it can materially reduce risk on a reasonable timescale, but it is not the default answer to every dangerous member. Rehabilitation/support can be separated from continued residential membership.

The user's online Signal therapy-circle rule is an informative contrast: an honesty problem does not itself require removal if the member acknowledges it openly; concealment/denial is more concerning. A commune raises the stakes because exposure is continuous, multiplex, and harder to bound.

## Candidate consequences generated before external retrieval

### P1 — Lifetime containment reliability

For a persistent high-consequence hazard, a control is inadequate if safety depends on near-perfect human vigilance across thousands of future interactions. Evaluate cumulative containment reliability over the expected duration of membership, not whether the restriction works at one moment. This can make ejection the least-risk robust intervention even where narrower controls are theoretically imaginable.

### P2 — Containment-burden threshold

Membership suitability should consider the ongoing surveillance, checking, coordination, and emotional labor required to keep risk acceptable. If safe inclusion requires permanent extraordinary vigilance from others, the containment burden itself can make the arrangement incompatible with ordinary communal life.

### P3 — Transparency-tax / adverse-selection risk

If admitting a manageable flaw causes a durable global stigma, honest imperfect members may self-select out while skilled concealers remain. A community can therefore undermine its own screening by making candid disclosure too socially costly. The target is strong accountability without rewarding impression management.

### P4 — Verifiability is stronger than confession

Admission of a flaw is not itself evidence of low risk. More diagnostic is whether the member accepts proportionate constraints, checking, and observable tests that reduce the ability to exploit the relevant weakness over time.

### P5 — Informal exit is part of the safety-selection process

People under scrutiny may leave before formal ejection because they do not want to remain known for the discovered flaw. Formal expulsion counts therefore understate the community's actual filtering activity. Research should separately record scrutiny-triggered voluntary exits and unresolved exits during a safety process.

### P6 — Exit-before-ejection can erase unresolved risk

Within a federation, voluntary departure before a final safety decision can allow unresolved risk to disappear from formal records and reappear as a clean application elsewhere. Right-to-exit must not imply erasure of properly scoped, evidence-provenanced unresolved safety information. This likely collides with the existing portable-risk-information lesson and should be treated as a narrowing candidate, not a new root claim.

### P7 — Risk memory should track mechanism persistence

Past problems should not all receive the same reputational half-life. Where the causal risk mechanism is plausibly transient and behavior changes under observation, trust can recover. Where the mechanism is persistent or the harm is catastrophic and difficult to contain, access/membership restrictions may rationally remain much longer. Forgiveness, relationship repair, access restoration, and residential membership are distinct decisions.

## Current best tail candidates

The strongest non-obvious candidates are P1 (lifetime containment reliability), P2 (containment burden), and P3 (transparency-tax adverse selection). P5 is also a promising measurement consequence. P6 is probably a residual under the project's existing portable-risk-information architecture.
