# Embedded seed experiments tail

Date: 2026-08-16
Status: practical architecture + novelty screening; no new strict finding promoted

## User architecture

Do not assume institutional experimentation requires members to leave or a faction to split off. Where compatible, allow small seed experiments inside the parent community or immediately adjacent to it while continuing to share selected resources, relationships, and infrastructure. A full daughter-community/resource transfer should be a later graduation option after the experiment demonstrates enough value and competence to justify larger irreversible support.

User analogy: do not hand a child $10,000 to buy a dinosaur merely because they propose it; show the dinosaur and show that it can be ridden safely before considering the larger commitment.

## Immediate disposition

The root architecture is practical and strong but not a strict novelty candidate. It compresses to familiar pilot/incubator/staged-commitment logic and has close analogues in corporate internal ventures/spin-offs and polycentric experimentation. Hutterite daughter-colony preparation also demonstrates that physical daughter-site development can precede the final split, although it does not establish the user's proposed institutional-variant incubation model.

## Hard-vetoed consequences

- Pilot before full resource transfer — common sense.
- Share parent resources during early experimentation — established incubator/internal-venture logic.
- Use reversible or staged commitments — ordinary real-options/pilot logic.
- Audit hidden subsidy before claiming autonomous viability — common sense/accounting.
- Let successful practices diffuse back into the parent — standard polycentric/organizational-learning logic.
- Gradually increase resources with evidence — standard staged venture funding.
- Use overlapping subgroups to permit experimentation — close to polycentric governance.

## Narrow issues worth retaining as practical design constraints, not novelty claims

### 1. Proofability bias

`Prove it first` preferentially favors variants that can be demonstrated cheaply inside the incumbent ecology. Some institutional packages may need complementary changes, scale, land, or autonomy before their value is visible. The project already has C003 asset-gated cultural mutation, and innovation/policy literature already recognizes incumbent constraints and complementarities. Treat this as a C003-adjacent implementation issue, not a new finding.

### 2. Graduation hold-up

If seed members invest substantial labor only to have the parent decide ex post whether successful proof earns land/resources/autonomy, the parent can become a post-proof bottleneck. Where stakes justify it, predefine what specified evidence or milestones entitle the seed to request or trigger, while retaining safety/fiduciary escape clauses. This has close source-domain analogues in incomplete-contracting and innovation incentive theory and is not yet promoted as a novel community transfer.

### 3. Parent-seed interface as proto-federation practice

An adjacent, partially autonomous seed can reveal how two governance regimes actually share infrastructure, people, records, childcare, land, conflict resolution, and resources before full separation. This may make incubation useful not only for testing the seed but for testing future federation interfaces. Close analogues exist in parent-spin-off and polycentric-governance literature; no originality claim.

### 4. Graduation is optional

A successful institutional experiment need not become a separate commune. It may remain a nested unit, be absorbed by the parent, diffuse as a module, or separate only when incompatibility, scale, or interface costs make autonomy useful. Therefore fission should be one outcome of experimentation, not its default objective.
