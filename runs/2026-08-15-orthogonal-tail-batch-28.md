# Orthogonal Tail Batch 28 — Capacity Revelation, Labor Ratchets, and Exceptional Effort

Date: 2026-08-15

## Outcome

**No new Creative Tail survivor.**

The candidate family is important for commune design, but the root mechanism is the classic dynamic **ratchet effect**: if demonstrated capacity or productivity causes future targets/obligations to rise, agents have reason to conceal capacity or restrict performance. The target domain also contains close protective precedents: Twin Oaks and Dandelion use broadly common labor quotas and allow over-quota work to become vacation/credit rather than automatically resetting an individual member's permanent labor baseline.

The practical lesson is strong enough to preserve in the communities repo, but not novel enough to promote here.

---

# A. Strongest practical result — ratchet-safe capacity revelation

## Plain proposition

If exceptional effort today is interpreted as proof of permanently higher capacity tomorrow, members can rationally avoid revealing spare capacity, exceptional competence, or efficiency improvements.

Examples:

- a member covers 60 hours during a crisis and is thereafter treated as someone who can normally carry 60 hours;
- an efficient worker automates half a task and is simply assigned more work until their free time disappears;
- a community copes for several months with reduced federation support and the temporary austerity becomes evidence that the lower support level is permanently adequate;
- a highly competent member repeatedly rescues a work area and becomes the default person for every future failure;
- a daughter community reveals a large reserve and its federation contribution is permanently raised from a one-time surplus.

The long-run consequence is that **capacity revelation itself becomes costly**.

## Source-domain collision

This is the classic ratchet problem in dynamic principal-agent and planned-economy systems.

- Keren, Miller & Thornton (1983), *The Ratchet: A dynamic managerial incentive model of the soviet enterprise*, explicitly models how future plan targets based on revealed output can make managers avoid full-capacity production.
- Meyer & Vickers (1997), *Performance Comparisons and Dynamic Incentives*, discusses ratchet effects and other implicit dynamic incentives.
- More recent dynamic-screening theory continues to model the loss of information revelation when a principal cannot credibly commit not to revise future terms after learning agent capacity.

Therefore the underlying incentive mechanism is not novel.

## Direct communal precedent

Twin Oaks' published labor system provides a close anti-ratchet architecture:

- quota is a community labor requirement rather than an individualized productivity target;
- one hour generally equals one credit regardless of task efficiency;
- over-quota work accumulates in a member's vacation balance;
- community-wide quota is adjusted through economic planning rather than automatically raising one member's quota because that member performed more;
- age and health-related quota reductions are separately recognized.

Dandelion's published labor-credit system likewise lets overtime create additional vacation and bounded rewards.

These systems do not prove that no informal competence tax or managerial dependency exists, but they show that **ratchet-resistant accounting is already intentional-community practice**.

## Practical design rule

> **Do not let temporary excess contribution, emergency sacrifice, or revealed competence silently reset a member's permanent baseline obligation.**

Possible controls:

1. label emergency/temporary surge work explicitly;
2. convert voluntary excess labor into vacation, credit, reciprocal support, or another bounded return rather than future baseline escalation;
3. require separate evidence/process before changing a person's ordinary quota or support entitlement;
4. distinguish a one-time efficiency gain from durable spare capacity;
5. after a crisis, explicitly restore ordinary expectations unless a new baseline is deliberately agreed;
6. train substitutes so competence does not become a permanent assignment trap;
7. for federation support/contributions, use long-horizon capacity measures rather than one unusually good/bad period.

## Important distinction

This is not a rule against changing quotas. A community whose material conditions change may need different common labor expectations. The ratchet problem concerns **endogenous concealment incentives** created when revealing high capacity causes unilateral future appropriation of that capacity.

# B. Candidate audit

At least fifteen plain candidates were generated.

## T128-01 — Individual productivity quota ratchet

If higher demonstrated output raises future personal quota, members restrict output.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL.** Direct ratchet-effect literature.

## T128-02 — Emergency-heroics ratchet

One crisis period of extreme effort becomes the next period's normal expectation.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.** Classic temporary-to-baseline ratchet / workload normalization.

## T128-03 — Efficiency appropriation

If every automation/time saving is immediately filled with additional assigned work, members lose private/community-local incentive to improve efficiency or disclose savings.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.** Ratchet/incentive mechanism.

## T128-04 — Low-need ratchet

A member/community temporarily coping with less support is treated as evidence that the lower support level is permanently sufficient, encouraging actors not to reveal resilience.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.** Dynamic benefit/need revelation and ratchet logic.

## T128-05 — Reserve-revelation federation tax

A community that reveals unusually high reserves faces permanently larger transfer/contribution demands and therefore has incentive to hide or dissipate reserves.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL.** Fiscal transfer / soft-information ratchet logic.

## T128-06 — Competence tax

The best person at a task receives increasing assignments until competence creates a net burden.

Verdict: **REJECT AS OBVIOUS / KNOWN.**

## T128-07 — Rescue skill trap

A skilled member spends so much time rescuing work areas that they cannot train replacements, keeping themselves indispensable.

Nearest neighbors: key-person risk, capability traps, delegation/training bottlenecks.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.**

## T128-08 — Over-quota banking as anti-ratchet mechanism

Extra labor produces personal vacation/credit rather than a permanent quota increase.

Verdict: **TARGET PRECEDENT, NOT NOVEL.** Twin Oaks/Dandelion already implement close versions.

## T128-09 — Nonprecedential exceptional contribution tag

Explicitly mark a surge contribution as temporary/nonprecedential so it cannot update baseline automatically.

Nearest neighbors: temporary assignments, nonprecedential settlements, ratchet-safe contracting.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.**

## T128-10 — Baseline-change cooldown

Do not revise normal workload/support immediately after exceptional performance; require repeated evidence across ordinary conditions.

Nearest neighbors: smoothing, performance evaluation, dynamic contracting.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.**

## T128-11 — Capacity-revelation safe harbor

A member may disclose spare capacity or efficiency improvement without that disclosure alone authorizing a permanent obligation increase.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL formulation of ratchet protection.**

## T128-12 — Automation dividend

Share some benefit of labor-saving innovation as lower labor, project choice or leisure rather than appropriating all savings as more quota.

Nearest neighbors: gainsharing, productivity sharing, labor-saving incentive design.

Verdict: **REJECT.**

## T128-13 — Need-recovery protection

Temporary improvement in health/function should not automatically erase accommodations before durability is established.

Nearest neighbors: disability/benefit policy, rehabilitation and return-to-work design.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; may be important rights practice.**

## T128-14 — Federation contribution smoothing

Use multi-period capacity rather than one-year surplus to update federation dues/resource obligations.

Nearest neighbors: tax/base smoothing, insurance experience periods, fiscal equalization.

Verdict: **REJECT.**

## T128-15 — Skill-replacement budget

When one member becomes a persistent bottleneck, explicitly allocate labor to training substitutes even if short-run output falls.

Nearest neighbors: succession/cross-training/key-person resilience.

Verdict: **REJECT AS NOVEL; PRACTICAL.**

# C. Practical lessons to mirror into communities

1. exceptional effort should not silently become the next normal labor baseline;
2. voluntary over-quota work can be returned as vacation/credit rather than appropriated as proof of higher permanent capacity;
3. label emergency surge obligations as temporary and reset them explicitly after the crisis;
4. separate durable capacity changes from one-off performance peaks;
5. give members some retained benefit from efficiency/automation so capacity improvements are worth revealing;
6. do not make competence a permanent workload tax; budget replacement training for chronic bottleneck roles;
7. temporary low support/low need should not automatically redefine future entitlement without durability review;
8. use multi-period measures rather than one exceptional period when updating federation dues/support expectations;
9. audit informal ratchets even when formal quotas are flat: the same capable person can become socially expected to rescue everything;
10. Twin Oaks/Dandelion-style over-quota banking is an existing communal anti-ratchet pattern worth comparing with other labor systems.

## Method lesson

A strong cross-domain mechanism can be practically important and still fail the Creative Tail novelty test when both its theory and a close intentional-community counterdesign already exist. In that case the correct output is a **status-quo improvement lesson**, not a discovery.
