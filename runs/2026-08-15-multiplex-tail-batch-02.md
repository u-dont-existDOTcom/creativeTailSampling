# Tail Batch 02 — Deeper Consequences of F004 + F005

Date: 2026-08-15
Seeds:

- F004 — failure-correlation inversion
- F005 — relational capability atrophy loop

Goal: move beyond generic claims about social support and find structural consequences of changing the *unit* in which relationships carry functions.

## T15 — Combinatorial uniqueness

**Plain proposition:** A person can be difficult to replace in a multiplex relationship even when none of their individual traits or functions is rare, because the joint bundle is rare; unbundling allows replacement component by component and destroys this combinatorial uniqueness.

**Familiarity adversary:** "People are unique" is vague and useless. "Relationship-specific capital" covers part of persistence but does not capture the replacement arithmetic. The distinctive claim is that bundling changes replacement from a joint-match problem into several independent match problems.

### Derivation

Suppose person A supplies functions f1...fk to person B. If B needs one replacement who jointly matches enough of those functions, the candidate pool is filtered by the intersection of requirements. Even common traits can form a rare conjunction.

If the functions are unbundled, B can replace f1 with person X, f2 with Y, f3 with Z, etc. The joint rarity disappears because no replacement must satisfy the conjunction.

### Three-step consequence

1. Multiplexity creates personal irreplaceability from combinations of otherwise ordinary capacities.
2. Component-wise unbundling lowers replacement costs without anyone becoming intrinsically less valuable.
3. Lower replacement costs reduce incentives to tolerate imperfections or invest in relationship-specific adaptation.
4. Reduced investment makes ties thinner and still easier to replace, producing another positive feedback toward unbundling.

### Discriminating prediction

Controlling for duration, affection, and interaction frequency, ties spanning more functionally independent domains should exhibit lower substitution/churn after a local disappointment than equally strong uniplex ties, especially where the domains require accumulated shared context rather than generic service provision.

**Verdict:** SURVIVED. Strong.

## T16 — Multiplexity hysteresis / bistability

**Plain proposition:** Because exercising functions makes relationships more capable of carrying future functions, while outsourcing prevents that capability from accumulating, relational systems may have high-multiplexity and low-multiplexity stable states rather than one smooth continuum.

**Familiarity adversary:** Generic path dependence is familiar. The candidate survives only if it generates a specific threshold prediction that ordinary "social capital matters" accounts do not.

### Three-step consequence

1. Existing role overlap lowers the cost/risk of adding another role because trust, context, reciprocal history, and communication channels already exist.
2. Additional role overlap then creates still more context and obligation, lowering the marginal cost of further role accretion.
3. Conversely, outsourcing leaves ties incapable, raising the apparent cost of bringing functions back into relationships.
4. The feedback can produce two basins: above some role-overlap threshold, multiplexity self-reinforces; below it, specialization self-reinforces.

### Discriminating prediction

Small increases in sociability may have almost no effect below the threshold. Coordinated role-sharing interventions can produce nonlinear gains and those gains may persist after the intervention ends. Conversely, removing a few key shared functions can trigger disproportionately large later unbundling.

**Verdict:** SURVIVED, but as a development of F005 rather than an independent root finding.

## T17 — Economies of scope in relationships versus economies of scale in providers

**Plain proposition:** Specialist institutions gain efficiency by repeating one function across many people, while multiplex relationships gain efficiency by reusing one trust/context channel across many functions.

**Familiarity adversary:** Compresses directly to economies of scale versus economies of scope, both named concepts.

**Useful consequence:** A system can appear efficient when measuring the specialist's production cost while ignoring repeated search, onboarding, verification, scheduling, explanation, and coordination costs paid by the user across many specialist interfaces.

**Verdict:** DEMOTED as a useful explanatory frame, not a creative-tail discovery.

---

## T18 — Bundle-positive generalists can be selected out by component-wise optimization

**Plain proposition:** A person who is moderately valuable across many domains can be highly valuable as one relationship bundle but lose every role-specific competition to different specialists.

**Familiarity adversary:** Close to specialization/comparative advantage, which is already below the novelty boundary. The selection implication is sharper but risks rediscovering the division of labor.

**Consequence:** Unbundling can systematically under-reward broad cooperative competence that only has high value when evaluated jointly.

**Verdict:** PROMISING but not promoted.

---

## T19 — Standardization creates social replaceability

**Plain proposition:** A role becomes easier to substitute when its requirements are standardized enough that providers can be compared on the same dimensions.

**Familiarity adversary:** Compresses to commodification / labor fungibility / standardization.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as familiar. T15 contains the less familiar structural result.

---

## T20 — Residual attachment concentration

**Plain proposition:** If most ordinary ties become component-wise replaceable while a few intimate ties retain many bundled functions, irreplaceability becomes concentrated in those few ties.

**Familiarity adversary:** "People depend too much on romantic partners" is familiar. The combinatorial-replacement mechanism is less so.

**Derived consequence:** Unbundling can simultaneously make most people feel socially replaceable and make loss of the residual multiplexer unusually catastrophic.

**Verdict:** PROMISING as a consequence of T15 + F001.

---

## T21 — Synthetic comorbidity from access-layer failure

**Plain proposition:** One failure in a common access layer can generate several downstream life problems that specialist systems then record as distinct co-occurring deficits.

Example structural chain: loss of income/access -> transport failure + childcare failure + housing stress + reduced social participation + missed healthcare -> multiple separate case categories.

**Familiarity adversary:** Social determinants of health and cascading poverty effects are well known. The diagnostic-fragmentation angle is useful but not sufficiently novel.

**Verdict:** PROMISING consequence of F004; not promoted.

---

## T22 — Specialist repair can recursively deepen the original coupling

**Plain proposition:** If common-access failure causes multi-domain problems and each problem is treated by another specialist service requiring the same access infrastructure, the repair architecture can increase dependence on the layer whose failure created the cascade.

**Familiarity adversary:** Resembles institutional dependency / poverty trap. The recursive topology is interesting but still close to known traps.

**Verdict:** PROMISING. Needs empirical examples or another non-obvious implication.

---

## T23 — Multiplexity can be dangerous when exit and support are bundled

**Plain proposition:** The same role bundling that creates resilience can make an abusive or destructive relationship expensive to leave because exit removes many functions simultaneously.

**Familiarity adversary:** This is a familiar dependence/abuse mechanism.

**Design implication:** The target cannot be maximum multiplexity. Healthy systems need *redundant multiplexity*: several overlapping capable ties so that leaving one relationship does not mean losing every function it carried.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novel finding, retained as a critical constraint on F001.

---

## T24 — Privacy and experimentation trade against multiplex context

**Plain proposition:** Unbundled relationships permit people to keep domains separate, experiment with identities, and conceal information that a multiplex network would integrate.

**Familiarity adversary:** Compresses to privacy, anonymity, role separation, and urban freedom.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novel; retained as counterweight to claims that multiplexity is uniformly beneficial.

---

## T25 — Network reconstruction cost after loss is super-additive

**Plain proposition:** Losing one multiplex tie can require establishing several new specialist relationships, each with its own search, verification, onboarding, scheduling, and trust cost.

**Familiarity adversary:** This is largely a direct transaction-cost consequence of multiplexity.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as too close to obvious economies of scope.

---

## T26 — More social ties can coexist with less relational capacity

**Plain proposition:** Unbundling can increase the number of distinct people a person interacts with while reducing the number of functions any one relationship can carry.

**Familiarity adversary:** This is a useful measurement warning but follows directly from multiplex network structure.

**Verdict:** MERGED with F006.

# Batch selection

## Strongest new survivor: T15 — combinatorial uniqueness

The important move is not simply that long relationships contain shared history. **Bundling changes the mathematics of replacement.** A joint bundle can be rare even when every component is common. Decomposition turns a hard joint-match problem into several easier component-match problems.

This produces a plausible feedback:

> unbundle functions -> lower replacement cost -> lower incentive for relationship-specific investment/tolerance -> thinner ties -> still lower replacement cost -> further unbundling

This is distinct from the F005 capability loop, though the two reinforce each other.

## Second survivor: T16 — hysteresis

F005 should be upgraded from a simple atrophy mechanism to a possible two-state dynamic. If role overlap lowers the cost of adding more roles, and absence of overlap raises it, communities may not be restorable by marginal increases in sociability. Crossing a functional role-sharing threshold may be necessary.

## Combined structural picture after Batch 02

At least three reinforcing mechanisms now point in the same direction:

1. **Capability feedback (F005):** outsourcing stops ties learning how to carry functions.
2. **Replacement feedback (T15):** unbundling makes people easier to replace component-wise, reducing investment that would thicken ties.
3. **Access-layer coupling (F004):** specialist routing can couple many functions to common system-access variables.

This suggests relational unbundling can be a self-stabilizing regime, not merely the passive result of preferences for independence.
