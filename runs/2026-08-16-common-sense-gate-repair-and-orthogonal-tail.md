## Orthogonal candidate O1 — anti-faction stability may trade off against communal reproduction

Plain proposition:

> A community that optimizes too strongly against cohesive subgroups/factions may become harder to split politically but also harder to reproduce by planned fission, because a viable daughter community requires a subgroup that is already cohesive and functionally complete enough to leave together.

Why it survived the first obviousness veto:
- it is not merely `factions cause splits`;
- it predicts a design tradeoff between parent-level stability and movement-level reproduction;
- the same latent subgroup structure can be treated as pathology under a stability objective and as reproductive infrastructure under a replication objective;
- it suggests that a movement optimized for durable parent communities can be reproductively sterile even when its institutional template is otherwise copyable.

Internal-corpus check:
- the communities synthesis already treats planned fission as a continuity mechanism and Hutterite fission as a major reproduction case;
- C005 measures reproductive distribution and `make reproduction boring`;
- current canonical material does not explicitly state the anti-faction-stability versus daughter-readiness tradeoff.

External nearest-neighbor attack (ordinary web retrieval, not yet Exa/Parallel benchmark):
- cooperative-game core theory formalizes coalition stability in terms of whether subcoalitions can benefit by leaving;
- social-fission literature models subgroup coalescence/cohesion as a precursor to permanent fission;
- Hutterite literature treats periodic colony fission as orderly group reproduction;
- multilevel-selection/community-fragmentation literature treats fission mode and retention of cooperative/functional packages as determinants of daughter success.

These are strong roots/adjacencies. The exact intentional-community design claim remains provisional and should be labelled CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION at most unless a more direct target-domain precedent is found.

Potential discriminating prediction:
- among communities of similar size/age, those whose social/work networks contain multiple internally cohesive, functionally complete but cross-linked subgroups should have higher probability of successful planned daughter formation than equally cohesive communities whose ties are maximally homogenized across the whole parent group;
- excessive subgroup isolation should instead predict hostile schism, giving a possible interior optimum rather than `more faction` or `less faction`.
