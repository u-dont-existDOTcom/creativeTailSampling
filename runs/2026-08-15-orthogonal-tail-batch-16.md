# C. Orthogonal candidate batch

## T1601 — Cohort-composition admission

**Source domain:** combinatorial cohort selection / matching with peer effects.

**Plain proposition:** When the value of admitting person A depends materially on which other applicants are admitted, evaluating applicants one by one against the current membership can produce systematically bad selections even if every individual decision is locally reasonable.

### Why this is not ordinary 'screen better'

Ordinary screening assumes approximate separability: score candidate A, score B, choose good individuals.

Here utility is non-separable:

`U({A,B}) != U(A) + U(B)`.

Examples include:

- two applicants whose skills make each other viable;
- several newcomers who together restore a missing age/cohort/social niche;
- two individually high-fit people whose interaction creates conflict or faction risk;
- a candidate who is isolated and likely to leave if admitted alone but stable if admitted with a compatible peer;
- applicants whose joint arrival alters childcare, work, language, romantic, or governance dynamics.

### Three-step consequence

1. Sequential admission compares each applicant to the *current* community.
2. But the relevant future state is the community **after several admissions**.
3. Therefore a candidate rejected now may belong to the best reachable future configuration, while a candidate accepted now can remove that configuration from the feasible set.
4. Early admissions can thus create path dependence even when nobody makes an obvious mistake.

### Distinctive operational implication

When several openings exist, periodically evaluate candidate **sets/cohorts** rather than only candidate-by-candidate fit.

Possible implementation:

- retain a provisional pool rather than immediately finalizing each acceptable applicant;
- model important pairwise or higher-order complementarities/conflicts;
- run joint trial periods where feasible;
- compare several plausible future membership compositions;
- preserve easy exit and individual rights rather than forcing applicants into bundles.

### Target-domain screen

Current intentional-community guidance found in this pass is predominantly sequential: application -> visit/trial -> provisional membership -> individual/community decision. Some forming cohousing projects explicitly form affinity groups from multiple prospective members, but this is mostly community formation rather than established-community admissions.

Matching/cohort-selection literature explicitly treats peer effects and combinatorial cohort utility as a distinct problem.

### Prediction

For communities admitting multiple people over a short period, cohort-aware selection should outperform sequential individual screening when interaction effects among newcomers are large. The advantage should vanish when applicant utilities are approximately additive.

### Verdict

**SURVIVES provisionally as a cross-domain operational transfer.** Promote as C009 unless a closer intentional-community precedent is found.

## T1602 — Early-offer preference discovery

**Source:** matching-market unraveling / dynamic multi-offer admissions.

Applicants often do not know which community they actually prefer until they spend meaningful time inside several communities. Early offers can therefore be accepted because they arrive first, not because they are best fits.

Potential design: federation-wide visiting windows with delayed commitment and multiple provisional offers.

**Reject/demote:** interesting but too dependent on the existence of a competitive multi-community applicant market; current evidence does not show this is a major bottleneck.

## T1603 — Anti-chain admission dependency

If admission of A makes B admissible, and B makes C admissible, sequential rules can produce hidden dependency chains where early decisions determine later feasible membership states.

**Merge into T1601.** This is a consequence of non-separable cohort utility.

## T1604 — Irreversible membership option value

When full membership grants hard-to-reverse property/governance rights, the optimal threshold should depend on the value of waiting for more information, not only present expected fit.

**Reject:** standard real-options / irreversible-decision logic.

## T1605 — Information hazard partitioning

Certain sensitive member information should be revealed only to decision-makers who need it, because community-wide disclosure can cause irreversible reputation effects even after allegations are cleared.

**Reject:** least-privilege / safeguarding / privacy architecture already in rejection frontier.

## T1606 — Correlated private signals in consensus

Ten members saying 'I independently noticed the same concern' may contain far less than ten signals if they all learned it from one source.

**Reject:** correlated-information/common-source problem is familiar.

## T1607 — Disturbance-window membership reset

Major relocation/fission may create a temporary period in which norms are unusually plastic; deliberate changes introduced then could persist more strongly than the same changes introduced during stable periods.

**Reject:** ecological priority effects + founder/imprinting/lifecycle effects are too close to existing boundary.

## T1608 — Deferred constitutional choice

A new community may improve institutional search by delaying some rules until real conflicts reveal which dimensions matter.

**Reject:** adaptive governance / option value / learning-by-doing; too familiar.

## T1609 — Mutation budget

A federation could deliberately allocate a fraction of daughter communities to high-variance institutional experiments while keeping most daughters conservative.

**Reject:** exploration/exploitation and policy-laboratory logic already established.

## T1610 — Hidden-information role auction

Instead of assigning scarce unpleasant work by volunteers or rotation, elicit willingness privately to avoid signaling/status distortions.

**Reject:** mechanism-design application is straightforward and target value uncertain.

## T1611 — Compatibility shadow prices

Some members consume scarce compatibility capacity (e.g., only one of several mutually incompatible lifestyles can be supported). Admission decisions should reflect opportunity cost of using that slot.

**Reject:** ordinary constrained matching/resource allocation once stated plainly.

## T1612 — Reproductive insurance pool

Federations could pool resources to subsidize daughters from parent communities that suffer unusually costly fissions.

**Reject:** insurance/risk-pooling and founder-export logic; not novel enough.

## T1613 — Failure-history inheritance

Daughter communities should inherit not just current rules but rejected alternatives and reasons they failed.

**Reject as novelty:** organizational-learning literature explicitly studies negative knowledge ('knowing what not to do') and routine replication. Potentially useful practice, but not a tail finding.

## T1614 — Scaling-replication attention tradeoff

Rapidly creating daughters can reduce a federation's ability to maintain fidelity in existing communities because transfer and retention compete for attention.

**Reject:** Organization Science 2026 directly studies spatial vs temporal routine replication and finds this attention tradeoff empirically in scaling organizations.
