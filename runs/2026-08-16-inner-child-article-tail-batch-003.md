## Strongest practical residuals

### B003-P1 — Function × context map

Plain proposition:

> Adult capacity is not one scalar. Map Nurturer, Protector, and Guide separately across the contexts in which they are or are not accessible.

Examples of contexts worth distinguishing:

- calm/alone;
- conflict;
- shame after making a mistake;
- attachment panic;
- exhaustion;
- receiving care;
- setting a boundary;
- being asked to disappoint somebody;
- after a missed self-care promise.

#### Article collision

Partial only. The article already treats the three adult functions separately and asks readers to test small amounts in ordinary life, but does not make `function × context` the unit of assessment.

#### Why it matters

A global label such as `I have an inner adult` can hide highly asymmetric capacities. A parentified reader might show excellent Protector/Guide access while caring for others and almost no Nurturer access when ashamed or receiving help. Another reader may self-soothe well but lose all Protector capacity in relationships.

#### Consequences

1. Intervention choice can target the missing cell rather than strengthening everything indiscriminately.
2. Apparent `regression` may be a context-transfer failure, not loss of all adult capacity.
3. Internalization can be tested more precisely: the function should become portable across increasingly difficult contexts rather than merely available in the practice setting.

#### Discriminating test

Have the reader rate or behaviorally test each function across several recurring contexts. A strongly uneven matrix predicts different treatment needs than a unitary adult-capacity model.

#### Disposition

High-value practical diagnostic; strict novelty rejected because context specificity/generalization is established.

### B003-P2 — Prediction-based trust building

Plain proposition:

> Instead of asking a distrustful child/protector to believe the adult, ask what it predicts the adult will do and create a small observable test.

Possible sequence:

1. `What do you think I will actually do when this happens?`
2. Record the prediction without arguing.
3. Pick one small adult act relevant to that prediction.
4. Do it.
5. Compare prediction and outcome later.
6. If the protector was right, update the adult plan rather than redefining the prediction.

#### Article collision

The article already treats `what are you gonna do for me?` as a literal question and recommends visible action. The additive piece is to make the distrust **prospective and testable** rather than retrospective only.

#### Supplemental prior art

Expectation-violation / behavioral-experiment models are established in psychotherapy. Therefore this is not a new mechanism. The article fit is the useful part.

#### Consequences

1. Trust-building becomes falsifiable rather than reassurance-based.
2. The protector can remain skeptical without being treated as resistant.
3. Repeated accurate child/protector predictions become evidence that the adult needs to change behavior rather than demand more trust.

#### Discriminating test

Compare generic reassurance/commitment with explicit prediction → observable action → review on perceived trust and future prediction accuracy.

#### Disposition

High-value practical operationalization; non-novel.

### B003-P3 — Do not make pain the admission ticket to care

Plain proposition:

> The inner adult should have an ordinary relationship with the child during good and neutral periods, not appear only when something hurts.

This does **not** mean withdrawing attention during distress. The opposite: keep responsive care during distress while adding proactive, non-crisis contact.

Possible forms:

- play;
- music;
- drawing;
- curiosity;
- celebration;
- humor;
- a walk;
- trying something badly on purpose;
- letting the child choose something harmless;
- noticing beauty together;
- simple companionship with no trauma-processing agenda.

#### Article collision

The article already calls the method `pl/ork`, explicitly values play, describes healed-child qualities, and encourages experimentation. Yet most concrete relational practices are still wound-activated: fear, distrust, protector work, vows, missing love, trauma material.

The residual is therefore not `children need play`; it is **relationship scheduling independent of crisis**.

#### Supplemental prior art

Positive/child-directed parent-child interaction is established; PCIT and attachment interventions strongly emphasize positive interaction, sensitivity, play, and secure-base/exploration functions. Behavior-analysis literature also shows that attention contingencies can shape behavior, although that literature should not be naively mapped onto inner distress.

#### Consequences

1. The child-adult relationship can acquire positive shared history instead of being built mostly around repair.
2. Nurturer credibility may become less dependent on the child reaching crisis intensity before receiving attention.
3. The Guide gains access to curiosity/play as developmental engines rather than operating mainly through correction or difficult growth.

#### Caution

Do not frame distress as manipulative or punish it with reduced care. The intervention is **additional proactive connection**, never strategic neglect.

#### Discriminating test

Compare a period of distress-only inner-child contact with a period that preserves the same distress responsiveness plus scheduled ordinary positive contact. Track felt trust, spontaneous child access, crisis intensity/frequency, play/curiosity, and avoidance.

#### Disposition

High-value article improvement; established roots, not strict novelty.

### B003-P4 — Explicitly renegotiate promises rather than silently moving the goalposts

Plain proposition:

> If an inner-adult commitment was unrealistic, retire it openly and replace it with a more realistic one rather than quietly behaving as though the original vow was never made.

#### Relationship to Batch 002

This is a refinement of broken-promise repair and no-arrears:

- repair acknowledges the breach;
- no-arrears prevents punitive backlog;
- explicit renegotiation prevents silent goalpost movement.

#### Consequences

1. Reduced promise size need not be experienced as another disappearance if the change itself is acknowledged.
2. The child/protector can distinguish `I changed the contract because reality taught me something` from `my words mean nothing`.
3. Outcome monitoring becomes cleaner because old and new commitments are not conflated.

#### Disposition

Useful practical refinement. Possible cross-domain configuration/versioning metaphor is not enough for originality; mandatory retrieval would be required even to consider a narrow connection claim.

### B003-P5 — Re-borrowing after stress can be maintenance, not failure

Plain proposition:

> A genuinely internalized adult capacity can become temporarily hard to access under unusually high stress; borrowing support again can restore access without implying the function was never internalized.

#### Owner-constraint compatibility

This does **not** remove internalization as the developmental goal. It distinguishes acquisition from stress-related access failure.

#### Why it matters

Otherwise the developmental sequence can be read as strictly one-way: needing help again = back to childhood / failed adulthood. That risks shame precisely when the system is overloaded.

#### Disposition

Useful clarification; non-novel skill-maintenance principle.
