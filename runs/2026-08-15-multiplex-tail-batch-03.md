# Tail Batch 03 — What Keeps a Low-Multiplexity Regime Stable?

Date: 2026-08-15
Seed: F009 — relational unbundling may be a self-stabilizing regime.

Goal: identify mechanisms that explain why people can sincerely want more community yet keep making individually reasonable choices that prevent multiplex relationships from forming.

## T27 — Local efficiency can consume relational option value

**Plain proposition:** Choosing the most efficient specialist for each task can be individually rational in the short run while destroying the future option for an ordinary relationship to perform that task or adjacent tasks.

**Familiarity adversary:** "Convenience weakens community" is too vague. "Learning by doing" is familiar. The distinctive claim is that a relationship has *option value* that is created by imperfect real use, so optimizing each transaction independently can lower total long-run capability.

### Derivation

1. A specialist is usually better at a narrow function today.
2. Giving the task to the specialist avoids the inconvenience/risk of letting a friend, neighbor, relative, or community member perform it.
3. The ordinary tie therefore gains no domain-specific practice, trust, routines, or reciprocal history.
4. The next time the need arises, the specialist advantage is larger because the ordinary tie is still unpracticed.
5. Repeating local optimization can therefore make the long-run relational alternative disappear.

### Consequence

Building multiplexity may require tolerating bounded, short-run inefficiency: some tasks must be performed relationally before relational provision is the best immediate option.

### Discriminating prediction

Two groups with equal social contact should diverge in later mutual-aid capacity if one group repeatedly routes low-stakes useful tasks through members while the other purchases superior specialist provision. The role-sharing group should become increasingly capable over time even if early task performance is worse.

**Verdict:** SURVIVED. Strong.

## T28 — Market crowd-out can operate through lost practice, not only lost time

**Plain proposition:** A paid service can crowd out informal support even when it saves people time, because it removes the interaction in which relational capability would have been learned.

**Familiarity adversary:** Crowding out informal care is known. The *practice-removal* mechanism is a more precise consequence of T27 rather than a separate root discovery.

**Verdict:** SURVIVED AS CONSEQUENCE of T27.

---

## T29 — Community outcomes may conflict with modern boundary norms

**Plain proposition:** People can prefer the outcomes of multiplex community while also preferring privacy, non-interference, professionalism, and strict role boundaries that prevent the behaviors required to create it.

**Familiarity adversary:** "You can't have your cake and eat it" is generic. The candidate matters only if the specific conflict is structural rather than moralistic.

### Derivation

1. Multiplex ties require people to cross domains: practical help, childcare, conflict involvement, emotional support, material reciprocity, advice, emergency intervention, etc.
2. Low-multiplexity norms often classify unsolicited cross-domain involvement as nosiness, burdening, amateurism, boundary violation, or inappropriate role mixing.
3. People can therefore sincerely want high-trust community while rejecting the micro-behaviors through which relational role overlap would form.
4. Conventional community-building focused on shared identity or social events can leave those anti-multiplexity norms untouched.

**Prediction:** Stated desire for community should poorly predict role overlap when norms against asking, offering, intervening, or mixing domains remain strong.

**Verdict:** SURVIVED, but culturally contingent and requires careful empirical differentiation from healthy privacy/boundary protections.

---

## T30 — Multiplexity has a bootstrap barrier

**Plain proposition:** People are reluctant to entrust a function to a relationship until that relationship has demonstrated competence in the function, but it cannot demonstrate competence until someone entrusts it with the function.

**Familiarity adversary:** Generic trust bootstrapping is familiar, but the implication for community design is useful.

**Consequence:** Low-stakes sanctioned role experiments may be necessary to cross the first-use barrier.

**Verdict:** PROMISING / mechanism supporting T27, not flagship.

---

## T31 — Specialist reliability creates an asymmetric evidence standard

**Plain proposition:** Informal provision may be abandoned after a salient failure while specialist-system failures are treated as provider-specific and solved by switching providers, because the system itself is assumed to remain valid.

**Familiarity adversary:** Resembles attribution bias and institutional legitimacy. Needs evidence before promotion.

**Verdict:** PROMISING.

---

## T32 — Formal liability can freeze role boundaries

**Plain proposition:** Once a function becomes professionalized, liability, certification, insurance, or organizational rules can make informal participation costly even when people retain the underlying competence.

**Familiarity adversary:** Professionalization and occupational closure are well-known.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novel; important empirical reinforcement mechanism.

---

## T33 — Reciprocity ambiguity can favor purchase over asking

**Plain proposition:** Purchasing a service closes the ledger immediately, while asking a multiplex tie creates an open-ended reciprocal obligation whose future repayment path is uncertain.

**Familiarity adversary:** Gift exchange versus market exchange is classic anthropology/economics.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novel.

---

## T34 — Unbundling can change moral vocabulary after changing capability

**Plain proposition:** Once ordinary ties stop carrying a function, expectations that they should carry it may come to feel unreasonable; moral norms can therefore adapt to the capability loss that unbundling itself produced.

**Familiarity adversary:** Norm adaptation/path dependence is known. The sequence is potentially useful:

> outsource -> lose capability -> expectation becomes unrealistic -> norm against expecting help -> further outsourcing

**Verdict:** PROMISING, perhaps a fourth feedback loop if evidence supports it.

---

## T35 — Independence from people can be dependence on permissioned systems

**Plain proposition:** Relational unbundling can increase freedom from specific people while making ordinary functioning more conditional on access to systems that require payment, documentation, infrastructure, or eligibility.

**Familiarity adversary:** This compresses readily to classic critiques of market dependence / impersonal systems.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as standalone novelty; F004 gives the sharper failure-topology claim.

---

## T36 — More choice can reduce future choice

**Plain proposition:** Having a specialist option for every current need can reduce the future set of viable relational options by preventing those options from developing capability.

**Familiarity adversary:** Choice crowding out capability resembles capability traps and path dependence. It is a concise consequence of T27 rather than an independent discovery.

**Verdict:** MERGED INTO T27.

---

## T37 — Community restoration may require protected amateur domains

**Plain proposition:** If professionals dominate every useful function, relationships may need domains where members are explicitly allowed to be merely adequate while learning to help one another.

**Familiarity adversary:** Apprenticeship and amateur clubs are familiar. The unusual point is that the purpose is not skill acquisition alone but creation of **relationship-level functional capacity**.

**Design implication:** Low-stakes cooking, repair, transport, childcare backup, teaching, mediation support, care during illness, gardening, logistics, and other useful exchanges can serve as training grounds for multiplexity when safely bounded.

**Verdict:** SURVIVED AS DESIGN CONSEQUENCE of T27, not general license to replace high-stakes professionals.

---

## T38 — Boundary protections and multiplexity need separate architecture

**Plain proposition:** The abuse/entrapment risks of multiplexity do not imply that functions must be unbundled; they imply that support functions should be redundantly distributed so exit from one relationship does not destroy access to all of them.

**Familiarity adversary:** Redundancy is familiar. However, this prevents a false binary between safe exit and multiplex relationships.

**Verdict:** CRITICAL DESIGN CONSTRAINT, not a novelty finding.

# Batch selection

## Strongest survivor: T27 — relational option value requires real use

The non-obvious claim is that **transaction-level optimization can destroy system-level relational capability**. If multiplexity is learned by use, some locally inferior relational transactions can function as investments in future network capability.

This gives a more precise mechanism than "markets crowd out community":

> specialist superiority today -> specialist chosen -> relationship gets no practice -> specialist superiority tomorrow increases

The mechanism predicts that simply making people friendlier or more connected will not reverse unbundling if every useful task continues to be routed elsewhere.

## Second survivor: T29 — outcome/norm incompatibility

A society can desire community as an outcome while enforcing micro-norms that suppress role overlap. This may explain why community rhetoric coexists with persistent relational specialization.

## Updated regime model

The low-multiplexity state may now be maintained by at least four processes:

1. **capability atrophy:** unused relationships remain incapable;
2. **replacement feedback:** decomposed functions make people component-wise replaceable;
3. **common access coupling:** specialist functions depend on shared system-access layers;
4. **transaction optimization:** the specialist's short-run advantage prevents relationships from obtaining the practice needed to close the capability gap.

Possible cultural reinforcement:

5. **boundary/norm adaptation:** once role overlap is rare, expecting it can itself become socially illegitimate.

This creates a sharp design hypothesis: rebuilding community requires not merely more contact, shared identity, or mutual affection, but **structured opportunities for real useful role exchange with bounded stakes and enough tolerance for early inefficiency to let relational capability compound**.
