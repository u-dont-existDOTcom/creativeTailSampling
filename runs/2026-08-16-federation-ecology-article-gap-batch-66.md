## Exa / empirical collision

### Historical Twin Oaks LEX

The Twin Oaks labor policy says LEX was outside the annual labor plan because labor was expected to return within the same planning period. It also says Twin Oaks members generally could not claim LEX credit while visiting a community that already owed Twin Oaks a large labor debt.

This is a concrete balance mechanism, not a generic theoretical analogy.

### 2010 East Wind stress response

A FEC Assembly report describes East Wind facing several member deaths in a short period plus business/regulatory stress. Delegates discussed special LEX opportunities but then created a **special federation budget for direct aid to East Wind**.

That episode separates ordinary reciprocal labor from asymmetric support in actual federation practice.

### June 2026 FEC redesign

The current `FEC Exchange Program Policy`, approved January 2026 and amended June 2026, is the decisive source.

It explicitly says the new program replaces LEX/LETS because the old systems were somewhat confusing/under-developed and were **transactional systems that falsely assumed parity between communities**.

The replacement:

- has no formal labor debt/tracking;
- treats exchange trips as non-transactional;
- prioritizes skill-sharing, time-sensitive mutual aid, and intercommunity connections;
- also supports cultural cross-pollination, communard renewal, and exploration;
- gives travelers full labor credit in their home community;
- uses an approximately three-hour/day host-work expectation partly to avoid reconciling different labor systems;
- assigns travel funding, host accommodation, coordinator/host roles, and emergency-cost responsibility at the interface;
- asks delegates to seek balanced distribution while considering differing community population sizes.

This is direct institutional redesign evidence.

## Article-gap disposition

### G-027 — B — Do not force asymmetric intercommunity support into a parity ledger

Residual:

> A federation should classify intercommunity transfers by purpose before deciding whether they create reciprocal debt. Routine exchange, emergency mutual aid, skill transfer, exploratory/renewal travel, and relationship-building should not automatically share one bilateral parity ledger.

Operational implications mirrored to the communities repo:

- use debt/balance accounting only where repayment is genuinely part of the agreement;
- do not silently convert emergency aid or federation-funded exploration into bilateral debt;
- preassign travel, accommodation, home labor credit, host burden, emergency-cost, and coordination responsibilities;
- preserve local systems when a thin interface suffices;
- track distribution normalized by relevant community capacity/population so non-transactional support does not become invisible permanent extraction;
- if aid later becomes reciprocal, create a new explicit agreement rather than retroactively converting past aid into debt.

This is **not** a strict Creative-Tail originality claim. Adjacent mutual-credit/cooperative-finance literature contains related countercyclical/non-bilateral ideas, and Parallel Task is unavailable.

## G-026 correction from the same primary source

The 2026 Exchange Program shows that interoperability does **not** imply harmonizing internal member-community systems.

For temporary mobility the FEC uses a thin adapter/interface: dual community consent, host/coordinator roles, FEC travel funding, host accommodation, simple work expectation, full home labor credit, no cross-community debt ledger, and defined problem/emergency handling.

Correct G-026 to:

> Standardize the minimum interface necessary to make movement usable; preserve local systems unless deeper portability actually requires harmonization.

This remains compatible with selective local admission/capacity. Temporary exchange is not an entitlement to residential membership.
