# Orthogonal Tail Batch 33 — Benchmark Selection, Transfers, and Outcome Laundering

Date: 2026-08-15

The strongest ideas collide directly with healthcare/school/provider-performance literatures on cream skimming, risk adjustment, patient transfer, case-mix selection and performance profiling. # A. Strongest practical result — intercommunity transfer should not launder outcomes

Suppose Community A has a member/household with severe conflict, high support needs, or a difficult safety/governance case.

If the person leaves or transfers to Community B:

- A's incident rate can fall;
- A's satisfaction average can rise;
- A's support costs can fall;
- A's retention metric can worsen or, if exclusions are coded differently, disappear from the denominator;
- B inherits the need/cost/risk.

The movement-wide question is not merely whether A's local metrics improved. It is:

> **What happened to the person/household and to total movement burden after the transfer?**

A well-supported transfer can be a genuine success. A hostile expulsion, case dumping, or denominator manipulation is not.

## Operational distinction

Track at least:

1. **origin-community outcome** — what changed locally;
2. **transfer process** — voluntary, mutually agreed, compelled, emergency, supported, unsupported;
3. **destination-community outcome** — what burden/support shifted;
4. **person/household later outcome** — housing, safety, relationships, health, education, material stability, belonging;
5. **movement-wide resource effect** — did total cost/risk fall or merely move?;
6. **reason for transfer** — fit, opportunity, fission, conflict, safety, care need, economic issue, etc.

# B. Source-domain collision

Healthcare provider-performance systems directly face the problem that providers can improve apparent performance/profitability by selecting easier patients or transferring high-severity cases. Cream-skimming studies explicitly analyze patient selection and hospital transfers. Risk-adjustment research shows that provider rankings can change substantially when case mix is handled differently.

Therefore:

- `performance depends on whom you serve` is not new;
- `transfers can shift hard cases between providers` is not new;
- `risk-adjust rankings` is not new.

The target transfer is practically useful but fails Creative Tail novelty.

# C. Candidate audit

At least fourteen candidates were screened.

## T133-01 — Community benchmark cream skimming
Communities can improve measured outcomes by admitting easier members.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL. Direct provider-selection analogy; also close to existing community admission evidence.

## T133-02 — Difficult-member dumping by transfer
A community can improve local metrics by shifting difficult cases elsewhere.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL. Direct hospital transfer/cream-skimming precedent.

## T133-03 — Movement-level person trajectory
Track people/households across community transfers rather than treating every community observation as independent.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; STRONGLY PRACTICAL. Longitudinal/multi-provider outcome tracking is established.

## T133-04 — Risk-adjust community outcome rankings
Adjust for member case mix before comparing communities.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; high danger of over-modeling and legitimizing exclusions. Use stratification/context, not one opaque score.

## T133-05 — Present-on-admission state
Distinguish conditions/problems present before entry from those emerging after entry.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; direct healthcare quality-measure precedent. Practical for causal attribution.

## T133-06 — Transfer-out is not resolved outcome
Do not mark a case resolved merely because the person leaves the origin community.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; practical.

## T133-07 — Supported transfer can be success
A movement may improve both fit and welfare by moving someone to a better community; do not penalize every transfer as failure.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; practical counterbalance.

## T133-08 — Movement burden accounting
Count whether costs/risks decrease or simply move.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL. System-level accounting/externality logic.

## T133-09 — Denominator versioning after exits
Record who remains/exits so satisfaction/incidents are interpretable.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; standard cohort attrition/survivorship control.

## T133-10 — Leaver follow-up floor
Every major outcome study should include some protected leaver follow-up.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; already supported by current communities research gap.

## T133-11 — Selection-adjusted best-practice inference
A practice used in communities serving easier populations may look superior due to case mix.

**Verdict:** REJECT. Confounding/risk adjustment.

## T133-12 — Admission severity vs outcomes
Publish outcome stratification by relevant baseline needs rather than one average.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; practical.

## T133-13 — Transfer networks reveal burden concentration
Map which communities become net recipients of high-need transfers and whether federation support follows.

**Verdict:** REJECT AS NOVEL; practical network/case-mix analysis.

## T133-14 — Metric gaming by exclusion coding
If expulsion, voluntary departure and transfer are coded differently, policy can shift labels rather than outcomes.

**Verdict:** REJECT. Goodhart/coding-gaming.

# D. Practical lessons to mirror into communities

1. do not call an origin-community problem resolved merely because the person/household left;
2. follow later outcomes across supported transfers, expulsions and voluntary moves;
3. distinguish successful matching/migration from burden dumping;
4. measure whether total movement burden changed or only moved;
5. record baseline state at admission/transfer when evaluating causal community effects;
6. stratify outcomes by relevant baseline needs rather than relying on raw rankings;
7. preserve denominator/attrition information for satisfaction, incident and wellbeing metrics;
8. include leavers in evaluation wherever safe/ethical/feasible;
9. inspect whether some communities systematically receive high-need transfers without matching federation support;
10. avoid opaque risk-adjusted league tables that can make rights/fairness disappear into a model;
11. separate local performance from movement-level person outcomes;
12. code transfer reasons/process consistently so metric changes cannot be manufactured by relabeling exits.

## Method lesson

The communities research center should optimize **people and movement outcomes**, not provider/community report cards. A local metric can improve because genuine care improved, because selection changed, or because the hard case moved somewhere else.
