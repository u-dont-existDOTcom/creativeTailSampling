# A. C013 adversarial attack — federated applicant clearing

## Finding

Historical and current kibbutz practice narrows the originality claim substantially.

Current Kibbutz Movement guidance aggregates accepting communities and helps seekers discover options, but joining remains a community-specific process: prospective members identify preferences/contact kibbutzim and undergo local screening/admission.

Historical scholarship reports a joint central `Department of Absorption and Demographic Growth` that coordinated/screened individual applicants to kibbutzim. Therefore **centralized intake/screening is not a new communal mechanism**.

What has not yet been found in the target domain is the narrower C013 structure:

> after reciprocal preference discovery through visits/trials, a movement-wide mechanism coordinates commitments across multiple autonomous communities so compatible seeker–vacancy pairs are not lost to isolated timing/search decisions.

This is still a transfer from known matching-market design, not a new theory.

### C013 disposition

**SURVIVES, narrowed.**

Do not describe C013 as centralized recruitment, central screening, a directory, or movement-wide placement. Its only potentially distinctive target transfer is **reciprocal preference clearing after exploration across autonomous communities**, preserving opt-out and local admission authority.

Sources screened include current Kibbutz Movement absorption guidance and Abramitzky's work on kibbutz migration/central applicant screening.

# B. C014 adversarial attack — hidden-harm under-ascertainment

## Direct collision

Capture–recapture has already been applied directly to family violence and child abuse using multiple organizations.

Oosterlee, Vink & Smit (2009) applied capture–recapture to registrations from eight collaborating organizations to estimate family-violence and child-abuse prevalence. Palusci, Wirtz & Covington (2010) used multiple administrative systems to estimate fatal child maltreatment and explicitly showed that the method worked much better for physical-abuse deaths than neglect deaths because the assumptions failed for the latter.

Therefore the conceptual transfer from hidden-population statistics to communal safeguarding is much too close to existing practice for the Creative Tail novelty bar.

### C014 disposition

**DEMOTE from Creative Tail survivor.**

Retain as a practical federation/research-center lesson with strong statistical and privacy warnings. The strongest practical insight may not even require a hidden-population point estimate: largely nonoverlapping channels are already evidence that a low formal complaint count may reflect poor ascertainment.

# C. Orthogonal candidates

At least sixteen plain candidates were generated before selection.

## T120-01 — Lineage-aware community comparison

**Plain claim:** daughter communities are not independent observations because they inherit institutions, members, practices and histories from common ancestors.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** this is the classic comparative-method / `Galton's problem` problem. Cultural phylogenetic methods explicitly exist to handle non-independence caused by shared cultural ancestry, including institutional traits.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as research-control lesson now that parent→daughter genealogies are being reconstructed.

Practical implication: comparative datasets should include lineage identifiers and, where appropriate, phylogenetic/multilevel models. Horizontal borrowing across communities must also be modeled because pure tree assumptions can fail.

---

## T120-02 — Sister-daughter matched comparisons

**Plain claim:** when two daughter communities descend from the same parent but later differ in one practice, their shared ancestry can sometimes make the comparison more informative than comparing unrelated communes.

**Nearest neighbor:** sibling/family fixed-effect designs and matched comparisons are established causal-inference logic.

**Critical confound:** a fission can sort people nonrandomly. The members selecting into daughter A versus B may differ precisely on the variable later associated with the outcome.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as research-control practice.

Strongest use: divergence caused by an external/legal/resource shock or another plausibly exogenous event, rather than a factional split whose member composition already encodes the causal difference.

---

## T120-03 — Cultural hitchhiking in successful communal packages

**Plain claim:** an inherited practice can spread with a successful community design even if the practice is neutral or harmful, simply because the practice is linked to genuinely successful traits in the transmitted package.

**Direct collision:** Yeh, Fogarty & Kandler (2019), `Cultural linkage: the influence of package transmission on cultural dynamics`, explicitly models neutral and detrimental cultural variants hitchhiking with adaptive traits when cultural traits are transmitted as packages.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as high-value practical lesson.

Practical implication for Hutterite/Zapatista/other exemplars: never infer that every distinctive practice in a highly reproductive or successful lineage caused its success. Treat the package as causally unresolved until variants, ablations, recombinations, natural experiments, or other evidence separate the load-bearing components from hitchhikers.

---

## T120-04 — Daughter-community ablation testing

**Plain claim:** when safe and ethically appropriate, let different daughter communities omit or modify different inherited practices to identify which components are load-bearing and which are hitchhikers.

**Nearest neighbors:** policy experimentation/laboratories of democracy, ablation studies, cultural linkage/recombination, organizational experimentation.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as practical experimental design with non-nettable safety/rights constraints.

---

## T120-05 — Constitutional holdout cases / cross-validation

**Plain claim:** when writing or revising rules from accumulated disputes, reserve some cases from the rule-design process and test the resulting rule on them so the constitution is not merely tuned to the controversies that produced it.

**Direct collision:** a 2026 `American Law and Economics Review` article, `Legal Overfitting`, explicitly proposes applying machine-learning holdout/test-set methods to legal precedent: derive a legal model from part of the precedent set and evaluate it on withheld cases.

**Disposition:** REJECT as Creative Tail novelty; KEEP as practical governance design.

Practical caution: small communities may not have enough cases for literal statistical validation. The useful transfer can be qualitative: reserve some archived/hypothetical cases until after the rule is formulated, and avoid repeatedly tuning the rule to the holdout set itself.

---

## T120-06 — Penalize constitutional complexity / regularization

**Plain claim:** adding an exception after every dispute can perfectly fit historical cases while producing an incoherent, gameable constitution; prefer simpler rule sets unless added complexity materially improves unseen-case performance.

**Direct/near collision:** `Legal Overfitting` explicitly extends regularization/parsimony ideas into legal precedent; rules-versus-standards and legal-complexity literatures are also longstanding.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as practical companion to holdout testing.

---

## T120-07 — Preserve neutral institutional variation to retain evolvability

**Plain claim:** two daughter-community variants can perform equally well today but occupy different positions in institutional design space, making different future improvements reachable; standardizing away harmless variation can reduce future evolvability.

**Direct collision:** Jain & Kogut, `Memory and Organizational Evolvability in a Neutral Landscape`, explicitly imports neutral-landscape evolutionary theory into organizations and models neutrality/drift, memory and evolvability.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as a movement-design caution against unnecessary homogenization.

---

## T120-08 — Institutional epistasis / co-adapted rule complexes

**Plain claim:** the effect of one communal rule depends on other rules; copying a single `best practice` from a successful community can fail because the source package contains interacting components.

**Nearest neighbors:** institutional complementarity, organizational configurations, NK fitness landscapes, cultural package linkage.

**Disposition:** REJECT.

Practical consequence already covered by replication/adaptation lessons.

---

## T120-09 — Privacy-preserving federation overlap analytics

**Plain claim:** communities could compute shared-record/intersection statistics across private datasets without pooling all raw records in one central database, using private set intersection or privacy-preserving record linkage.

**Source mechanism:** established PSI/PPRL/secure multiparty computation; used for cross-organizational health, fraud, security, advertising and research data collaboration.

**Target application:** could support federation-wide research or duplicate-pattern detection while reducing disclosure of nonmatching records.

**Why not promoted:** technological feasibility is not the bottleneck proven by the community research. Governance, lawful basis, case-definition quality, false matches, due process, and the risk of creating a covert blacklist could dominate the benefit. The application is too infrastructure-specific and insufficiently validated to count as a community-development finding.

**Disposition:** REJECT as tail finding; retain only as a future technical option if a concrete privacy-constrained federation analytics need appears.

## T120-10 — Commit-reveal decisions or forecasts

**Plain claim:** participants cryptographically commit to votes/predictions before seeing others, then reveal later, preventing opportunistic revision/bandwagoning while retaining verifiability.

**Nearest neighbors:** secret ballots, sealed bids, commit–reveal protocols, independent-first elicitation.

**Disposition:** REJECT. C001/epistemic-independence material already captures the useful part without unnecessary cryptographic machinery.

## T120-11 — Federation near-miss pooling

**Plain claim:** rare failures may occur too infrequently within one commune to learn from; a federation can pool near misses and incidents across many communities.

**Nearest neighbors:** high-reliability organizations, aviation/medical incident reporting, learning collaboratives.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; likely useful practical movement infrastructure.

---

## T120-12 — Safety-reporting paradox

**Plain claim:** communities with better detection/reporting can appear less safe if performance is judged by raw incident counts, creating incentives to suppress reporting.

**Nearest neighbors:** surveillance/ascertainment bias, Goodhart/Campbell effects, safety-culture literature.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as practical measurement warning.

---

## T120-13 — Selective transparency for anti-gaming

**Plain claim:** some exact screening/detection details should be withheld when disclosure would make the safeguard trivial to game, while criteria, rights, appeal and oversight remain transparent.

**Nearest neighbors:** fraud detection, exam/item-bank security, security engineering, adversarial classification, investigative confidentiality.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty. Practical use is domain-specific and must not become unreviewable secret law.

---

## T120-14 — Common-garden / reciprocal-transplant institutional tests

**Plain claim:** move the same practice across several communities/environments to distinguish a generally useful practice from a locally adapted one.

**Nearest neighbors:** reciprocal transplant/common-garden experiments, organizational replication, policy external-validity research.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; practical variant already covered by cross-community comparison.

---

## T120-15 — Institutional seed bank

**Plain claim:** preserve multiple living variants of communal practice rather than letting one federation-wide standard eliminate alternatives, so old variants remain available if conditions change.

**Nearest neighbors:** bet hedging, organizational diversity, policy experimentation, cultural preservation, exploration/exploitation.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty. Useful only when the cost of maintaining variants is low enough.

---

## T120-16 — Federated reliability-growth library

**Plain claim:** every significant failure/near miss updates a shared movement-level library of failure modes, tests and mitigations so daughters do not relearn the same failure independently.

**Nearest neighbors:** reliability growth, incident databases, high-reliability organizations, safety management systems, organizational learning.

**Disposition:** REJECT as novelty; KEEP as practical movement practice.

# Batch 20 disposition

## No new survivors

The batch produced **zero new Creative Tail findings**.

### Retained survivor, narrowed

- **C013** remains provisional only as reciprocal preference clearing after exploration across multiple autonomous communities; central intake/screening itself is old kibbutz practice.

### Demoted

- **C014** → practical/research-control only. Family-violence and child-abuse capture–recapture are direct precedents.

### Practical lessons to mirror into `communities`

1. lineage-aware comparative analysis / Galton's problem;
2. sibling-daughter comparison with self-sorting caveat;
3. cultural hitchhiking: success of package does not validate every component;
4. daughter/variant ablation where safe;
5. constitutional holdout cases and parsimony to reduce overfitting;
6. preserve harmless institutional variation when standardization has no demonstrated gain;
7. near-miss pooling across a federation;
8. raw incident counts can punish good reporting;
9. federation reliability-growth memory.

## Method lesson

This batch is a useful test of the stricter protocol: several ideas were unusually phrased and operationally useful, but none survived the nearest-neighbor literature attack. They belong in the practical lessons layer, not the Creative Tail survivor set.
