# Orthogonal Tail Batch 48 — Destination-Blind Fission Partitioning

Date: 2026-08-15

## Outcome

**No Creative Tail survivor.**

The strongest mechanism is already an intentional-community practice: Hutterite colonies have historically formed balanced groups and then used a lot to determine which group remains at the mother colony and which moves to the daughter. The practice is therefore target-domain precedent, not a new transfer.

It is nevertheless a high-value community-development lesson and a new empirical question for the C005 reproduction program.

---

# Observed Hutterite practice

Hutterite sources describe colony fission around roughly 30 families / 130–150 people.

Practices vary by Leut and period:

- Schmiedeleut/Dariusleut may use volunteers/mutual consent or divide into groups and cast lots.
- Lehrerleut traditionally build the daughter colony fully, divide members into two roughly balanced groups, have families pack, and only then cast the lot deciding which group moves and which remains.
- Nuclear families are kept intact; some accounts describe attention to family support/viability and balanced operating capacity.
- Assets are carefully valued/divided, with financial adjustment when exact physical equality is impossible.

A 1970 Supreme Court of Canada case concerning Rock Lake/Interlake records a concrete historical split in which members formed two groups **without knowing which would go to the daughter**, and the destination was then decided by lot.

---

# B48-01 — Separate composition from destination

## Plain rule

> **First design two viable groups. Only after composition is fixed should the federation/community decide which group receives which site/status position.**

This prevents `which side gets the old home?` from contaminating `which people make two viable communities?`.

## Why it can matter

If the incumbent site is perceived as safer/better:
- people can lobby to remain;
- leaders can retain allies/preferred workers;
- unpopular members can be pushed into the daughter;
- the daughter can become a residual/loser group;
- composition choices can optimize local comfort rather than two-community viability.

Destination-blind assignment reduces these channels.

**Disposition: target-known practice / practical.**

# Practical lessons

## B48-L01 — Build two viable communities before assigning the desirable/undesirable destination

Balance relevant capacities first:
- leadership succession;
- farming/business/work skills;
- childcare/care;
- age structure;
- family units;
- health/support needs;
- maintenance/trades;
- social/familial continuity.

Then allocate the physical mother/daughter site by a process decided in advance.

## B48-L02 — Randomize destination, not necessarily people

Pure random person-by-person assignment can split families/care networks and create nonviable groups.

The Hutterite logic is stronger:
1. construct viable groups;
2. keep protected relational units intact;
3. randomize which viable group gets which site.

## B48-L03 — Use destination blindness as an anti-manipulation device

The composition committee should not know which group will inherit:
- established housing;
- familiar land;
- local reputation;
- easier infrastructure;
- specific desirable assets

when those differences could bias assignment.

Where the sites are materially unequal, assets/support must compensate separately.

## B48-L04 — Define protected units before partition

Examples:
- nuclear households;
- coparenting structures;
- dependent elders/caregivers;
- critical medical support links.

Do not use `randomness` to justify avoidable family/care harm.

## B48-L05 — Treat skill balance and social preference as separate objectives

A self-selected group can be socially comfortable but operationally weak. A centrally balanced group can be operationally strong but coercive/socially damaging.

Fission planning should expose the tradeoff and allow bounded preference exchange after viability constraints are satisfied.

## B48-L06 — Make both sides transition-ready before the lot where feasible

The Lehrerleut pack-before-lot practice is a strong commitment device: both groups must be prepared to move rather than one group treating daughter formation as somebody else's problem.

A modern equivalent could be:
- both groups complete staffing plans;
- both have asset/budget plans;
- both have transport/records ready;
- both can demonstrate operational readiness before destination assignment.

## B48-L07 — Asset equality is not the same as functional equality

Meticulous asset valuation helps, but the daughter can still receive:
- worse land;
- less mature infrastructure;
- weaker external market access;
- more debt/maintenance risk;
- fewer experienced operators.

Compare **productive/function capacity**, not just book value.

## B48-L08 — Transitional mother→daughter support can be part of fair fission

Historical Hutterite branching includes transitional support when the daughter cannot immediately produce everything it needs.

Plan explicitly:
- duration;
- goods/services;
- repayment if any;
- mentoring;
- sunset/independence criteria.

Avoid indefinite dependency that makes reproduction look more robust than it is (C017/C005 support exposure).

## B48-L09 — Do not confuse lot legitimacy with outcome quality

Random assignment can reduce favoritism but does not guarantee:
- good group composition;
- consent;
- child/family wellbeing;
- functional viability;
- fair land/assets.

Evaluate outcomes afterward.

## B48-L10 — Mutual-consent fission and lot-based fission may select different mechanisms

Modern Hutterite branches reportedly differ: Lehrerleut often retain lot-based branching, while other Leuts increasingly use discussion/mutual consent.

This creates a potentially valuable comparative research design, but branch differences are confounded by many other institutional/cultural factors.

## B48-L11 — C005 research extension: branching-process covariate

For future parent→daughter datasets, record:
- lot vs volunteer/mutual-consent partition;
- whether composition fixed before destination known;
- group-size balance;
- family-unit constraints;
- skill/leadership balance;
- asset/capacity balance;
- transitional subsidy;
- daughter survival/reproduction interval.

Then test whether branching architecture predicts:
- `P(K=0)`;
- daughter time-to-reproduction;
- variance in reproduction;
- conflict/failure;
- subsequent member transfer.

Do **not** infer causality from current cross-Leut aggregate differences alone.

## B48-L12 — Destination-blindness can be useful outside literal fission

Possible analogous transitions:
- two teams sharing one desirable facility;
- allocating old/new housing after reorganization;
- assigning leadership portfolios after balanced committees are formed.

The generic fair-allocation mechanism is known; retain only when status/location knowledge could contaminate composition.

---

## Disposition

No Creative Tail survivor because Hutterites already implement the target structure.

High-value practical lesson:

> **For planned fission, separate `who forms two viable groups?` from `which group gets the incumbent versus daughter position?` Where feasible, fix two viable compositions first and assign destination afterward under a precommitted neutral process.**

Research extension:

> Add branching-process variables to C005 so reproduction success is studied not only as parent-offspring counts but as a function of how the parent actually partitioned people, skills, assets and destination status.
