# Reproduction Tail Batch 05 — When Local Success Conflicts with Movement Spread

Date: 2026-08-15

Trigger: the multiplex/commodification branch failed the novelty gate. This batch targets the user's stronger success criterion: communities that reproduce/spread rather than merely persist.

Method: generate candidate cross-domain transfers, then attack them with common-sense compression and target-domain/literature nearest neighbors. A technically elegant old idea is a rejection, not a finding.

## T53 — Cultural packet / propagule reproduction

**Plain proposition:** Complex community institutions may reproduce more reliably when an experienced subset of people physically seeds a daughter group than when outsiders attempt to copy written rules.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Strong. Hutterite daughter-colony fission already does this in practice. Cultural-evolution work on the "social protocell" explicitly models cultural inheritance when parent communities split, including failure when essential cultural traits are missing from daughter groups.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty.

---

## T54 — Institutional transmission error threshold

**Plain proposition:** The more interdependent cultural/institutional components a community requires, the higher the transmission fidelity required for daughters to reproduce it; above some complexity/fidelity threshold the package will systematically simplify or break.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Cultural-evolution/cumulative-culture literature already studies transmission fidelity and extensive cultural complexity; the social-protocell model explicitly discusses loss of institutional function when necessary cultural traits are lost during group fission.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty.

---

## T55 — Germline specialization

**Plain proposition:** A mature community may need a subgroup whose explicit function is reproduction—leaving, founding, and carrying transmissible structure—rather than expecting all residents simultaneously to optimize local life and movement reproduction.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Germ–soma specialization is a standard evolutionary transition mechanism; missionary orders, colonizing groups, franchises, spinouts, and planned Hutterite daughter colonies are obvious human analogues.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty.

---

## T56 — Multi-parent founder recombination

**Plain proposition:** Daughter communities founded by experienced members of several parent communities may recombine institutional adaptations rather than clonally copying one parent.

**Nearest-neighbor screen:** Evolutionary recombination is obvious in the source domain. Intentional-community networks and exchange programs create cross-community learning. No direct literature found in this screen systematically testing multi-parent founder composition in land-based intentional communities. Online-community genealogy research finds that a diverse prior-community portfolio is the strongest predictor of becoming an early member of a new community.

**Distinctive prediction:** holding founder experience roughly constant, founding teams whose members have independent experience across multiple successful community lineages should initially have more interpretive conflict but fewer parent-specific blind spots and a larger repertoire of institutional alternatives.

**Verdict:** PROMISING CROSS-DOMAIN TRANSFER, not promoted yet. It may still compress to ordinary "cross-pollination is good."

## T57 — Alumni / diaspora seed bank

**Plain proposition:** Former members are not necessarily lost capacity; maintaining low-cost affiliation with them may create a dormant reserve that can reactivate after shocks or seed new communities.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Alumni networks, diasporas, seed banks, reserve pools, and communities of practice are established ideas. Intentional-community networks include many long-term cross-community people, and anecdotal examples of ex-members starting other projects are common.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as root novelty. Potentially useful metric/design consequence only.

## T58 — Local retention is the wrong movement-level fitness metric

**Plain proposition:** A member leaving a particular community can be a reproductive success if they remain in the movement and create/strengthen another community.

**Common-sense compression:** Given the user's explicit spread/reproduction criterion, this is nearly definitional.

**Verdict:** REJECTED AS OBVIOUS TO THIS PROJECT.

## T59 — Source/sink community classification

**Plain proposition:** Some communities can look healthy because they continuously import trained/cooperative people while producing few such people themselves; others can look unstable because they export members who strengthen the wider movement.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Direct source–sink models already exist for group-structured societies and institutional evolution. Organizational ecology also treats founding, dissolution, density, and resource flows among organizational populations.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty.

---

## T60 — Strong behavioral diffusion can weaken institutional selection

**Plain proposition:** If useful community-generated behaviors diffuse into groups that lack the institutions producing them, those receiving groups can temporarily gain the benefits, reducing selection pressure for adoption of the supporting institution itself.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** This is almost exactly the result of Hébert-Dufresne et al. (2022), "Source-sink behavioural dynamics limit institutional evolution in a group-structured society."

**Application:** could matter for movements whose techniques diffuse into mainstream life without the institutional package, but it is not ours to call a new finding.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty; retained as potentially important existing theory to apply later.

## T61 — Flagship founder trap

**Plain proposition:** A highly attractive, mature community can reduce movement-level reproduction by absorbing and retaining exactly the high-agency people most capable of founding daughter communities.

This differs from simple institutionalization or "success makes people complacent" by specifying an allocation conflict over a scarce class of people: **potential founders**.

### Mechanism

1. A successful flagship becomes a low-risk, high-reward place for capable community-seekers to live.
2. The same people face much higher cost/risk if they leave to found a new community.
3. The flagship rationally wants to retain its most competent members because they improve local function.
4. The movement needs some of those same members to leave because novices have much lower probability of founding viable daughters.
5. Therefore local-community optimization and movement reproduction can conflict even when everyone shares the movement's stated goal.

### Nearest-neighbor attack

- Social-movement literature has extensive work on institutionalization, professionalization and co-optation.
- Organizational ecology explicitly says dominant existing organizations affect the resources available to entrepreneurs/new foundings.
- Entrepreneurship literature studies employee spinouts and founder human capital.
- Intentional-community diffusion literature studies movement-level intermediaries and project founders.

This screen did **not** locate a standard intentional-community theory or practice framed as incumbent communities competing with their own movement for scarce potential founders.

### Distinctive prediction

Among movements with similar inflow of capable newcomers, flagships with unusually high retention of their highest-agency/most experienced members may show **lower daughter-community formation** unless they have explicit founder-export mechanisms.

### Stronger consequence: reproduction externality

The cost of exporting a strong member is paid immediately by the local community, while much of the benefit of a successful daughter accrues to the wider movement. Therefore each community can rationally underproduce founders even if all communities endorse expansion.

This implies a federation-level intervention: compensate, honor, replace, train, or otherwise subsidize communities that export proven founders.

**Verdict:** SURVIVING CROSS-DOMAIN CONNECTION, provisional. It is not a claim of academic unprecedentedness. Needs direct empirical testing in intentional-community genealogies.

## T62 — Founder-export subsidy

**Plain proposition:** If T61 is true, movement-level institutions should subsidize or reward the export of capable founders because local communities do not capture the full reproductive benefit.

**Nearest neighbor:** positive externality/subsidy is standard economics. The nonstandard part is only the target variable identified in T61.

**Verdict:** DESIGN CONSEQUENCE of T61, not an independent finding.

## T63 — Prestige can be anti-reproductive

**Plain proposition:** Increasing a flagship's attractiveness can increase recruitment into the movement but reduce the fraction who become founders, producing a non-monotonic relationship between flagship quality and movement reproduction.

**Derivation:** at low attractiveness there are too few recruits; at high attractiveness the flagship becomes difficult to leave; maximum daughter production may occur at an intermediate point or when founder export is institutionally separated from ordinary retention.

**Nearest neighbor:** ecological traps, organizational retention/spinout tradeoffs, institutionalization. No target-domain standard located.

**Verdict:** SURVIVING PREDICTION of T61, not independent.

## T64 — Collective forkability

**Plain proposition:** A group should be able to split institutionally, with assets/knowledge carried by both sides, rather than treating disagreement as winner-take-all exit.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Democratic-forking theory explicitly models optional communities that may fork; cooperative law often provides legal scission/division; Hutterites deliberately fission.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty.

## T65 — Intermediate migration preserves both innovation and transmission

**Plain proposition:** Too little movement among communities blocks learning; too much homogenizes experiments and correlates failures; intermediate migration may maximize movement-level adaptation.

**Nearest-neighbor collision:** Classic gene-flow/diversity, exploration/exploitation and network-diffusion tradeoff.

**Verdict:** REJECTED as novelty.

**Common-sense compression:** This is essentially the project's already stated success criterion.

# Batch result

This batch again produced **no new grand social theory**.

One cross-domain candidate survives provisionally:

## C002 — Flagship founder trap / founder-export externality

A spreading movement may face an internal allocation problem: its most successful communities are often the places most able and motivated to retain exactly the experienced, high-agency members the wider movement needs as founders.

The interesting claim is not "people get comfortable." It is a multilevel incentive conflict:

> **local communities capture the cost of exporting founders; the movement captures much of the benefit.**

If true, reproduction cannot be left entirely to local-community incentives. A movement that wants to spread needs explicit machinery for generating and exporting founders, and should measure not only member retention but **founder output per experienced member-year** or an equivalent lineage metric.

Evidence status: conceptual/cross-domain survivor only. Organizational ecology and movement-institutionalization are nearby literatures; no direct intentional-community test was located in this screen.
